The three phases, in words and symbols

Detection. Attack the artefact; update belief that a flaw remains. R_det = R(1−q)/(1−qR), with effective detection q = η·p. Restated findings (η→0) update nothing.

Resolution. Apply the fix; it works with probability σ. R_base = σ·R_det + (1−σ)·R. A failed fix leaves you at the old risk.

Re-injection. The act of changing the artefact adds new risk ν. R_new = R_base(1−ν) + ν. This is the substrate floor: lim R ≥ ν — no amount of detection outruns bad repair. But that bound is slack. Solving the recursion for its fixed point gives the floor actually reached, R* = ν / (q(σ + ν(1−σ))), which is ν/q when σ = 1. Detection quality multiplies the floor, so the trajectory settles above the dashed ν line rather than on it — at the default settings, 2.46× above. The bound is tight only at q = 1. Each question has its own floor, and the page draws the one you asked for. The plotted line conditions on the detection branch, so it settles at R*ΔR = ν / (q(σ + ν(1−σ))). Iterating the expectation instead gives the lower R*E = ν / (qσ + ν(1−qσ)), because averaging over detection credits the branch the drawn path conditions away. SymPy gives the gap in closed form, R*ΔR − R*E = ν²(1−q) / D with D > 0 on the open cube — so the trajectory floor is never the lower of the two, and they coincide only at ν = 0 or q = 1. Drawing the trajectory floor to a prospective reader overstates the floor their own question implies by 21.51% on the “leaky fixes” preset. The ν bound and the selected question’s floor are both drawn.

Scope. A bifurcation diagram, not a data plot — a property of the equations, not an observation of any process. Parameters are indicative; the tool exists so that measured values can replace them.

Break-even. A cycle helps only while ν < ν* — and ν* is also two numbers, one per question. For the plotted line it is ν*ΔR = σ·R·q / (1 − qR(1−σ)), the ν at which the drawn curve stops falling. For the prospective question, which is what this page gates on by default, it is ν*E = σ·R·q / (1 − R(1−qσ)) — never below ν*ΔR, rising in R, and tending to 1 as R → 1: when a flaw is near-certain the harm term ν(1−R) vanishes, so re-injection costs almost nothing in this single-flaw model. That is a real property of the modelled quantity P(≥ 1 flaw remains) and an artefact of its saturating state space, not a licence to churn a real, many-flawed artefact. Above ν*, stop fixing and defer to the HIL. The regime sentence under the chart quotes whichever ν* the selected question gates on, so cross-check it against the matching formula here — the two are genuinely different numbers at the same risk. On the “leaky fixes” preset the pass inspector reports ν*ΔR = 0.3000 for the drawn line and ν*E = 0.3857 for the default question at the final pass. Reading one formula against the other question’s sentence was how this page came to print a ν* its own printed formula could not produce; both are now listed for every pass, and the row the selected question gates on is marked there.
