## What was built, and how it was checked

### 1. A seat's own vendor token no longer survives in a finding identifier

The founder's ruling, verbatim: *"We normalise going forward, since recording vendor names is purely a recording statistic and has no impact on capability, or how our experiments work."*

The runner prefixed the seat label to whatever identifier a model chose for itself, so a seat dispatched as `Gemini-SIM` writing `GEMINI_F004` produced `Gemini-SIM_GEMINI_F004`. The seat label was always correct; the bare vendor name arrived inside the model's own text.

The project carried 3 different figures for this 1 defect and none of them said what it was counting. All are now reproduced by a single producer, which prints the whole lattice so they cannot drift apart again: 64 occurrences under the master task list's predicate (quote-delimited, underscore separator, JSON files), 81 widening the separator set to hyphen and colon, 98 including console logs, 4 recorded findings, 2 distinct identifiers, 1 affected run. The figure of 38 quoted in an earlier brief is reproducible by NO predicate in that lattice and is withdrawn.

The fix covers 6 composition sites, not 1. `_structurally_valid_fid`'s own docstring already enumerated 5 model-supplied paths, each carrying its own copy of the 2-line composition, and a 6th sat on the verdict-owner path. A single shared `_compose_finding_id` replaces all 6. 5 of the 6 reverts turn the test suite red; the survivor is provably inert, because the heading pattern constrains its token to `F` followed by 2 to 4 digits and 0 of 135 token-and-seat pairs change there.

**A hazard was found in the first version of this fix by trying to break it.** That version took its candidate tokens from the whole model roster, so under seat `CC2-SIM` the identifier `GEMINI_F004` — a CC2 reply REFERRING to Gemini's finding — stripped to `F004` and composed to `CC2-SIM_F004`, which resolves to CC2's own F004. A cross-model mis-attribution, and the same shape as a defect `lookup_alias_any` records from 30 August 2026. It was reachable rather than theoretical: the forms it added were new in 36 of 48 key-and-seat pairs, 75.0000 per cent, Wilson [61.2156, 85.0794]. Narrowing the strip to the seat's own token loses nothing measured, because both archived instances are a seat prefixing its own name, and it removes the function's dependency on the roster entirely.

The normalisation had to follow the identifier into the resolvers as well, because a miss there is not cosmetic: an unresolved MERGE source is mandated to be read as a CONFIRM, so a model saying "these two are the same defect" would be recorded as AGREEING the target is real. `_resolve_finding_key` needed the stripped forms and has them. `_resolve_merge_source` did NOT, and the candidates added there were reverted: its regex can only ever yield `F` or `C` followed by digits, 0 of 48 pairs change, and the composed form equalled an existing candidate in 48 of 48. An addition nothing reaches is not additive either.

### 3. The coherence budget recalibration finally has an input, and the root cause was arithmetic

The founder's ruling, verbatim: *"It should be fixed, providing it doesn't trim the operational directives. It exists so that models never get fed work that is larger than they have been demonstrably proven to be able to handle in any experiment. Not to trim the operational directives."*

The calibrator prefers a recorded constraint density and otherwise estimates it as the floor of the character count over 70, divided by the floor of the character count over 4. No archived dispatch entry carried any composer field: 0 of 5,946 under the predicate the consumer itself iterates, Wilson [0.0000, 0.0646], and 0 of 30,442 under a recursive predicate, Wilson [0.0000, 0.0126]. Both denominators are named in the producer, because they are different measurements.

What the entries DO carry is a prompt length, and 3,589 of 3,709 of those equal 203 — 96.7646 per cent, Wilson [96.1451, 97.2874] — because every save call in the dispatch is passed the prompt truncated to 200 characters with an ellipsis appended. SymPy, NumPy and mpmath agree that the floor of 203 over 70, divided by the floor of 203 over 4, is 2 over 50, which is 1 over 25 exactly. And z3 and SymPy independently agree that the set 200, 201, 202, 203 is the COMPLETE integer band on which that ratio holds, so the truncation length lands at the top edge of the only band that produces it. Every threshold the calibrator has ever produced was an artefact of a truncation constant rather than a measurement of any model. A real prompt length would approach 2 over 35, which is 0.0571, and that is the figure the free seats showed.

The fix is a write, not a rewrite. The composer already computed all 5 values and they were simply never persisted. They are now recorded at all 4 dispatch sites, with the untruncated size recorded BESIDE the existing prompt length rather than replacing it, because rewriting that field would silently change what 3,709 archived entries mean. A composer fallback is recorded explicitly, because an absent key and a failed composition are different facts and the calibrator must not read the second as the first. Replay is NOT invalidated, which is narrower than what the founder was told on 15 October: older runs simply lack the new keys and take the existing fallback exactly as before.

### 4. The boundary-band verdict no longer rests on a sentinel

The gamma estimator returns zero for 4 distinct reasons and only 1 of them is a slope of zero; its own mirror names the other 3. The band block compared the 2 band edges for equality, so 2 SENTINELS compared equal and the run was reported *robust to the whole rubric disagreement* on the strength of a quantity that was never measured.

Measured over the archive: 10 of 26 recorded blocks claim robustness on 2 sentinels, 38.4615 per cent, Wilson [22.4286, 57.4651], Clopper-Pearson [20.2260, 59.4292]. 4 more compare a real edge against a sentinel, so 14 of 26 verdicts carried no information, 53.8462 per cent, Wilson [35.4579, 71.2441].

It failed in both directions, which is why the repair is a gate rather than a sign flip. 2 sentinels read TRUE where the honest answer is "unreadable", and 1 real edge against a sentinel read FALSE, which is a false alarm claiming fragility on the basis of a number that does not exist. A false robustness is the worse half, because it retires a measured question: in that band the numeric proxy and the 5-clause rubric agree on only 141 of 259 judgeable findings, while 2 independent blind readers agreed with each other on 86 of 93, Cohen's kappa 0.837.

Estimability now travels with the number, from the SAME predicate the live gate uses, so no consumer re-derives a second opinion. Both edge values are still reported; only the verdict that cannot rest on them is withheld.

## What needs a founder ruling

### A. Should the coherence DENSITY budget govern anything at all now?

The assessment is that it probably should not, and saying so is more useful than wiring it up. Its only effectful consumer was the mechanical directive pruner, retired by default on the founder's ruling of 13 October. With pruning off, its sole remaining effect is a warning stating that a coherence penalty applies to effective detection — and nothing computes or applies that penalty. Every instance of that symbol in the repository is the convergence metric, and the budget and density values are only ever printed or asserted, never used to scale anything. So the warning asserts a penalty that is not in force.

And the purpose the ruling names is a different quantity. "Never get fed work that is larger than they have been demonstrably proven to be able to handle" is a SIZE ceiling. The coherence budget is a directive-DENSITY ceiling. What serves the stated purpose instead is the per-seat capability fingerprint under `bench/fingerprints/`, a stored record of the largest prompt each seat has handled, and that is item B below.

### B. The capacity number that cannot detect overload

Item 5 of the founder's list of smaller defects, verbatim: *"A capacity number shared identically by 4 paid models and untouched for 185 days."*

Measured. The field stands at 465,206 on CC2, ChatGPT, Codex and Gemini, 4 of 5 paid seats, against DeepSeek's 110,076. It first entered a committed fingerprint on 13 April 2026, which is 186 days before the repository's newest commit. Against each seat's own history window of 50 dispatches it sits 6.49 times above CC2's recent maximum, 6.64 above ChatGPT's, 6.50 above Codex's, 4.89 above Gemini's and 1.51 above DeepSeek's. 2 derived fields repeat the same number on all 5 seats, so 3 fields carry 1 figure.

It has never been bounded from above. The failure fields are zero or unset on every paid seat, so the arm that would lower the number has never fired, and 1 large dispatch in 1 old run still sets today's routing ceiling.

This is not fixed here, and the runner's own comment says why: changing it alters routing for paid seats, which is a design decision rather than a repair. 3 options: decay the number toward the recent window, bound it by a measured failure, or leave it as a proven floor and stop calling it a capacity.

### D. Admitting a model: loud first, or consolidate first?

The founder's question, verbatim: *"adding, removing, or changing models in the harness should be an entirely trivial affair (by design) and should not require major code changes to enable researchers to do so. The question is, is it? Have we adequately addressed this particular design objective?"*

Answered 2 ways. Structurally, an AST walk finds 156 per-model tables across 67 files, but that total overstates the answer about threefold and the split is load-bearing: 86 sites are tests, which guard the roster rather than admit to it; 50 are historical one-off experiment runners whose edit would alter what those runs did; 5 are measurement scripts; 3 are simulated-seat tools. The LIVE harness is 12 tables across 6 files plus one per-model registry file.

So the number of edits, 12 tables and 1 registry file, is modest, and the failure mode is not. A model that does not exist, Kimi, was put through the real resolution chain. Of 7 steps, 5 default SILENTLY and only 2 fail loudly: 71.4286 per cent, Wilson [35.8934, 91.7781], Clopper-Pearson [29.0421, 96.3307]. A researcher who misses 1 table gets a seat that RUNS while losing whatever that table gated.

The assessment is that the loudness comes first and the consolidation second, because a single registration point that still defaults silently would hide 12 misses behind 1. What the loud failure should be — a raise, a refusal to dispatch that seat, or a run-level preflight — is the founder's call, and it is also question 3 of the free panel's brief.
