# The cap is a switch, not a dial, and trimming the directives cannot fix what the models receive

2026-10-14, 23:53 BST. Branch sim/shakedown-2026-09-29.

## Why This Exists

The founder asked for every remaining fault on the test board to be fixed, then for the owed panel round to be run, then for a summary and whatever rulings are left. He also said that if his energy and the hour allow, he would leave the simulated run and the programme of study to run overnight while he sleeps. This document is the summary, and the rulings still waiting are listed at the end. There are 2 of them, and 1 is a choice between 2 well argued positions rather than an open problem.

## The Single Most Important Finding

The per model character caps on the core directive do not do what everyone involved, including the founder and the orchestrator, assumed they did. They are not a dial that delivers more text at a higher setting. They are a switch.

The core formal directive is 27,803 characters. The caps are 4,000, 6,000, 8,000 and 12,000 depending on the model. Because the source text exceeds every one of those numbers, the composer takes the same branch in all 5 cases and emits a reduced rendering. Measured across the whole configured range, the number of distinct delivered lengths is exactly 1. Every model receives the same 2,503 characters whatever its cap says.

What those 2,503 characters contain matters more than their number. One of the panel seats printed the actual delivered text. It is a dump of 43 configuration keys plus 5 lines of behavioural instruction. It contains no recursive self assessment equation, no corroboration model, no requirement to supply a runnable falsifier, and no falsifier integrity section. The sections most directly about the property this project exists to establish do not arrive.

## And This Changes the Answer To a Question the Founder Asked Yesterday

He asked which sections of the directive set should be cut, once the historical chatter is trimmed out. That question now has a definite answer, and the answer is that cutting does not help.

To bring the core file under even the most generous cap, 15,803 characters have to go. That is 56.8392 percent of the file. The broadest of the 4 chatter predicates measured removes 4,114 characters, which is 14.7970 percent, and leaves 11,689 characters still over the cap. An automated theorem prover was asked whether any cut within the measured range could reach the threshold and returned unsatisfiable. A symbolic algebra system and an arbitrary precision library agree on the share to 4 decimal places.

So trimming the historical narrative is worth doing on its own merits, because it is narrative rather than specification, and it cannot change what a single model receives. Only removing or raising the cap can do that.

## What the Panel Returned

Two rounds of 2 free seats ran tonight, with no paid dispatch and none authorised. The second was the joint half owed on the first, and the harness confirmed it carried its parent's replies word for word rather than merely being labelled as joint.

On the caps, both seats refused outright removal, using the project's own additive standard against the orchestrator's proposal. The standard requires a committed measurement showing the replacement is better on a named property before anything is removed, and no such measurement exists in either direction. One seat then specified the measurement in full and supplied the power arithmetic: at the effect size worth detecting, each arm needs 44 determinate outcomes, the archive yields a median of 28 per run, so 2 runs per arm are needed and a single run per arm is underpowered. The other seat put the shortfall at a factor of 6.7. The important part is that one half of the question needs no statistics at all: whether the full text delivers all 16 section headings instead of none is deterministic and already measured.

On the never attempted findings, the founder's recollection was confirmed by 2 independent producers and then bounded. Of the findings carrying no falsifier, 83.1389 percent predate the falsification machinery entirely, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 81.3822 to 84.7609 percent. April and May of this year are 100 percent falsifier free for the simple reason that nothing existed to carry one. A panel 11 days ago had already called this an era artefact, and the reason that conclusion did not carry forward is that its producing script was never committed. The part that is live is narrower and real: of the findings marked untoolable, none predate the falsifier verification component, which landed on the 3rd of June.

On their own earlier work, both seats changed position. One refuted its own proposed amendment to the core directive. The other refuted both of the positions held in the blind round on the question of whether any finding has ever been genuinely irreducible.

## The One Disagreement, Kept Rather Than Settled

The seats disagree about which definition of chatter the project should adopt, and both arguments are good. One argues for the paragraph level definition, on the ground that a cut operates on a removable unit and a definition that marks single lines cannot answer the question of what to remove; it measures 89.7959 percent of its hits inside sections an earlier review already marked as compressible. The other argues for the narrow definition, on the ground that the purpose is a deletion list and the costly mistake is deleting operative instruction; it shows that the previously published definition is wrong between a sixth and a half of the time, and rejects the paragraph level one because paragraph expansion cannot tell a provenance stamp from the rule it stamps.

Both reject the definition used in yesterday's report. This is recorded as a disagreement rather than resolved by agreement between models, because agreement between models is not evidence in this project.

THE BOARD

The full test board was 32 failures, 11,152 passes and 18 skips. It now stands at 2 failures, and neither should be repaired.

Of the 30 cleared, 14 had a single cause: the morning report generator was refusing to run because its authored detail file still carried entries for 2 tasks that had closed. Several others were faults introduced during the evening's own work, including 2 places where a missing statistics library would have silently removed a confidence interval from a reported figure, and 1 test that set and leaked the environment variable which disables the guard protecting against spending money on a defective brief.

The 2 that remain are deliberate. One is the task list burndown check, which requires that work closed outpaces work discovered on the most recent day, and tonight discovered 1 item and closed none, so its failure is honest and forcing it green would be gaming the burndown check itself. The other is a convention question about which standard a long lived tracker declares, where restamping it would assert the file meets a standard it does not meet.

## Four of the Orchestrator'S own Instruments were Wrong Tonight

Each was caught by checking what the instrument would do before trusting what it said. A test of whether a seat had read its partner's reply matched on a bare substring, and scored a seat as having read the parent round because the joint round's name contains the parent's name. A repair applied to a planted defect rewrote an average as well as the divisor it was meant to change. A citation was added to the one file in the repository that is forbidden from vouching for a script, because that file is part of the checker. And a report of intervals that were wider than computed, which the tool explicitly labels as valid, was read as a list of failures.

The pattern is consistent and worth stating plainly: the failures were in the measuring, not in the reasoning about what was measured, and they were all found by asking what could make a check return the wrong answer.

## What Needs the Founder'S Ruling

There are 2 items, and the other 2 that were open earlier are now settled without him.

First, the caps. Both seats refuse removal without a measurement and 1 has specified that measurement precisely. The deterministic half of it, whether the uncapped text delivers all 16 headings rather than none, is already answered. The decision is whether to add a 2 arm comparison to the overnight programme, which needs 2 runs per arm rather than 1, or to accept the deterministic result on the headings and remove the caps on that basis alone.

Second, which definition of chatter the project adopts, between the 2 the seats argue for. This decides a deletion list and nothing else, since the finding above establishes that no deletion can change what models receive.

Settled without him: the prose target for the fourth arm stays as it is, because it is proven and has produced findings in 2 archived runs, so the overnight run is the programme exactly as written with prior runs to compare against. And the measured capability module both seats delivered stays out of the tree, because one of them flagged its own copy as not connected to anything, and connecting it would change how work is assigned to seats, which is a design decision rather than a repair.
