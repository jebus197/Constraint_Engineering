DECISION 1. IS A MODEL'S POSITION THE SAME LIST AS ITS FUNCTION

WHAT IT IS. A model in an experiment can be described 2 ways at once: where it sits, meaning 1 of several equal participants against the model running the experiment, and what it does, meaning sorting findings, writing falsifiers, reviewing. The question is whether those are 1 list or 2.

WHAT IT AFFECTS. 2 entries, the role layer and the general purpose question, and through them the demotion rule, because that rule acts on whatever a role turns out to be. The role layer itself is already built as the 5th section of the configuration system.

WHAT YOU DECIDE. A single list, or 2 separate lists.

RECOMMENDATION, against the SIMPLEST SUFFICIENT standard. A single list, which is your own reading: you wrote that a classifier is clearly both a position and a function and that separating them makes no sense. It is also the smaller change, because the role layer already accepts 1 list without modification, where 2 lists would need a second list, a rule for how they combine, and a guard for the combinations that make no sense. Nothing already built is withdrawn under either answer.


DECISION 2. WHAT COUNTS AS A SEAT BEING RIGHT IN A ROUND

WHAT IT IS. The demotion rule needs a definition of a seat being right before it can be wired to anything. You supplied the primary candidate yourself: a finding marked closed by the harness registry.

WHAT IT AFFECTS. The demotion rule's wiring, and nothing else today, because there is still no live demotion mechanism to connect it to.

WHAT YOU DECIDE. Which of 4 definitions counts: a finding marked closed, a finding resolved favourably, a finding a tool confirmed, or a finding not withdrawn.

RECOMMENDATION, against the SIMPLEST SUFFICIENT standard, and it is a recommendation to choose rather than a recommendation of 1 answer. Your suggestion was measured against the other 3 rather than adopted, because adopting a proxy because you suggested it would be the same error as adopting a proxy because it was convenient. The 4 definitions order the seats almost independently: worst pairwise agreement is 0.600000 across the whole set and 0.200000 within each population, on a scale where 1 is perfect agreement. So whichever is chosen decides who would be demoted, and that is a decision rather than a measurement. Adopting yours silently would have hidden it inside a number.

AND A SECOND FINDING THAT BOUNDS ALL 4. A demotion rule run across the current data would demote the entire paid panel and keep every simulated stand in. On 3 of the 4 definitions the 2 populations are completely separated, every real seat scoring below every stand in, with a Mann Whitney p value of 0.002165. The right rate, which runs from 0.262899 to 0.433557 across the real seats and from 0.610390 to 0.837500 across the stand ins, is dominated by run type and not by capability, so any wiring must compare within run type. Nothing in the rule currently says so.

Recording the problem beside the value loses nothing and is reversible. Correcting the labels is also defensible and it is your call, which is why both are set out.
