The Specialist Cells. What They Are For, What The Panel Decided, And The Brief For The Experiments Ahead

2026-07-28, 20:45 BST.


What A Specialist Cell Actually Is

A specialist cell is best understood as a subject matter checkpoint. During a review, the five models make claims of many different kinds. Some are claims about mathematics, some about statistics, some about chemistry or engineering or physics. A specialist cell does three things with such a claim. It recognises what kind of claim it is. It hands the claim to the right checking tool, the algebra system for a mathematical claim, the statistics library for a statistical one, the chemistry balancer for a reaction. And it reports back what the tool found.

The crucial point is what a specialist cell is not. It is not another artificial intelligence giving another opinion. It is a router that connects each claim to a mechanical calculator that cannot be argued with. This is the project's founding rule, tools decide, not votes, made real for each separate subject.

There are eight of these subject checkpoints, and all eight are now switched on: mathematics, statistics, biology, information science, software, physics, chemistry, and engineering.


What They Are For

The project record is unusually clear about their purpose, and the panel confirmed it. The specialist cells are the noise filter that makes the whole convergence system honest. The mathematics that decides when a review is finished only works if the findings being counted are genuine. If unverified claims were counted, the system would be measuring chatter. The cells sit upstream of that count: only claims that survive their tool checking count as real discoveries. Their presence is never decoration. If they do not visibly improve the outcome, they fail their own test, and the panel was asked to define exactly what that test should be.


What The Five Models Decided

The panel reached firm decisions on five questions, with disagreements kept on the record rather than smoothed away.

First, how to test the cells. Four of the five models, independently, said the same thing: write four separate test documents, one per subject, chemistry, engineering, physics, and biology, each between fifteen and twenty five thousand characters, each deliberately seeded with planted errors. The planted errors are the whole trick. They make each document an exam with an answer key. We know exactly what is wrong in each document, so we can measure precisely whether the cells catch it. One model disagreed, proposing one big mixed document run four times instead. The disagreement was recorded, but the majority position stands, partly because the mixed document would cost the same and partly because one unusually hard section in one subject would smear the scores of everything else.

Second, the panel found and repaired holes in the existing test plans. The chemistry plan had no section testing logical reasoning and none testing statistics, even though the chemistry checkpoint is wired to check both. The engineering plan had no logic section. The panel wrote the missing sections, complete with specific planted traps. Two examples give the flavour. One planted claim will say that pumping an inert gas into a sealed reaction vessel shifts the chemical equilibrium, which sounds plausible and is false, and the checking tools can prove it false. Another will present an error calculation that simply adds two uncertainties together to get eight percent, when the correct combination gives about five point eight percent. Any model that waves such claims through gets caught by the answer key.

Third, and most importantly, the panel defined the pass mark, the earn their keep test. To be declared ready for the big benchmark, each subject checkpoint must do four things in its exam. It must catch every single planted error, and the credit must belong to the checkpoint itself, not to the ordinary review happening around it. It must never condemn a true statement, the planted true claims must all survive. It must change at least one outcome, which is measured by running the same exam twice, once with the checkpoint switched on and once with it off, and comparing. And on a completely clean document with no errors, it must not disturb convergence at all. One model added a sensible warning that was adopted: if the five reviewing models happen to be highly accurate that day, there is simply no noise to filter, and the checkpoint must not be marked a failure for having nothing to catch.

Fourth, the panel answered the deepest question, which reaches beyond these experiments to Bench Run 2: what does checking look like when there is no code? The twenty seven benchmark problems are real science and engineering questions, prove this theorem, design this steel column, balance this electrochemical reaction. There is no source file to point to. All five models converged, without dissent, on the same design. A finding cites the step or equation in the solution it disputes, quotes the claim, states the assumptions, and attaches a small runnable check written in the language of the right tool: an algebra script for a proof step, the reaction balancer for a chemistry claim, a unit and load calculation for an engineering claim. The check must fail if and only if the disputed claim is genuinely wrong, exactly as the current code falsifiers do. The existing verification engine, the machinery that re-runs every check independently and refuses to accept anything on a model's word, is reused unchanged. This unanimous design is now the specification for the benchmark bridge.

Fifth, the final head to head experiment. The old plan pondered testing the system on its own runner. All five models rejected that as circular, the measuring instrument must not become the thing measured. Instead, unanimously: a sixth purpose written document, loaded with ten to fifteen planted defects ranging from obvious to subtle, giving the head to head comparison of the feedback and divergence mechanisms real headroom and a real answer key.


How This Maps Onto The Experiments Ahead

Experiment 47 is separate from all of this. It is the next module review, the divergence machinery, already designed, nothing to decide.

Experiments 48 through 51 are the four subject exams: chemistry first, then engineering, the two whose wiring changed most recently and is least validated, then physics, then biology. Each is a normal review run of the seeded document, judged against the answer key and the pass mark above.

Experiment 52 is the head to head factorial on the sixth document.

Then Bench Run 2, where the checkpoints do the job all of this exists to prepare them for: helping decide, mechanically, whether answers to real frontier science questions are actually correct.


What Awaits The Founder's Approval

Four decisions: the four separate exams and their order; the pass mark as just described; the no code checking design as the benchmark specification; and the sixth document as the head to head target. On approval, the remaining sequence locks in at roughly one hundred seventy to two hundred sixty dollars against a balance of four hundred fifty two.
