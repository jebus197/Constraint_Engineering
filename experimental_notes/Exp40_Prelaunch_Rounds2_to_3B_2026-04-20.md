# Experiment 40 Prelaunch Review, Rounds 2 Through Final Arbitration

20 April 2026.

This note continues the record begun in `Exp40_Prelaunch_Round1_Panel_2026-04-20.md`. Round 1 was an open-ended five-model panel review that produced broad agreement on the closure work but left four live questions unresolved. What follows describes how those four questions were taken to convergence in four further rounds, the reasoning the panel adopted, and the single locked pre-launch specification that resulted.

## Why four further rounds

The founder's standing rule is that a panel of models is not meant to hand the human a menu of options. Its job is to produce a single answer to each question, with the reasoning on display, so the human can approve or reject a definitive position rather than adjudicate between calculators. Round 1 produced a menu on four questions. The remaining rounds were structured to close each question in turn, under progressively tighter constraints, until a single answer survived.

The five models through all rounds were Gemini 3.1 Pro, Codex GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6, ChatGPT GPT-5.4, and DeepSeek R1-0528. Each received the same briefing at each round and answered independently without seeing other replies, which is the star topology the panel itself unanimously preferred in Round 1. Full logs live under `bench/logs/confer_exp40_prelaunch_round2a/`, `bench/logs/confer_exp40_prelaunch_round2b/`, `bench/logs/confer_exp40_prelaunch_round3/`, and `bench/logs/confer_exp40_prelaunch_round3b/`.

## Round 2A, yield or refute

The rule for Round 2A was simple. Each model saw the Round 1 consensus and the remaining points of divergence, and was required to either yield to the consensus with reasons or refute it with reasons. Open-ended re-proposal was not permitted.

The outcome was that the panel aligned on three standing policies. First, the activation-now rule for items currently in shadow: anything that has already passed review and could be enabled should be enabled now rather than deferred to the experiment that exercises it. Second, the preservation of runner version one as the default for Experiment 40, on the grounds that it is the first runner with demonstrated convergence evidence and that test-count parity alone does not justify retiring an instrument with experiment-level evidence behind it. Third, the full fifteen-experiment plan, Experiments 40 through 54, rather than shorthand that compresses thirteen intermediate experiments into a single integration phrase.

What remained were four technical questions. One asked where a conditional novelty ceiling should live, in the runtime or computed after the fact. One asked for the exact fields in a reason-trace schema that models must fill when they change their position. One asked for the exact predicates that prove runner version one's behaviour has been preserved. And one asked which topology label to write into the runner configuration file. These four questions were taken through Rounds 2B, 3, and 3B.

## Round 2B, yield or refute

By Round 2B four models had yielded at least once on at least one question. Codex yielded on the topology label, accepting the star variant without the paired-challenge extension. Claude Opus yielded on a literal-substring check for quotations, accepting Gemini's stricter mechanical form over its own similarity-based form. Claude Opus and Gemini both yielded on counterfactual sensitivity, accepting ChatGPT's tighter perturbation protocol. DeepSeek yielded on structured telemetry, accepting Codex's explicit field set over its own looser version. ChatGPT largely held its prior position, though it absorbed two refinements from others.

What this left was genuine residual divergence on narrow technical points. The runtime-versus-post-hoc question split three to two. The field set for the reason-trace schema was ninety percent agreed, with variance on whether two particular enum values should be allowed. The preservation-predicate families were unanimous at the structural level, with variance on perturbation magnitude and selection rule. These three points, and the topology label, went into Round 3.

## Round 3, final lock

Round 3 was the final-lock round and introduced an additional set of constraints the panel had to satisfy. Answers had to be computationally efficient where that applied, rational in both human and machine terms, robust, effective, and had to produce a system that humans could use without friction. Demonstrable meant defensible to third-party scrutiny, internally and externally self-consistent, and a seamless fit with the rest of the project's schema. The briefing made clear that these constraints were cumulative with the standing policies from Round 2A.

Under these constraints Round 3 produced a unanimous agreement on the topology label — all five chose the plain star variant for Experiment 40, with the paired-challenge variant held back for a subsequent experiment. It produced a unanimous agreement on the ten-field structure of the reason-trace schema. It produced a unanimous agreement on the four-family structure of the preservation predicates. But on the runtime-versus-post-hoc question it produced a three-to-two split that did not close. Three models chose post-hoc computation; two chose a runtime code path.

A split at this stage could not be resolved by another yield-or-refute round, because the same arguments would recur. Instead, Round 3B drafted a single canonical answer per question, from the consensus and the Round 3 reasoning, and required each model to approve or refute with a literal quoted substring. Under the founder's synthesis qualifier, the canonical answer would stand unless a refutation was accompanied by a counter-proposal that was demonstrably better under the constraint box.

## Round 3B, canonical-draft arbitration

On the runtime-versus-post-hoc question, the canonical answer was post-hoc only. The ceiling is computed at report generation, from fields the runner already logs, and there is no runtime code path. The rationale is load-bearing. Experiment 40 is the preservation run for runner version one, and anything that changes runtime behaviour, even by emitting a boolean flag, changes what the preservation evidence is evidence of. Post-hoc computation is cheap, it runs once over the complete log, and it is reproducible by any third-party reviewer with the log and the declared policy. It satisfies the preservation rule, the efficiency rule, and the third-party defensibility rule in a single move.

Claude Opus, which had voted for runtime in Round 3, yielded to the post-hoc answer in Round 3B and named its own earlier error — it had overweighted effectiveness and underweighted the preservation rule. Gemini did the same. The runtime-versus-post-hoc split closed at five to zero in approval of post-hoc only. The literal quoted substring each model approved was: *"Any new runtime code path — even one that only emits a boolean flag — changes v1's runtime behavior and confounds the preservation evidence."*

On the reason-trace schema, the canonical answer was a ten-field record. The schema names which issue a model is responding to, whether its position has changed, which peer it is referring to, the literal substring from that peer's prior-round answer, the reason type drawn from a closed six-value list, the free-text reason, the hash of the prior position, the hash of the new position, a compact representation of the state change, and a compliance risk flag computed by the runner from the other nine fields rather than written by the model. Two values are explicitly excluded from the reason-type enumeration: the catch-all that would let a model hide a novel failure mode behind a non-committal label, and the agreement value that is already covered by the unchanged-position case.

Three models approved the schema in full. Two models partially refuted it. ChatGPT wanted the agreement value re-admitted, on the grounds that it would be needed for a rare case where an unchanged position required a fresh citation. DeepSeek wanted the target-model field opened to free text, on the grounds that a hardcoded enum of the current panel's five models would not generalise to other panels. Under the synthesis qualifier, both refutations were weighed against the canonical answer. The schema's closed enumerations make it machine-checkable without similarity heuristics, which is the efficiency and the non-gameability rule taken together. The refutations argued from hypothetical future needs rather than demonstrated present cost. The canonical schema stood.

On the preservation predicates, the canonical answer was four families of tests, each expressed as a deterministic predicate over fields the runner already logs, and each covering a distinct causal property of the runner's behaviour. Math-path fidelity tests whether the mathematics was load-bearing. Correction fidelity tests whether upstream revisions propagated to downstream dependents. Counterfactual sensitivity tests whether a small perturbation of a load-bearing parameter produces a corresponding change in the outcome. Convergence stability tests whether the panel's divergence actually closed. The experiment gate is the logical conjunction of the four — all must pass.

Three refinements were adopted from the panel. Gemini caught a false-negative trap in Correction fidelity. The canonical predicate would fail the test if a model proactively revisited a downstream claim without any upstream trigger, which would be good behaviour being penalised as bad. The fix is to require downstream revisits to be greater than or equal to upstream revisions, rather than equal. Codex caught a mismatch between the Counterfactual sensitivity protocol and its acceptance predicate. The protocol names four perturbations per selected dispatch — two parameters, each moved up and down by a fixed magnitude — but the acceptance predicate as drafted only required one replay per selected dispatch. The fix is four replays per dispatch, explicit in the predicate. ChatGPT caught a subtler point, that the coverage expression should round up rather than down, so that a small log with one applicable dispatch still produces at least one probed dispatch, and that the final round of the panel must show zero unresolved divergences for the preservation to count as locked.

On the topology label, the canonical answer was the plain star variant, and five of five approved.

## The locked specification in plain English

Experiment 40 will run with five models answering independently from the same briefing. Each round's outputs will be written into a ten-field schema that records who changed what and why, with verifiable links back to the text they are responding to. When the experiment is over, the report generator will run four families of predicate tests over the logs, and will separately compute a conditional novelty ceiling from the same logs. If all four predicate families pass, and the ceiling is consistent with the logged parameters, the preservation of runner version one is confirmed. No part of this adds a new runtime code path to runner version one. No part of this depends on a similarity heuristic or a tunable threshold.

## What is still open

The only decision the founder must make before Experiment 40 launches is the activation-sequence gate. Three items have been waiting through five rounds of the panel. The first is a small fix to the mathematics sandbox that currently returns uncertain on every claim. The second is a runtime assertion wrapper that makes a planned future wiring event visible at review. The third is the live-promotion flip for the physics, chemistry, and engineering specialist cells that have been built and tested but are still running in shadow. The standing policy is that items in this state should be activated now rather than deferred. All five rounds of the panel have been consistent with that policy; the founder has not yet ruled.

The remaining work once the gate is cleared is implementation of the locked schema in the runner, implementation of the locked predicates in the preservation audit harness, post-hoc wiring of the conditional novelty ceiling into the report generator, and setting the topology to star in the runner configuration file. None of these touches runner version one's runtime behaviour.

## Summary

Five rounds of panel review, four of them under progressively tighter constraints, produced a single locked pre-launch specification for Experiment 40. The topology is star. The reason-trace schema has ten fields with closed enumerations and a runner-derived compliance flag. The preservation predicates are four deterministic families with a conjunction gate. The conditional novelty ceiling is post-hoc only. What remains is one founder decision and the implementation of the specification in the existing runner. No code changes were made to the bench this session; the full test suite at twelve hundred and fifty passes remains green.
