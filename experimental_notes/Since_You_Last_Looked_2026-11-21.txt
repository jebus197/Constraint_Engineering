WHY IT REFILLED, AND WHOSE DOING THAT IS

From the trough of 7 on 11 November the queue has climbed at 1.264423 decisions a day, with a p value of 0.045659, an r squared of 0.672484 and a 95 per cent confidence interval from 0.399666 to 2.129180 a day. 2 independent fitting routes agree on that slope to 4 parts in 10 to the 16th.

Over the same 13 days I raised 75 entries and 57 closed. Arrivals outrun closures on the point estimate, although those 2 intervals do overlap, so the gap itself is not established at that confidence.

The refill is arrivals. Arrivals are mine to raise. Your clearance rate was never the problem.

WHEN DOES THIS END

The honest answer has 2 halves and the first one is not comfortable.

On the whole backlog, measured over the last 13 days, it does not converge. More arrives than closes. If nothing changes about how often I raise an entry, there is no end point to quote, and I am not going to manufacture an end point that the data does not support.

On your own queue it is different, and your queue is what you actually experience day to day. You took it from 29 to 7 in 4 days. The net change since the 29 is minus 8, so you are still ahead of where you started. What has happened since is that I kept raising decisions faster than you were asked to absorb them.

That makes the lever mine rather than yours. The things that would actually shorten this are: raising fewer items to you, routing more of them as my own work, and marking the ones that only need ratifying so they stop reading as questions. The third of those was built today and is why the number is 11 rather than 21.

WHAT HAPPENED TODAY

3 repairs were built as 1 batch on your instruction to do both and stream the test phase.

First, the memory index that holds the project's standing lessons had 14 characters of room left before saving would start refusing. Your account of the design was right and the earlier diagnosis was wrong. The automatic trim has existed since early October; what nobody had written down is that it could only reach a part of the file accounting for 0.063955 of its length, while the part that actually grows accounts for 0.810667 and is excluded on purpose. A permanent ceiling now runs on every save. The index went from 24,486 characters to 22,938, and the room left went from 14 characters to 1,563.

Second, the check that runs before every commit has a phase lasting about 44 minutes that produced no visible output at all while it ran, because the output was being collected into a variable. It now streams, roughly a line every 36 seconds. The obvious 1 line repair would have quietly broken the whole check: piping the output through a copying tool shows it on screen but makes the shell report whether the copy succeeded rather than whether the tests passed, and against deliberately failing runs that version reported success 3 times out of 3.

Third, your own decision list now says, where the record warrants it, that the work behind a decision is already built or its premise already dead. That is what turned 21 into 11.

The first commit check refused all 3, and it was right to. It reported 18 failing checks against 5,131 passing. 10 of the 18 were broken by my own change to the commit script, because 5 separate checks were using the line I replaced as a landmark and I had not traced them first. A 19th failure was invisible to that check entirely, because the file holding it is not on the list of checks the gate runs. All 19 are now repaired and verified, 1,309 tests passing with none failing.

The same pass found that the commit check had run none of this batch's own 70 tests, because all 4 of the new check files were missing from its list. A census written on 13 November measures exactly that gap and reported 4 of 245 missing, every one of them written that day. They are on the list now, and their cost was measured before adding them at 70 tests in 64.88 seconds, about 2 and a half per cent of a full check.

2 new issues were raised today rather than fixed. B204 is the one in your list above. B205 is mine: the gate that prods me to keep working told me 6 times that I had committed when nothing had been committed, because it looks for the words git commit anywhere in the session rather than asking whether a commit happened, and the commit script contains those words as advice. It also called an entry unstarted whose work is built. Neither false sentence reaches the gate's actual decision, so it is a reporting fault and not a defeatable check, and both repairs are specified but deliberately not applied, on the precedent that a repair found while clearing a refusal is raised rather than folded into a batch already agreed.
