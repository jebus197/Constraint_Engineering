## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] The seat's recommendation, in one line

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] Adopt it — **but define it by REFERENCE to the five material-defect categories
already live in `bench/directives/universal/cdsfl_core_formal.md` §10, rather than
as fresh text.** One definition, two call sites.

## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] Why by reference, and it is measured rather than argued

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] The project has already paid for a standard that was declared twice in different
words. `.claude/CLAUDE.md` records it: the composability standard reaches every
panel seat through the dispatcher's SYSTEM prompt with the demonstration
requirement intact, while the BRIEF TEMPLATE states the same standard as *"say
whether the repairs compose, conflict, or are independent"* — the weak
non-interference form the founder's own definition excludes by name. **2
declarations of 1 standard exist and they disagree**, and the measured consequence
was that 10 of 10 uses of the word across the decisions file and the morning
report used the excluded sense, Wilson 95% [0.722467, 1.000000].

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] A 4th standard about whether a change *matters* would be the second declaration of
materiality, because §10 already enumerates it: wrong result, hard-constraint
violation, verification-integrity corruption, silent evidence loss,
unreproducibility. That file is 33,108 characters and reaches every working seat
through the composer. Writing a fresh definition of materiality beside it
reproduces the exact fault above.

## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] Why a 4th standard is nonetheless not redundant

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] §10 scopes materiality to a FINDING inside a review round — what a reviewer may
stop enumerating. It says nothing about a FIX. The existing 3 standards all govern
the SHAPE of a fix: additive asks whether it fits the aims and fills a gap or
beats an incumbent; simplest sufficient asks whether it is the smallest change
reaching the root cause; composability asks whether a pair beats its parts. **None
of the three asks whether the person using the system would notice.** That gap is
real, and his wording names it.

## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] PROPOSED WORDING, for him to accept, amend or reject

*[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE]*
> **4. REAL-WORLD APPLICABILITY.** A change must have a meaningful consequence
> for the human in the loop. Invoking this standard requires NAMING that
> consequence — which of the five material-defect categories of
> `cdsfl_core_formal.md` §10 the change bears on (wrong result, hard-constraint
> violation, verification-integrity corruption, silent evidence loss,
> unreproducibility), and for whom. **Asserting that a consequence exists is not
> naming one, and a change whose consequence cannot be named is not refused — it
> is REPORTED as having none, and ranked accordingly.** This standard governs
> PRIORITY, never admissibility: it decides what is worth doing first, and it may
> not be used to refuse a fix that the other three standards admit. Where the
> consequence falls outside the five categories, say so and describe it in full
> rather than forcing it into one.

## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] The two guards this wording would need before it binds

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] 1. **It must be reachable.** `additive-standard`'s symmetric clause: a standard
   nothing reaches is an addition that does nothing, and this project has 11
   confirmed defects of exactly that shape since 2026-08-01 against 0 removals of
   something needed. A 4th standard needs the same treatment the 3rd got — carried
   into the dispatcher SYSTEM prompt, and the brief template's wording fixed at the
   same time, or it will drift the way composability drifted.
2. **It must not become a veto.** The scoping sentence above exists because a
   materiality standard that can REFUSE work is a mechanism for declining
   unglamorous repairs, and `cdsfl_core_formal.md`'s Objective section already
   forbids exactly that: *"Never downgrade a material defect for being
   uninteresting."* Priority-only keeps the two consistent.

## [CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] What would refute this recommendation

[CDSFL:NOT-IN-FORCE] A case where a change has a real consequence for the human that maps to NONE of
§10's five categories and is not describable as an extension of them. That would
show the reference is too narrow and fresh text is needed after all. The
repository can produce the test today: enumerate the open entries' stated
consequences and check each against the five. It has not been run here, and this
recommendation is therefore marked as resting on an unexecuted check.
