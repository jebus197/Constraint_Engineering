# The project's own specification was never delivered to a model, and 4 mechanisms were keeping it out

2026-10-15, 02:28 BST. Branch `sim/shakedown-2026-09-29`, parent commit `748fc044`.

## What this document is

On 2026-10-14 the founder rejected the orchestrator's proposal to trim the core directive set. His words: *"7000 or so words for a system prompt is hardly excessive for any modern model? Why should we need to cut it at all? The whole point of measuring what our models can do and then giving them exactly the kind of work they are best suited to, is that no resource and no model's capabilities are wasted, by either giving them work they demonstrably can't do, or work that is demonstrably too simple for our more capable models. Where does cutting core system directives come into that picture at all?"* And, on what the caps were ever for: *"as far as any caps have ever existed, their intention was to never feed any model more work than they were demonstrably able to handle, especially for weaker models like Deepseek, and we built considerable machinery around this principle. There was never an intent as far as I recall to applying this same reasoning to the core operational directives themselves."*

On 2026-10-15 he ruled that the fixes be applied before any further run: *"Apply any outstanding fixes you have identified first, including any as yet unbuilt/unapplied machinery, before you begin the runs. Make sure you mark these items as high priority considerations in the accompanying/outstanding program of study."* And on the cost of changing what every model receives: *"We cannot go back and change time, so the confounds discovered in the last 24 hours can only be noted and added to the record honestly."*

The distinction he drew — a cap on WORK is measured machinery, a cap on SPECIFICATION is a silent loss — turned out to be implementable as a single line in code, and drawing it exposed 4 separate mechanisms that were keeping the specification out. This document records all 4, what each cost, what was changed, and the 3 further defects found while fixing them that are recorded rather than repaired.

Every figure here is produced by `scripts/the_spec_is_not_work_2026-10-15.py`, `scripts/would_the_severity_promotion_reach_anything_2026-10-15.py` or the scripts those name, and every proportion carries a 95 per cent interval computed by 2 independent tools.

## The specification, and what the caps were measured against

The core formal directive is 27,803 characters, 3,991 words, 136 blocks by the composer's own splitter, of which 22 are HARD by the project's own `_block_is_hard` predicate. The operational directive is 46,374 characters and 7,070 words. Together they are 74,177 characters and 11,061 words.

The per-model `max_directive_chars` values are 12,000 for `opus_4_6`, 8,000 for `chatgpt_5_4` and `gemini_3_1_pro`, 6,000 for `codex_5_3` and 4,000 for `deepseek_v3`. **All 5 are below the specification — 5 of 5, 100.0000 per cent, Wilson [56.5518 per cent, 100.0000 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [47.8176 per cent, 100.0000 per cent]** — and the most generous of them is smaller by a factor of 2.3169.

## Mechanism 1: the loader chose the reduced form by the work cap

`_load_universal_directive` decided between the full specification and a reduced rendering by comparing the specification's length against `max_directive_chars`. Since every cap is below the specification, the reduced form was served unconditionally, and the docstring's promise of *"Full text for large-context models"* described a branch no model could reach. This is finding C0040, raised at severity 0.88, confirmed on 2026-09-10, and deliberately left unfixed because changing what every model receives invalidates replay against the archive.

`PhenotypeTransform` now carries 2 fields instead of 1. `max_directive_chars` keeps its value and its job: it bounds the domain checklist, the review target, the code extracts and the adversarial brief — the quantity the harness measures per experiment and the quantity the routing ladder respects. `spec_chars` bounds the specification and is `None` for every model, which means deliver it whole. The loader consults `spec_chars`. `deepseek_v3`'s work settings are untouched: the 4,000-character bound, the example stripping, the rationale stripping, the language simplification and the table compression all stand.

One per-model setting had to move. `deepseek_v3` carried `format_style="minimal"`, which was the only route by which a style setting decided whether the specification was delivered, and it is now `"concise"`. The reduced form remains reachable for any seat by setting `spec_chars`.

## Mechanism 2: the reduced form carried none of the HARD specification

The reduced form was 2,503 characters of configuration key-value lines plus the bullets of one section. Measured by verbatim containment over the 22 HARD blocks: **0 of 22 carried, 0.0000 per cent, Wilson [0.0000 per cent, 14.8655 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [0.0000 per cent, 15.4373 per cent]**. It now carries **22 of 22, 100.0000 per cent, Wilson [85.1345 per cent, 100.0000 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [84.5627 per cent, 100.0000 per cent]**, at 8,367 characters.

Among the 22 it dropped were the whole of the section titled *Falsifier Integrity — Do Not Reach for the Answer*, and within it the instructions *"Do not read a scoring key or answer file"*, *"Do not modify, delete, weaken or skip a test to make it pass"* and *"Do not terminate early to obtain a passing result"*. Those are the anti-cheating rules every verdict in this project rests on, and no model had ever been sent them by either path: the operational directive does not carry the section either.

The cause was 2 definitions of HARD inside 1 module, only 1 of which was live code the models saw. `_extract_hard_behavioural_directives` read bullets from the single section headed `## Non-Formalisable Directives` and called that the HARD set. The HARD set is whatever `_block_is_hard` accepts, and that predicate reads MUST, NEVER, HARD, REQUIRED, NON-NEGOTIABLE, FORBIDDEN, DO NOT and ALWAYS across the whole file.

**2 figures are withdrawn, and the instrument was the defect in both.** A first measurement used a probe that takes the first 8 words of a block, joins them with spaces and looks for that string. It space-joins across newlines, so it cannot match a multi-line block against text that keeps its newlines, and 9 of the 22 HARD blocks are multi-line. It reported 1 of 22 for the pre-repair render where the honest figure is 0 of 22, and 13 of 22 for the repaired one where the honest figure is 22 of 22. The producer now records that the withdrawn probe understates by 9.

## Mechanism 3: the conflict resolver deleted the specification, mostly by resolving it against itself

`resolve_layer_conflicts` splits each packet into blocks, assigns each a topic by keyword, and deletes any block whose topic matches an already-kept block with an opposing stance. Its own docstring says it resolves conflicts *"between directives from different layers"*.

Measured over 10 domains with the specification delivered whole, by reconstructing the pre-repair resolver: 42 conflicts, of which **40 were the specification resolved against ITSELF — 95.2381 per cent, Wilson [84.2101 per cent, 98.6842 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [83.8358 per cent, 99.4180 per cent]** — 0 were same-layer, and 2 were genuinely cross-layer. Two blocks of one packet are one author's text at one layer with one specificity score, so every rule the function states has nothing to decide and the tie broke on list order. The specification says "concise" in one section and "full formal proof" in another; those are different scopes, not a contradiction.

**41 of the 42 deletions had a HARD loser** by the resolver's own HARD predicate, which is `constraint_class == "HARD" or _block_is_hard(text)` — the same tuple it sorts on. The universal packet is classed HARD, so 40 of the deletions were removing blocks of the project's own specification, and the 41st was the biomedical requirement that sample size be pre-specified. **An earlier reading of 11 is withdrawn**: it applied `_block_is_hard` to `ConflictRecord.loser_text`, which is truncated to 100 characters and carries no packet class.

Two guards were added. A packet may not resolve against itself. And a HARD directive is recorded as conflicting but never deleted, because rule 1 of the function says "HARD always wins over SOFT" and never licensed deleting a HARD, while `p-pass-hard-precedence` ranks conflicting HARD constraints rather than discarding one. `ConflictRecord` gained a `deleted` field, defaulted to True so every existing construction is unchanged, so a record can distinguish a conflict that was noted from one that removed text.

After the repair, over the same packets: **2 conflicts recorded, 0 HARD deletions, 0 packets altered.**

The 2 surviving cross-layer conflicts are both spurious. `_directive_topic_and_stance` assigns the topic `table` to any text containing the word "table" or a pipe-delimited row, so the specification's formalisation table was recorded as conflicting with the automotive domain's *"Material specification per OEM standard (VW TL, BMW GS, GM GMW, Ford WSS-M)"* and deleted it. Deletion of a cross-layer SOFT loser is therefore retired from the default path and retained behind `CONFLICT_DELETES_SOFT_LOSER`, which is exactly the treatment the mechanical directive pruner received on the founder's ruling of 2026-10-13. What is given up is stated rather than left to be discovered: a model may now receive 2 directives that a cruder reading calls contradictory, and whether that costs anything is what the free runs answer.

## Mechanism 4: the resolver's rebuild was lossy even when it dropped nothing

A fourth defect was found by the guard rather than by reading. The resolver rebuilds each packet by joining its kept blocks with a single newline, while the splitter splits on blank lines and strips every line. So a round trip through the resolver destroyed blank-line structure and indentation even when it deleted nothing: measured on the structural domain packet, 4,515 characters and 18 blocks in, 4,403 characters and 5 blocks out, with 0 blocks lost. Harmless for a flat checklist. Not harmless for a markdown specification carrying fenced blocks, formal notation and tables, all of which are delimited by blank lines.

It surfaced only once the specification was delivered whole, when a new test compared the first 400 characters of the specification against the render and they differed at the first blank line. A packet that loses nothing now passes through byte-identical.

**This defect also accounts for 2 further withdrawn figures.** A first producer reported "HARD deleted: 45" and "SOFT deleted: 475" across the compose grid. Neither measured deletion: both counted blocks that failed to reappear in a re-split of the rebuilt text, which is the round-trip asymmetry and not a loss of content. Under verbatim containment, 0 blocks were lost. That script, `scripts/the_resolver_deletes_its_own_spec_2026-10-15.py`, is retained and now prints its own withdrawal before delegating to the correct producer, because `measured-rate-travels-with-its-script` has an inverse: a withdrawn number needs a record of its withdrawal, or the same mistake stays available.

## Mechanism 5: the cap would have truncated inside the specification

The length cap runs on the final assembled render, and the specification is first in packet order. So delivering it whole with the cap unchanged would have truncated inside the specification and deleted the domain and situation packets outright for all 5 models, since every work bound is below 27,803. The repair that delivers the specification and the repair that keeps the cap meaningful are the same repair, and neither is safe alone.

The cap now slices at the specification's own length plus the separator plus `max_directive_chars`, so the work a model receives is still capped at exactly the per-model value and only the quantity the cap is measured against has moved. The manifest records `work_cap`, `spec_chars_exempt` and `effective_cap` beside the existing `fired`, `chars_cut` and `packets_cut`. Verified on the real compose path: `deepseek_v3` still has the cap fire on a large work payload and still loses situation text, and no model loses any specification text.

## Mechanism 6: the decomposition decision counted a payload it cannot split

Delivering the specification whole adds **25,300 characters** to what a seat receives as its standing instructions, taking that text from 48,877 to 74,177 characters. The decomposition decision was made on the total of those standing instructions plus the round prompt against an 80,000-character hard floor, so work headroom fell from **31,123 characters to 5,823 — a loss of 81.2904 per cent of it.** An automated theorem prover returns unsatisfiable for "a 20,000-character work payload crosses the floor" before the repair and satisfiable after it, so nearly every dispatch in the next run would have been force-decomposed on the size of its own instructions.

Counting those standing instructions is not merely imprecise, it is self-defeating. `_multiturn_fallback` passes them whole into `decomposed_dispatch` and splits only the target, so decomposition re-sends them on every chunk turn. SymPy gives the extra instruction bytes as exactly `S*n`, strictly positive for every chunk count at or above 1; z3 returns unsatisfiable for "decomposing sends no more instruction bytes"; mpmath agrees on the concrete case. So counting them toward the floor triggers a mechanism that multiplies them.

The hard floor now judges the decomposable payload while the fingerprint comparison still judges the total, because the fingerprint's `max_successful_prompt_chars` and `max_failed_prompt_chars` are total prompt sizes an interface actually accepted or rejected. Legacy callers pass neither new argument and are unchanged, verified over 26 payload sizes from 0 to 200,000 with 0 divergences.

**The P-pass of that fix found it was inert on its own, and the fix for that is the more important half.** Reverting the floor basis changed 0 of 3 test cases, because the underlying decision judges the total at every one of its 4 steps and exceeding the static 80,000 fallback still returned the same answer by another route. So a futility guard was added: when the non-decomposable part of the payload already exceeds the effective limit, decomposition is dominated on all 3 measures that matter — per-request bytes, where `S + W/n > L` for every chunk count when `S > L`; total bytes, by `S*n > 0`; and request count, by `n + 1 > 1` — and all 3 negations return unsatisfiable in z3. In that case the monolithic dispatch is sent and the interface is allowed to speak, which is the decision's own stated data-driven design: a rejection records a failure ceiling and every later round decomposes on evidence instead of on a proxy. An explicit `pre_decompose_models` override is honoured over the futility argument, because a derived argument must not silently countermand an instruction.

**This confound would have fired only in simulation.** Against the project's own fingerprint cache, **6 of 6 simulated seats — 100.0000 per cent, Wilson [60.9666 per cent, 100.0000 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [54.0742 per cent, 100.0000 per cent]** — have an effective limit below the 74,177 characters of standing instructions: CC2-SIM at 72,465, ChatGPT-SIM at 65,640, Codex-SIM at 70,922, DeepSeek-SIM at 72,465, Fable-SIM at 69,775 and Gemini-SIM at 67,510. **No paid seat would have**: CC2 sits at 418,685 and DeepSeek at 99,068. A confound that fires only in simulation is exactly the hazard the founder named when he asked why a simulated run would test anything in shadow.

## The severity specification, promoted because a shadow cannot be tested by a simulation

The founder's objection of 2026-10-14: *"why run them in shadow in a simulated run, when the purpose of a simulated run is to test how they will behave in a real experiment and to fix any deficiencies/issues you encounter so that we don't run into those same issues in a paid run, and risk pointlessly burning money we don't have?"*

He is right, and the circle is sharper than he put it. Of the 10 mechanisms the programme revision listed, 6 are live, 1 is a record by design, 1 is a measurement rather than a mechanism, and 2 are genuine record-only shadows — **2 of 10, 20.0000 per cent, Wilson [5.6682 per cent, 50.9838 per cent]**. The severity specification shadow's own promotion criterion asks for non-distortion evidence *from a run*, and a shadow never decides, so a run can never supply that evidence. It would have stayed a shadow for ever and a paid run would have been its first live test. Promotion needed a build, not a setting.

**Whether it would reach any decision was measured before it was built, and the first attempt at that measurement was wrong.** The A4 door is conjunctive: a finding is released only when its severity is sub-critical AND proven by recomputation of its stated R_k. Promoting the specification lowers the severity, which can only make the first half easier, so if the proof half almost never holds the promotion is an addition nothing reaches. A first producer read the wrong archive key and returned 0 entries across 59 runs; the existing shadow reads 3,298, so 0 was an instrument result and not an archive result. Corrected, over 2,994 entries in 59 runs:

- `severity_is_proven` holds for **295 of 2,994 — 9.8530 per cent, Wilson [8.8364 per cent, 10.9726 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [8.8084 per cent, 10.9768 per cent]**. The proof half does open, about 1 entry in 10.
- The A4 population is **196 of 2,994 — 6.5464 per cent, Wilson [5.7149 per cent, 7.4893 per cent]**.
- Of those, **85 — 43.3673 per cent, Wilson [36.6227 per cent, 50.3670 per cent]** — are asserted-critical and specification-sub-critical.
- Of those 85, **7 are also proven and would therefore be released — 8.2353 per cent, Wilson [4.0464 per cent, 16.0360 per cent]**.
- **195 of the 196 A4 entries carry a verification factor of 0.5 — 99.4898 per cent, Wilson [97.1671 per cent, 99.9099 per cent]** — because the A4 population is by definition the entries whose falsifier has not resolved. That is where the whole effect lives.

So it is reached: 7 releases change. `effective_severity` returns the asserted float while the flag is off and `asserted times V` while it is on, with `V` taken from the harness's own falsifier verdict. The key property holds: a disproved finding scores zero regardless of confidence, so the product is annihilated rather than reduced. The proof requirement is not weakened — the release stays conjunctive. One decision site was moved, the A4 door, because that is the site convergence turns on and the measurement that justified the build is a measurement of exactly that door. Every reporting read still shows the asserted number, so a reader can always see both.

`RunnerConfig.severity_spec_enabled` defaults to False and `bench/tools/run_simulated_experiment.py --severity-spec` turns it on. Also measured on the archive, and worth recording: **25 of 30 disproved findings carry a non-zero severity — 83.3333 per cent, Wilson [66.4356 per cent, 92.6635 per cent]** — so the appendix's key property is currently violated on most of them.

## The other shadow is NOT circular, and promoting it would be the opposite error

The pre-verdict sweep shadow replays the convergence gate against the swept ledger and records whether the verdict would have differed. Unlike the severity shadow it can supply its own evidence: it does not need to decide in order to tell us whether deciding would matter. Its own stated criterion is that if the replayed gate ever disagrees with the recorded verdict then sweep ordering decided a convergence outcome and the reorder is justified by evidence, and if it never disagrees it is not. Its own code calls reordering the sweep ahead of the verdict *"the highest-blast-radius edit available in this file"*.

So it stays a shadow, deliberately, and both free runs produce its reading. Promoting it now would be changing the highest-blast-radius thing in the runner on an argument rather than a measurement, which is what the additive standard forbids. This corrects the orchestrator's own earlier statement that neither of the 2 shadows could be promoted by a flag: that is true of both, but only one of them is circular.

## 3 defects found while fixing these, recorded and not repaired

**The coherence budgets are now stale by construction, and the pruner's ablation arm is inert.** Constraint density divides a count of 20 active constraints, read from the merged policy file, by a token estimate of 8,096, derived from the rendered length, giving 0.002470 on a structural composition for `opus_4_6`. The numerator comes from the policy file and does not grow with the specification; the denominator does. Delivering the specification whole multiplies the rendered length by about 5.7, so density fell below every budget: **0 of 50 real compositions are over budget — 0.0000 per cent, Wilson [0.0000 per cent, 7.1348 per cent], Clopper-Pearson [0.0000 per cent, 7.1122 per cent]** — with a maximum density of 0.002835 against a smallest budget of 0.010, 3.5277 times below it. It was 65.56 per cent of 90 before. The consequence is a named loss: the lean-versus-full comparison that the 2026-07-18 pruning review gates every cut on cannot be run until the budgets are re-derived. They were **not** hand-retuned, because a hand-set cap with no measurement behind it is precisely what the founder objected to. The project derives them from experiment logs and the 2 free runs are where that measurement comes from. The coherence pruner's reachability is still pinned by `bench/tests/test_composer_pruning_ablation_switch_2026-10-13.py`, which supplies a binding budget explicitly and still shows the flag flipping it.

**The concise phenotype style deduplicates the code under review.** On a 1,492-character situation packet holding 20 repeated function definitions, `opus_4_6` receives 1,491 characters and the other 4 models receive 339 — the repeats collapsed to 1 and every line stripped of indentation. **4 of 5 models, 80.0000 per cent, Wilson [37.5535 per cent, 96.3776 per cent]**. The harm is bounded, because the code under review also reaches the model through the user prompt, so a model is not reviewing nothing; the situation packet is supplementary context. But 2 genuinely different functions with similar token sets can be collapsed, and indentation is syntax in Python. It is pre-existing, it is not in the causal path of either free run's hypothesis, and 3 large changes in one night with no measurement between them is how a confound becomes unattributable.

**A proven floor is being used as a ceiling.** The decomposition decision reads `max_successful_prompt_chars` and returns true when the payload exceeds 90 per cent of it. Its own docstring calls that figure a proven floor — *"The model has PROVEN it can handle at least that much input"* — and the code uses it as a ceiling, so a seat that has only ever been sent 72,934 characters can never be sent more and the observed maximum never rises. That is a ratchet that cannot climb, structurally the same defect as the routing ladder's. The futility guard above treats the symptom; changing the rule alters routing for paid seats and is a design decision.

All 3 are in `experimental_notes/STUDY_IN_FLIGHT.json` marked `priority: HIGH`, with the measurements `directive_delivery`, `severity_promotion` and `coherence_budget_recalibration`. They were written there by `scripts/study_high_priority_2026-10-15.py`, which is idempotent and re-runnable rather than a hand edit, so the programme's state and the record of why it changed cannot drift apart. The silently-unmatched domain is measured by `scripts/a_domain_name_that_matches_nothing_2026-10-15.py`.

## What the 2 free runs are and why they differ by 1 flag

Both runs use `bench/cdsfl_registry/targets/prose_with_computable_listings.md`, 10 rounds, domain `statistics`, `--seat-models ladder` and `--routing-max-rungs 0`. Run 1 measures the directive repair alone. Run 2 adds `--severity-spec`. Same target, same seats, same round budget, so run 2 minus run 1 is attributable to the severity promotion. Enabling both at once would make neither attributable, which is the error the programme already rejects for per-fix attribution.

The prose target commissions task A19, which a Python target cannot: `sk_score_prose_listings` is inert on a `.py` file because the gateable-source reader returns Python unchanged, so a prose target carrying fenced computable listings is the only way the admissibility flag is exercised at all.

Two flags are passed rather than defaulted, and neither default is changed, because changing them is the founder's ruling and both are open. `--seat-models` defaults to `uniform`, which answers every seat with 1 model and is cause 2 of 2 in the project record for the inert routing ladder. The stated limitation is that in simulation the ladder maps 6 seats onto 2 backing models, so it discriminates 2 behaviours and not 6. `--routing-max-rungs 0` means exhaust, which is the founder's ruling of 2026-10-08; the dataclass default stays 2 for parity with 49 real configs.

Cost is none. The simulation shim patches the single function where a paid model is called and substitutes subagents on the founder's own plan. Seats carry the mandatory `-SIM` suffix at source. The budget limit is 0.00.

## Replay against the archive is invalidated, knowingly

Delivering the specification whole changes every dispatch, so no archived run can be replayed against the current composer and expected to reproduce. That is the cost finding C0040 was parked for, and the founder has now paid it in terms: *"We cannot go back and change time, so the confounds discovered in the last 24 hours can only be noted and added to the record honestly."*
