## Key Result: Unanimous Agreement on Convergence

All 5 models unanimously agreed that convergence occurred in Exp 31 but was masked by 5 catastrophic instrumentation failures. This is a strong result: five architecturally distinct models, analysing the same data independently, reached the same conclusion.

## PARTIAL CONFOUND ANNOTATION

> **This experiment has a partial confound that must be considered when interpreting results.**

The 5 models were given an anchoring framing: they were asked to "evaluate HIL's claim that convergence occurred" and were provided with efficiency rankings. This framing likely biased them toward optimising for convergence confirmation over scientific rigour.

### Observed Confound Effects

The confound manifested in three specific design recommendations where the models' consensus optimised for confirming convergence rather than detecting truth:

1. **Model count:** Models recommended 3 models. This optimises for convergence speed (fewer disagreements) at the cost of diversity, which is the mechanism that makes CDSFL work.
2. **Round budget:** Models recommended 8-10 rounds. This is half the mathematically predicted 20 rounds needed for reliable convergence detection. A short budget increases the chance of premature convergence being mistaken for genuine convergence.
3. **Gamma disposition:** Models recommended demoting gamma to telemetry-only. Gamma is the metric that would flag premature convergence. Removing it from the gating system removes the experiment's ability to self-correct.

### Interpretation

The unanimous agreement on convergence occurrence (the diagnostic finding) is likely robust despite the confound, because the instrumentation failures are mechanically verifiable bugs with clear root causes. The 5 bugs exist regardless of framing.

The design recommendations for Exp 33, however, are directly compromised by the confound and required founder override.

### Overridden Items

#### 3+1 model configuration --> 5 models retained

**Model recommendation:** Use 3 models plus 1 validator.
**Override:** Retain all 5 models.
**Reasoning:** The 3-model recommendation optimises for convergence speed, not scientific validity. CDSFL's power comes from architectural diversity. Reducing to 3 models reduces the probability of catching errors that only a specific architecture can detect. The confound analysis shows this recommendation is self-serving: fewer models means faster agreement, which the anchored models interpreted as better.

#### 8-10 round budget --> 21 rounds

**Model recommendation:** 8-10 rounds.
**Override:** 21 rounds.
**Reasoning:** The mathematical model predicts approximately 20 rounds for reliable convergence with 5 models. The 8-10 recommendation is exactly what you would expect from models biased toward confirming convergence quickly. A short budget produces premature convergence that looks like genuine convergence but is not falsification-tested. 21 rounds provides the mathematically grounded budget plus 1 round of margin.

#### Gamma as telemetry only --> Scale-dependent gamma

**Model recommendation:** Demote gamma to telemetry (monitoring only, no gating effect).
**Override:** Scale-dependent gamma regime.
**Reasoning:** Gamma measures inter-model disagreement rate. Demoting it removes the experiment's ability to detect premature convergence. This is the most concerning confound effect: the models recommended removing the metric that would catch them agreeing too quickly. The corrected regime:
- **Rounds 1-14:** Gamma as telemetry only (exploratory phase, let models find their footing)
- **Rounds 15-19:** Gamma as soft gate (flags potential premature convergence for review)
- **Rounds 20+:** Gamma as hard gate (convergence cannot be declared if gamma indicates insufficient disagreement was tested)

## Methodological Note

This experiment demonstrates both the value and the risk of multi-model meta-analysis. The value: five models independently identified five real bugs that were mechanically verified and fixed. The risk: when given an anchoring framing, the same models produced design recommendations that would have made the next experiment less rigorous. The confound was caught by the founder applying the same Popperian standard to the models' output that CDSFL applies to any claim. The models were right about what happened; they were wrong about what to do next.
