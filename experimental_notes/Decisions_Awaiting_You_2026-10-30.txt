PART 4. THE OPEN QUESTIONS

HOW OFTEN A HEALTHY MODEL IS WRONGLY DEMOTED, AND YOU WERE RIGHT TO PUSH BACK

Your words: Can we improve on this statistic? A 1 in 20 chance of demoting a model that is performing just fine seems unreasonably high?

The 1 in 20 was never a live setting. It was the common false alarm level that 3 candidate rules were set to so their detection rates could be compared fairly, and nothing in the working code reads it.

Measured over 3000 replications: at 1 in 20 the rule wrongly demotes a healthy model 3.50 per cent of the time and catches a real decline 93.53 per cent of the time. Tightening it to 1 in 50 cuts the wrong demotions to 1.57 per cent, a reduction of 2.23 times, and costs 4.63 points of detection. Both changes are real and neither is within measurement noise.

Recommendation: 1 in 50. Additive, because it is a single number in a rule not yet connected to anything, so no behaviour changes until it is. Composable, because every other threshold in the rule follows arithmetically from it. Simplest sufficient, because the alternative is building an externally anchored difficulty model.

1 caveat that bounds all of it: these figures come from a simulated model of how a model degrades, not from this project's own runs.
