## PART 1. THE QUEUE ALARM WAS RIGHT AND I WAS WRONG, AND THIS IS WHY THE RUNWAY HAS BEEN STUCK

You asked me to explain exactly what the queue alarm does. Answering that question refuted my own recommendation, which had been to soften it from halting the run to merely refusing convergence.

The alarm's own documentation makes the case against me. A pure refusal is described there as the worst available response, because it names no cause, hands over no evidence, and lets the run burn its remaining rounds against a fault no round can fix. It was suppressed twice on 1 August by raising its bound, and it was right both times. The halt and notify form exists precisely so that an alarm which only obstructs does not get switched off. My proposal would have recreated the failure it was built to end.

Then I looked at what it actually caught, and it is the answer to why nothing has run for 12 days.

Every alarm event in the entire archive, 4 of 4, fired at round 0. On experiment 55: no fix evaluation existed for any queued item, 6 of 7 items had no falsifier, and every item was still open at round 0. Findings were entering the irreducible queue at round 0. That flag is set in exactly one place, after the full routing ladder is exhausted, and at round 0 the ladder cannot have been exhausted in any meaningful sense. An item with no fix evaluation was never assessed at all.

So these are not findings no machine could handle. They are findings that were never processed, labelled as findings no machine could process. The alarm has been correctly refusing to let runs proceed on a broken instrument, and softening it would have buried that.

The alarm is unchanged. The root cause of round 0 escalation is now the open question, and it is bigger than a ruling. It is also, I think, the blocker standing between us and the runway.
