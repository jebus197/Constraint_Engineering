# PANEL BRIEF — What severity IS, and whether it should gate convergence

**The founder ordered this review added to the outstanding set on 2026-10-30,
and the question is his.** It is NOT a request to confirm a position of this
project's. The previous report told him that no recommendation could be offered
because "whether severity is a confidence inside a range is a question about
what severity means, and no archive can answer it". He rejected that as an
answer and supplied the definition himself.

## HIS QUESTION AND HIS DEFINITION, VERBATIM

    This goes back to a fundamental question of what severity should be
    and how it should be calculated. You make it sound as though there is
    no reliable way it can be calculated? But sever5ity is a fundamental
    property of our maths model. Without it, our maths model makes
    considerably less sense. What I asked about severity ‘being within a
    range’ was probably poorly expressed on my part. But given what
    severity is, which as I understand it is what are the chances that a
    particular flaw, or class of flaws might remain after a given number
    of passes under severe scrutiny in any given test, this seems to me
    like a fundamental measure of uncertainty and/or of confidence that
    can be assigned to any given outcome in any STEM focused experiment?
    But this also seems like a good candidate for a panel review. So you
    should add it to the panel reviews that remain outstanding. Should
    severity be a purely recorded statistic, or should it feed into the
    convergence mechanics of the schema, so that convergence only triggers
    when there is high confidence in any outcome and uncertainty (and
    therefore severity) is low and what should realistic boundaries for
    these be? Clearly just stating that severity should be set at 0.7 in
    all circumstances may ultimately tell us very little about the
    validity, or applicability of a result one way or another. For example
    (and the example I gave previously in another context) if you were to
    ask all the models on the harness to calculate the sum of 4 + 4 and
    call this an experiment, the severity/residual risk of the final
    calculation being wrong would be likely to be very low. But if the
    models on the harness were asked to resolve the Riemann hypothesis,
    the residual risk/severity that the resulting outcome (after a given
    number of passes through the harness) would remain uncertain, or could
    not fully be resolved could potentially be significantly higher.
    Therefore ‘severity’ by this definition, is unlikely to be a fixed
    number, and would depend on the complexity of the topic under
    consideration, and the confidence once can derive in the accuracy of a
    given answer. Hence ‘it might exist within a range’, rather than as a
    fixed number. That much I thought should be obvious?

("sever5ity" is "severity".)

## WHAT HIS WORDS ESTABLISH BEFORE THE PANEL STARTS

**Do not re-argue these. They are his, and they are the premises.**

  1. **Severity is a fundamental property of the mathematical model**, not a
     reporting convenience. Without it the model makes considerably less sense.
     The standing directive on gamma applies by analogy: treat the model as the
     fixed point and hunt the mechanics first.
  2. **His definition:** the chance that a particular flaw, or class of flaws,
     remains after a given number of passes under severe scrutiny in a given
     test. That is a residual-risk quantity, so it is a measure of uncertainty
     and therefore of confidence in an outcome.
  3. **It is therefore NOT a fixed number.** It depends on the difficulty of the
     question and on the confidence derivable in the answer. His worked
     contrast: ask every seat to compute 4 + 4 and the residual risk that the
     result is wrong is very low; ask them to resolve the Riemann hypothesis
     and the residual risk that the outcome stays uncertain, or cannot be
     resolved at all, is potentially far higher.
  4. **So "set severity at 0.7 in all circumstances" may tell us very little**
     about the validity or applicability of a result. That is his charge against
     the current convention, and the panel is asked to answer it rather than
     defend it.
  5. **His earlier phrase "being within a range" is clarified by him**: he means
     severity varies with the problem, not that a single finding carries an
     interval. The previous report treated the phrase as the former and he has
     said that reading was his own wording being poorly expressed, so the
     clarified reading is the one to work from.

## THE 2 QUESTIONS HE ASKS, AND A THIRD THEY IMPLY

**Q1. Should severity be a purely recorded statistic, or should it feed the
convergence mechanics?** His proposal is explicit: convergence triggers only
when confidence in the outcome is high and uncertainty — and therefore
severity — is low. Answer with reference to what the two-sided gate does today:
`gamma_critical >= gamma_alt_threshold` (default 0.30) AND K consecutive
zero-new-critical rounds, in `_check_gamma_alt_convergence`
(`bench/reference_runner_v3.py`). Severity enters that gate today only through
what counts as CRITICAL, by way of the 0.7 cut. State precisely where a
severity term would enter, and whether it is a third condition, a modifier on
an existing one, or a replacement for the CRITICAL count.

**★ HARD CONSTRAINT ON ANY ANSWER TO Q1: GAMMA IS LOAD-BEARING AND MAY NOT BE
DEMOTED.** The decay curve and diminishing returns are the foundation of the
model. An answer that makes gamma reported-only, or that replaces the two-sided
gate with a severity gate, is refused by standing directive. A severity
condition ADDED to the gate is in scope; a severity condition SUBSTITUTED for
part of it is not.

**Q2. What are realistic boundaries?** If severity varies with difficulty, then
0.7 as a universal CRITICAL cut is at best a default. Say what the bounds
should be and what sets them. Name the observable: his definition is a
probability of a flaw surviving N passes under scrutiny, so a bound must be
expressible in those terms and estimable from a run rather than chosen.

**Q3, implied by Q1 and Q2 together and the one most likely to be skipped.**
If severity varies with the difficulty of the question, then severity values
from different experiments are not on a common scale, and a fixed cut applied
across them compares unlike quantities. Say whether severity needs
normalisation against question difficulty before it can gate anything, and if
so what difficulty measure is available in this harness. An answer that sets
boundaries without addressing comparability has answered Q2 for one experiment
only.

## REQUIRED SECTION — Use the harness, and the evidence that already exists

Form your answer by running things. 3 measurements already exist and bear
directly on this; verify them rather than trusting this brief.

**1. The spread between independent judgements of the same defect.** Measured
over 147 archived cases: 0.12 at the midpoint, 0.18 on average. This is the
quantity he first called the wobble. Reproduce it and say whether it is
consistent with his definition — a residual-risk estimate SHOULD vary between
judges if the question is hard, so a spread is not automatically a defect.

**2. Nothing combines those independent judgements.** The merge keeps whichever
record happened to be the target and discards the rest, so the kept figure
falls outside the range of the judgements it came from in 35 of 147 cases,
between 18 and 31 per cent. Establish this by execution. It bears on Q1: a
severity that gates convergence cannot be whichever of several estimates
happened to survive a merge.

**3. The 0.7 cut's provenance and its untested proxy.** 0.7 is pre-registered
in `bench/exp40_baseline/CRITICAL_DEFINITION_PREREG_2026-05-18.md` as the cut
above which a finding counts as critical. A separate measurement
(`scripts/the_severity_proxy_was_never_tested_2026-10-28.py`) establishes that
the 2026-09-02 blind audit is UNINFORMATIVE about whether severity predicts the
CRITICAL judgement — kappa = -0.022688, Fisher p = 0.778818, but over a
severity window only 0.09 wide, with power 0.144250 against a strong
relationship. **Do not cite that pair as evidence against the proxy.** 2,807 of
3,237 archive entries were classified at a severity the audit never examined,
and 1,119 of the 1,408 CRITICAL calls ever made sit ABOVE the band. Say what
measurement WOULD settle it, and whether your answer to Q1 depends on it.

**4. Run the instruments.** `effective_severity` and
`spec_verification_factor` are where severity is consumed. Report what they
return, and what a severity expressed as a residual-risk distribution rather
than a scalar would break.

## REQUIRED SECTION — Produce a fix, and test it

A finding without a fix is incomplete, and a fix without an executed falsifier
is a hypothesis. Write the fix into the sandbox tree at its real path and
report the command and output. If your answer is that severity should stay
recorded-only, that is a legitimate verdict and still needs a fix: the fix is
then whatever makes the recorded figure trustworthy — a combination rule for
independent judgements, at minimum.

## REQUIRED SECTION — What would refute you

State it before concluding. At least these:

  1. **A severity term in the gate is inert.** If adding it changes 0 archived
     outcomes, it is an addition nothing reaches and fails the additive
     standard. Test it against the archive.
  2. **Difficulty is not measurable here.** If no available quantity tracks the
     difficulty of a question, then his 4 + 4 against the Riemann hypothesis
     distinction cannot be computed, and a difficulty-normalised severity is
     not implementable today. Say so plainly if so, and say what would be
     needed.
  3. **The spread is measurement error, not uncertainty.** If the 0.18 average
     spread is judges disagreeing about the same thing rather than genuine
     residual risk, then it argues for a combination rule and against treating
     severity as confidence. Distinguish these by execution if you can.

## REQUIRED SECTION — Output shape

- `verdict` — one of: RECORDED ONLY / GATES CONVERGENCE (added condition) /
  GATES CONVERGENCE (modifier on an existing condition) / NOT IMPLEMENTABLE YET
- `definition` — severity as you would implement it, in his terms
- `boundaries` — the bounds, and what sets them
- `comparability` — your answer to Q3
- `instrument_results` — for each of the 4 items above: command, output, and
  whether it matched this brief's statement
- `fix` — the sandbox path written and what it changes
- `falsifier` — the falsifier written, the command run, its output
- `refutation_condition` — what would overturn your answer
- `strongest_disagreement` — your strongest disagreement with this brief,
  stated even if your verdict agrees with it

## REQUIRED SECTION — Termination

Stop when a further pass produces no new above-threshold findings. Report how
many passes you ran and what the last one added.

There is no compelled convergence in this round. Disagreement with the other
seat, and with this brief, is preserved as information rather than smoothed to
consensus.
