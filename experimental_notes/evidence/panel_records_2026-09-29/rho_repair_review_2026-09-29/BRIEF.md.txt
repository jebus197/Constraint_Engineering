### The 5 open questions, stated neutrally

Each is a DECISION the implementing session declined to take alone. None has a preferred answer in this brief.

1. **Should the CRITICAL novelty series be corroboration-aware?** `novel_critical_history` is the COUNT side of the two-sided convergence gate and currently reads `_settled_novelty_series`. The corroborated critical novelty total is computed and logged as a shadow line that gates nothing. Promoting it would change a convergence trigger. Decide whether it should be promoted, and on what evidence.

2. **Should `rho`'s history be retroactively resettled?** Only `novelty_counts[-1]` is overwritten each round, so earlier rounds keep the value known at the time. In the dead run's archive, round 1 holds 7 where the final registry yields 6. The runner's own comment records this class of defect for `novel_critical_history` and says the whole-series correction was applied there on 2026-08-19, leaving this copy. Decide whether a round's `rho` is a statement about what was known then, or about what is known now.

3. **The 2 repairs the founder did not choose, and whether they compose.** (a) Lower the routing dedup threshold from 0.85 so real duplicates become merge CANDIDATES, keeping the tool-verdict gate: measured precision at the threshold that catches all 4 known duplicates is 13.79%. (b) Let a falsifier that fires on both findings' locations BE the tool verdict that authorises a merge. Assess both, including whether either is now redundant given the landed repair.

4. **Is keying simulation on `-SIM` seat labels in dict KEYS the right general invariant?** The landed repair recognises a simulated run from its own `novelty_counts_per_model` keys. Measured over 103 keyed archive documents: 30 caught by both the per-file and per-directory rules, 0 by either alone, 73 by neither. Ask whether a better invariant exists, and whether the per-directory rule should be retained, given that its redundancy is a property of this corpus rather than of the rule.
