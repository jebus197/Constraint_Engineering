## Established, verified, not up for re-litigation

**The threshold.** `CRITICAL_SEVERITY_THRESHOLD = 0.7` was pre-registered
2026-05-18 as the operational proxy for a five-clause **consequence** rubric
("the rubric governs, the number encodes it"). The pre-registration exists
because 0.7 was previously "an undocumented code convention... the single largest
cooking vector". Its "both unanimous on this point" refers to *that* — not to the
number. The 2026-05-17 confer **split** on the number: one reviewer called it
"arbitrary... a fragile heuristic proxy", another "sound, not arbitrary".

**The distribution** (6,865 real archived findings):
- **401 (5.84%**, Wilson [5.31%, 6.42%]**) sit exactly on 0.70**
- **72.1%** [71.0%, 73.1%] are quantised to a 0.05 step → severity is **ordinal**, ~20 rungs
- No natural break at 0.70: scipy KDE minima 0.12/0.37/0.62; numpy histogram valleys 0.67 and 0.71
- Moving 0.70→0.72 reclassifies **456 findings, 6.6%** of the archive

**The calibration gap.** On code **byte-identical** between the pre-repair
revision and HEAD (AST-verified), the real panel rated defects **0.72 / 0.80**;
the simulated panel re-found them, executed working reproductions, and rated them
**0.45 / 0.45 / 0.45 / 0.40**. Offset **−0.132** severity points (Mann-Whitney
p=0.0006, Welch p=0.0012). One sim reviewer reproduced a July finding *exactly*
and rated it 0.45 against 0.72 — "found the defect and proved it, priced it wrong".

**The amplification.** A ~0.12 mean shift converts **mechanically** into a ~7.8×
critical-count ratio, because the boundary sits on that 5.8% spike.

**The target confound.** Target grew 317→489 lines in one commit (`043a0a8`,
2026-08-15). **4 of 6** exp45 defect themes are fixed = **9 of 12** critical rows.
Split of the 12→2 critical drop: **~75% target repair, ~25% under-rating, 0% dedup**.

**Evidence provenance.** **88.4%** [80.4%, 93.4%] of scored discrimination-control
records come from **simulated** runs. The mechanism carrying "tools decide, not
votes" is presently evidenced overwhelmingly by simulation.

**A power calculation I ran for you.** Offset 0.132, pooled sd 0.218 → Cohen's
d = 0.606 → ~44 per arm for 80% power (statsmodels 43.8, scipy 42.8). **But the
unit is a MODEL, not a finding**: 5–6 clusters per arm gives effective n ≈ 5–6.
**A single paired run cannot calibrate this.**

## The four questions. Answer these, concretely.

**Q1. What do we actually DO — in priority order, with cost?** Not a list of
considerations. An ordered plan: what first, what it costs in runs and wall-clock,
what it buys, what we stop doing. Include "do nothing / accept the limit" as a
serious candidate and say why it loses if it does.

**Q2. Do we need another target, and should it be clean or NOT clean?** The
founder's phrasing is "(not) clean" — deliberately. Argue the case. If a
*deliberately dirty* target is right, what makes a good one, and how do we avoid

exactly must be excluded from the sandbox to keep the blinding honest?

**Q3. Would it make a difference to ANYTHING?** This is the question I am most
likely to hand-wave. Given the power calculation above — effective n ≈ 5–6 model
clusters against a required ~44 — **is a calibrated offset actually reachable in
any realistic number of runs?** If not, say so plainly, and say what that implies:
does the simulated panel get permanently demoted to machinery-validation only? Is
that acceptable, or fatal to its purpose?

**Q4. What does this mean for Bench Run 2?** Does anything here block it, change
its design, or change what it can claim? Be specific about the go/no-go.
