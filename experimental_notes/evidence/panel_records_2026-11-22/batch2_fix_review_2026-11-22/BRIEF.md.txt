## THE 3 STANDARDS YOU ARE JUDGING AGAINST

1. **ADDITIVE** — work adds to reliability, functionality, accuracy and
   robustness. Nothing is removed unless a COMMITTED MEASUREMENT shows the
   replacement dominates on a named property. **And symmetrically: an addition
   nothing reaches is not additive either.** Every new flag, gate or entry point
   must have a caller and be executed by a test.
2. **SIMPLEST SUFFICIENT** — the smallest change addressing the root cause and
   the consequences traced downstream of it.
3. **COMPOSABILITY** — the founder's own words, 2026-10-02: *"Composability
   doesn't mean composing two solutions just because they can be composed. It
   means this principle should be applied where two composed solutions
   demonstrably provide a better, more robust and/or more efficient solution than
   either fix in isolation. Where a single fix out performs a composed one, that
   fix should continue to be preferred."* **So a composability claim REQUIRES A
   MEASUREMENT showing the composed fix dominates either part alone on a named
   property.** Saying two repairs do not conflict is NOT the standard and will be
   refused. If you cannot measure dominance, prefer the single fix and say so.

## Q5. I CLOSED A HOLE NOTHING REACHES. IS THAT ADDITIVE, OR IS IT THE SHAPE THE
##     STANDARD WARNS ABOUT?

**FILE:** `bench/tools/run_simulated_experiment.py`, `install_paid_tripwire`.

**WHAT THE MECHANISM IS FOR.** It is a billing tripwire: it patches name
resolution so that any attempt to reach a PAID model host during a simulated run
raises rather than spending the founder's money.

**THE HOLE.** Installing it twice and restoring once uninstalled the guard while
the outer frame was still running. The nested install returned the ORIGINAL
resolver, so an inner frame's `finally` handed back the real function and the
billing guard was gone for the remainder of the outer run.

**MY FIX.** A nested install now returns the PATCH. An inner frame's restore is
therefore a no-op while the outermost still genuinely uninstalls — the returned
value IS the token for whichever frame holds it, and no depth counter is needed.
I replaced the single assertion that PINNED the hazard (`assert second is
original`) with 3 tests, one of which drives 2 nested frames and asserts a paid
host is still refused after the inner restore. 21 tests pass; 2 of 2 mutations
go red; the subject restores byte-for-byte.

**HERE IS THE PROBLEM WITH MY OWN FIX, AND IT IS WHY I AM ASKING.** The hole is
**LATENT, NOT LIVE.** Two routes agreed the protocol admits it — 43 witnesses,
shortest being install, install, restore, dispatch; solver satisfiable — while
the call graph does not reach it, because the actuator drives the wrapper as a
separate process. So I have spent work on a path no caller takes, and the
additive standard's symmetric half says **an addition nothing reaches is not
additive.** The record holds 11 confirmed defects of exactly that shape.

**YOUR QUESTION, and please do not simply agree with me.** Is this fix justified,
or is it the 12th such addition? The case FOR is that the cost of being wrong is
the founder's money on a run he explicitly refused to pay for, which is not
symmetric with an ordinary latent bug. The case AGAINST is that I cannot name a
caller, and "it would be expensive if it happened" is the argument every unwired
addition makes for itself.

**If you judge it unjustified, say what should happen to it** — reverted, kept
with the hazard re-pinned, or kept and WIRED so a caller exists. **If you judge
it justified, name the property that distinguishes it from the 11**, because
without that distinction the standard has no teeth and the next unwired addition
will cite this one as precedent.

**Do NOT propose composing my fix with the narrow one unless you can MEASURE the
composed form dominating either alone on a named property.** Where the single fix
