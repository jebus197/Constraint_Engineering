## 2. WHAT THE DISTINCTION IS, AND THE STATE OF THE EVIDENCE

**Two properties share the word "simple".** *Cost simplicity* is a small artefact — few
lines, low blast radius. *Compressive simplicity* is a short statement with wide reach,
small because it captured the structure rather than because something was dropped. The
founder's position is that the second is what the project should aim for, and that in
that regime simplicity and sufficiency do not compete.

**The proposed criterion.** A simplification is admissible iff the simpler form S and
the fuller form F agree across a scope D declared **before either was proposed**
(residual empty), or the residual is named, bounded, and filed as its own claim with its
own test. Establishable by proof or exhaustive enumeration. **Sampling can refute a
simplification but can never admit one.**

**What is already verified, and you can re-run all of it.**
`bench/tests/test_appendix_reduction_properties_2026-09-05.py` executes 30 tests over
the appendix's own reduction claims. Every test asserts both that the residual is zero
under the stated condition AND that it is non-zero for a wrong model, so none is
vacuously true. The unified equation's own collapse — recursion reproduces the batch
posterior with m = prod(1 - q_j) — holds and fails for a wrong update.

**Two defects were found in the appendix while doing this. Both are pinned as tests
rather than patched, because amending the mathematics is the founder's decision:**
- L38 claims a coupling bound is required "to ensure all state probabilities remain
  non-negative. (Verified by SymPy, March 2026.)" **This is false.** Every state weight
  is a product of non-negative factors times an exponential, and exp(x) > 0 for all real
  x. Verified at 1000, three orders past the bound; a second reviewer reproduced it at
  36 times the bound independently.
- The G_n reduction table row "K=1, d=1, uniform p -> C(n)" omits its necessary
  condition n_H = 0.

**A prior panel's position, which you may disagree with.** Two reviewers on 2026-09-04
held that a reduction property is a theorem ABOUT the equation, not a term IN it, and
that folding it in would make the equation piecewise — the opposite of collapse. One
went further and proposed a rule rather than a definition: **reductions must be
discharged, not sampled.** Where a symbolic or exhaustive proof is available it must be
attempted first, and a sampled agreement may be recorded as REFUTED or UNDISCHARGED,
never CONFIRMED. Neither is a vote; both are tool-decided.

## 3. THE QUESTIONS

**Q1. Does the existing model already encode the distinction?** Read
`docs/MATHEMATICAL_APPENDIX.md` sections 1.1 and 7.1 and the reduction statements
throughout. The claim to test is that the appendix's re-injection term nu and its
break-even nu* already express the trade, with sufficiency as the benefit side and
simplicity as the cost side. **Derive your answer.** If the encoding is real but
incomplete, say exactly what is missing.

**Q2. Is new mathematics warranted, and would it survive the founder's constraint?**
If you propose a new term, an operator or a metric, you must show: what it buys that
the existing form does not; that it does not make the equation piecewise; and that the
result remains comprehensible to a competent reader who is not its author. **A proposal
that fails the comprehensibility test is a refutation of itself.** "No new mathematics"
is a permitted and possibly correct answer.

**Q3. Does the model meet its own standard?** The criterion says a simplification is
admissible when the residual is empty over a declared scope, or the residual is named
and filed. **Apply that to the mathematical model itself.** Its central collapse is
verified. Are there simplifications inside the model whose residual is neither empty nor
named? Name them.

**Q4. The rule, not the definition.** Should "reductions must be discharged, not
sampled" be adopted as an asymmetry in the verdict vocabulary — sampled agreement may
record REFUTED or UNDISCHARGED, never CONFIRMED? State the cost as well as the benefit.
What does it forbid that is currently permitted and useful?

**Q5. The two appendix defects.** Independently of the above: is the L38 refutation
correct? If the coupling bound is not needed for non-negativity, **what is it for?** A
constraint with a false justification may still have a true purpose, and naming it is
more useful than deleting it. Same question for the G_n table row.

**Q6. What did this brief get wrong?** Given the error rate disclosed in section 0, the
most valuable thing you can return may be a defect in the framing rather than an answer
to the question as posed.
