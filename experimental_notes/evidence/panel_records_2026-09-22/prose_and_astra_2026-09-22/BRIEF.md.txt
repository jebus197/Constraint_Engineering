# PANEL BRIEF — the founder's prose fix, and Astra's 3 unanswered questions

## REQUIRED SECTION 1 — The question

Two questions, both narrow.

**Q1. Is the founder's prose design right, and what is the simplest sufficient way to build it?**

His words, recorded 2026-09-10: *"Having the admissibility switch off for prose targets is also questionable, as clearly it should simply mark a purely prose input as 'inadmissible', while still solving any computationally reducible elements within that prose! This is STEM. No one will be using CDSFL to write poetry!"*

The record already accepted the principle and refined one word: `reference_runner_v3.py:1809` predates his point and says NO_SCORE is *"NOT a third grade of admissibility — it is the statement that S_k has no opinion"*, so a correct prose fix is **unscoreable, not defective**. The record then named the buildable form as **per-hunk classification**: score the reducible hunks, NO_SCORE the rest.

**What was actually built is coarser.** `_gateable_source` extracts every fenced listing and CONCATENATES them into one blob, gated together. Demonstrated: a document with `def good(): return 1` and `def bad(: return 2` yields one 41-char blob, so a syntax error in the second condemns the first. Per-hunk was named in September and never built.

Is per-hunk classification the right target? Is there a simpler sufficient fix? What breaks if `A` is computed per-hunk rather than per-file — `compute_rk` consumes a single `sk`, so what does a hunk-wise `A` mean downstream?
