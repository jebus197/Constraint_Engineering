## The founder's proposal

He has been turning over an idea first raised for the specialist-cell exams — testing
whether a cell computes a real answer or games the exam — and asks whether it
generalises:

> "Couldn't we use this more widely, given the machinery we already have, as a means of
> dupe/sameness/novelty etc. detection, both so that we can detect this, and be able to
> say for sure when models truly are computing any given outcome, or when they might
> simply be being 'performative' or are gaming the CDSFL schema as a whole? We ideally
> might be able to use this as a means of detecting genuine cognition and novelty?"

He points at an interview describing the same paradigm independently. Verbatim from the
transcript:

> "one of the big problems with the AI evaluations is that the systems can kind of cheat
> them… they can memorize your data… one of the keys to my approach is something that's
> called the perturbation paradigm in which we perturb, meaning we disrupt the data. So
> for example, if I ask you a simple word problem and you learn a particular answer, but
> then on the test I sneakily change two words in the question and it flips the correct
> answer. And if you've memorized the questions then you're going to get it wrong. But
> if you've learned the reasoning… you're going to get it right… it's a more rigorous
> way of testing for actual thinking than almost any of the AI labs are doing right now.
> Almost all of the evaluations, almost all of the benchmarks, they just test for how
> did they do on the test? They don't test for what happens when you flip the question."

## What already exists here, measured today

**The discrimination control is BUILT AND HAS NEVER RUN.**
`reference_runner_v2.py:2117-2165` implements exactly this paradigm for falsifiers: run
the same falsifier against a CORRECTED copy of the target, in which the claim under test
has been fixed. A sound falsifier must go quiet. If it still fires, it was never testing
that claim.

It is careful work. It refuses to synthesise the corrected copy from the model's proposed
fix (a bad indent makes the falsifier crash, the crash reads as "still fires", and a
genuine defect is silently un-confirmed — two reviews killed that route on 2026-08-04).
It distinguishes "fires on both" from "crashed". It proves its own apparatus first with a
tripwire probe, a determinism probe and a baseline check, each failing to a distinct
INDETERMINATE outcome rather than a verdict.

MEASURED 2026-08-21: `discrimination_control_ask` and `discrimination_control_blocks` are
**unset in all 14 forward configs**, and **zero archived reports mention it**. It has
never executed.

A known defeat is recorded at `:2972`: the access-versus-dependence hole — a falsifier can
satisfy a file-access check by opening the target and discarding the contents, and it
**fails GREEN**, reporting full coverage while discriminating nothing.

**The method currently relied on for sameness is counterfactual repair** — apply one
finding's fix, re-run both falsifiers, require both directions to agree. Measured over
133 archived pairs, 252 directional attempts:

    strict bidirectional verdict          33 of 133   (24.8%)
    no applicable fix existed to apply    29.4% of attempts
    the fix failed to cure its OWN falsifier  17.5% of attempts
    cost                                  0.287 s/pair, no metered charge

So roughly 47% of attempts fail because the PROPOSED FIX is missing or ineffective —
a dependency perturbation would not have.

## The questions

**Q1. DOES IT GENERALISE TO SAMENESS?** Perturb the target at finding A's location; does
finding B's falsifier change behaviour? If both respond to the same perturbation they
depend on the same thing. Is that a sound identity test? It needs no proposed fix, which
is the measured failure mode of the incumbent method. Name what it would get WRONG —
specifically, two genuinely distinct defects that a single perturbation would move
together, since that is the exact objection three panellists raised against counterfactual
repair ("the project has replaced a text-similarity problem with a patch-equivalence
problem").

**Q2. DOES IT DETECT GAMING, AND WHAT IS ITS OWN DEFEAT?** The founder's larger question is
whether this distinguishes genuine computation from performance. Given the recorded
access-versus-dependence hole, what is the analogous defeat for a generalised
perturbation control, and can it be closed or only detected? Be concrete about what a
model optimising against this test would do.

**Q3. NOVELTY.** Can perturbation measure novelty rather than sameness — is a finding that
responds to a perturbation nothing else responds to, by that fact, novel? Or is this a
category error?

**Q4. COST AND SCOPE.** Perturbing a target and re-running falsifiers costs local compute
and no metered charge. At what scale does it stop being free? Should it run per-pair, per
round, or only at convergence? Note it cannot run on a target the runner may not modify.

**Q5. SHOULD THE EXISTING CONTROL BE SWITCHED ON FOR exp46's RE-RUN?** It has never
executed. `_ask` and `_blocks` are separate flags. What is the correct setting for a run
whose purpose is validating repairs, and what would you predict it reports?
