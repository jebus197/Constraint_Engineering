## What the founder is asking for. These are his words and they are the brief.

> "We just need a definitive status for each issue, how they are marked as
> confirmed/unconfirmed, merged, dupes, resolved, or whatever, and to do this in a way
> that Bugzilla doesn't simply become a 'ledger' of what we did (although that is
> clearly also necessary), but also a way of gathering and assessing fixes and how they

> to a singular overall solution, whatever that solution might be?"

## The five questions

**Q1. THE STATUS SET.** Give the definitive list of statuses a finding can hold, and for
each: what it means, what transitions into it, what transitions out, and — the part
that matters most — **what evidence is required, and whether a model may assert it or
only a tool may establish it.** Say explicitly where a human reviewer is the only
possible arbiter. Note that `OPEN → CONFIRMED` currently fires on counting two
independent model verifications, which is a vote by the founder's definition; say
whether that should change, and distinguish voting on IDENTITY ("these are the same")
from voting on EXISTENCE ("I also observe this") if you think the distinction holds.

**Q2. LEDGER VERSUS INSTRUMENT.** The founder's core objection is that this must not be
merely a record of what happened. How does a finding's status, and the accumulated
duplicates and extensions attached to it, actually **improve the next answer**? Be
concrete. If your answer is that duplicate counts inform priority, say what consumes
that signal and when.

**Q3. SOFTWARE AND STEM IN ONE SCHEMA.** Every prior panel broke the Bugzilla analogy at
the same joint: a software bug is a defect in an artefact the maintainer controls; a
STEM claim can be wrong about the world. Does one status set serve both, or do you need
a discriminator? Consider specifically the state nobody has: **the finding was right and
the target was wrong.**

**Q4. BUILD SO THE INTERFACE IS TRIVIAL LATER.** The discussion-board layer is deferred
by founder ruling — expensive, and unnecessary to prove the theory. But work done now
should make it trivial to add. **What must be true of the data model today** so that a
future interface is a read-only view rather than an archaeology exercise? CC1's answer
is that every status change must carry its reason, its adjudicator and its timestamp at
the moment it happens, since the registry stores status and round but not why or who.
Attack or extend that.

**Q5. HUMAN AND MACHINE COMPREHENSIBLE, SIMULTANEOUSLY.** A requirement stated repeatedly
and never addressed. The same record must be readable by a researcher and consumable by
the runner. What does that constrain? Is there a real tension, or is it satisfied by
keeping a typed payload beside a natural-language rendering?

## How to answer

**Commit to a recommendation.** The founder's standing objection: "a system that can only
find more problems than it fixes is of limited value to anyone." Name what to build
first, what to defer, and what to drop.

Weight by cost. Work validated by replaying the existing archive is effectively free; a
live experiment run is not; a UI build is expensive and deferred.

State your strongest falsification of your own answer. If you think a question is
malformed, say so and why. Cite file and line for anything you check.

Do not pad. Every word is read, so make every word carry weight.
