# Is the instrument at proof-of-concept standard? Argue BOTH sides, then decide.

You are in a disposable COPY of the repository. Read and run freely; do not delete
things to test that.

This is a STRATEGIC judgement, not a code review. Do not hunt for defects — the
question is explicitly about what to do GIVEN that defects certainly remain.

## THE SITUATION, stated without spin

The founder runs this project alone on a Mac Mini M1, months behind his hoped
schedule. Over the last 3 days a large number of instrument defects were found and
fixed. A new enforcement layer now flags, today, that 22 of 89 runner config
fields are named in no test — Wilson [16.9%, 34.6%]. It is near-certain that a
systematic sweep would surface not tens but hundreds of comparable items.

His question: **given that, does the instrument — once the remaining build
experiments on the runway are done — do what it says on the tin to a standard
sufficient for a formal proof-of-concept release?** And is the simplest sufficient
path to ship it and let others with far greater compute find the refinements,
rather than continuing to polish it alone?

He notes this cuts against the project's ouroboros framing, in which the
instrument has always been used to improve the instrument.

## EVIDENCE YOU SHOULD CHECK RATHER THAN TAKE FROM ME

* 25 archived runs record a convergence. Verify what they actually demonstrate,
  and whether any depends on machinery since found defective. The S_k
  admissibility gate, for instance, never rejected anything: 0 of 3816.
* 13 committed `verify_`/`measure_` scripts exist. How many headline claims in
  `PAPER.md`, `README.md` and `docs/MATHEMATICAL_APPENDIX.md` travel with a
  reproducing script, and how many do not?
* Of 60 commits since 2026-09-05, by subject line only 2 touched a reported
  result; the rest touched the instrument's own checks. Test that claim properly.
* `experimental_notes/CDSFL_Agent_Operational_Plan.md` holds the runway.

## THE THREE QUESTIONS

**1. Do the demonstrated CLAIMS survive the known defects?** A proof of concept
does not need a defect-free instrument; it needs its claims to hold. Separate
"the instrument has faults" from "the reported results are unsound". Name any
result you find that does NOT survive.

**2. What is the MINIMUM remaining work before a PoC release is honest?** Not the
ideal list — the minimum. For each item say what claim fails without it. If the
answer is "nothing further is required", say that plainly.

**3. Is "ship it and let others polish" sound or self-serving?** Argue the
strongest case AGAINST it: what breaks if a stranger runs this on better hardware
and it does not reproduce? What would they hit first? Then argue the case FOR.
Then give your own verdict and say which way you would move if you were wrong.

## HOW TO ANSWER

Take a position. Do not hedge into "it depends". If you think the honest answer is
that it is not ready, say so and name the smallest thing that would make it ready.
If you think it is ready, name the one claim most likely to embarrass the founder
in public and say why it holds anyway.

Cite what you executed. Budget 25 minutes. Disagreement between seats is
information here and will not be smoothed away.
