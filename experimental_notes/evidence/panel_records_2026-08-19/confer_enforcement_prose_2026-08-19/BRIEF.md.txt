# MEASUREMENT 1 — THE ENFORCEMENT GAP

The operational directive (`cdsfl_operational.md:76-79`) states:

  "CORROBORATION: After falsifying your own claim, compute your residual risk using the
   self-assessment equation below. You MUST show your working: state R_old, your
   estimates for eta, d, p, S_k, nu_b, nu_f, and the resulting R_k."

The runner recomputes R_k from the model's own stated parameters and compares
(`_validate_rk_computation`, reference_runner_v2.py:7306). Its docstring, verbatim:

  "Advisory only - logs discrepancies, never rejects findings."

MEASURED today, over 385 corroboration blocks in the RAW round responses of exp44-49:

    PASS (recomputes within 0.01)          90    23.4%
    WARN (within 0.05)                     12     3.1%
    FAIL (recomputed and WRONG, >0.05)    138    35.8%
    SKIP (too few parameters to check)    145    37.7%

So 35.8% of the time a model states a residual risk that does not follow from its own
stated numbers, and 37.7% of the time it does not show enough working to check. Nothing
is rejected. Ever.

The founder's position: "CDSFL was designed from the outset to be tough to game. The
models must formulate conclusions through the mathematical model AND tools, and
demonstrate their workings, otherwise their solutions should be rejected and fed back
to them or up the ladder to a more capable model. If elements are being ignored, that
is a mechanical failing on our side, not the models'."

**Q1. Is that diagnosis right, and what is the SIMPLEST SUFFICIENT enforcement?**
Options include: reject the finding outright on FAIL; accept but zero its contribution;
return it to the source model once for correction; escalate up the routing ladder; or
something else. Consider the failure direction of each. Consider that at 35.8% FAIL and
37.7% SKIP, hard rejection would discard roughly three quarters of all findings on the
first round it is switched on. Say explicitly what you would do about that.

# MEASUREMENT 2 — THE PROSE-TARGET WALL (the founder's explicit question)

The three-phase model is:

    Phase 1 detection:    R_det  = R_old * (1-q) / (1 - q*R_old),  q = eta*d*p
    Phase 2 resolution:   R_base = sigma*R_det + (1-sigma)*R_old
    Phase 3 re-injection: R_k    = R_base*(1-nu) + nu

sigma = fix efficacy (does the fix resolve the flaw). nu = re-injection rate. The
appendix defines nu as: "Localised one-line changes have low nu. Changes to shared
interfaces have higher nu." That is a COMPLEXITY measure.

VERIFIED WITH SYMPY TODAY. The break-even condition in the appendix,
nu* = sigma*R*q / (1 - q*R*(1-sigma)), solves dR = 0 exactly; d(nu*)/d(sigma) > 0;
sigma=0 gives nu*=0 and sigma=1 gives nu*=q*R. So sufficiency already earns complexity
budget, and a fix that resolves nothing earns none.

MEASURED: the CODE computes nu as
    nu_eff = 1 - (1-nu_b)*(1 - (1-sk)*nu_f),  nu_b=0.05, nu_f=0.20
Its only free variables are sigma and two CONSTANTS. It depends on nothing about the
fix: not its size, not how many places it touches, not whether it moves a shared
interface. The mathematics for "simplest sufficient solution" is already in the model;
its input is a constant.

THE WALL. On a PROSE target (a paper, not code) there is no AST, no test suite, and no
compiler. `apply_sk_to_rk` already returns NO_SCORE for sigma on prose. The code comment
at :7202-7215 records what happens when that is mishandled: with sk=0 the re-injection
term is at its MAXIMUM, so risk is pushed UP by 0.12 per finding as a penalty for a fix
nobody assessed, and on a prose target nothing can ever move it back down.

nu will hit the same wall. BR2 and the exam experiments use prose/markdown targets.

**Q2. What is the fix?** How should sigma and nu be obtained on a prose target where no
compiler, AST or test suite exists? Note one measured surprise: counterfactual repair
(apply a fix, re-run both falsifiers, require both directions to agree) decides 41.9%
of pairs on MARKDOWN targets versus 16.7% on PYTHON targets - prose decides BETTER, the
opposite of what the project's own notes assume. Does that change your answer?
Is NO_SCORE with zero movement the right behaviour, or is there something better?

**Q3. Should nu be measured from the fix artefact** (lines changed, distinct locations
touched, whether a function signature or shared interface moves), and if so how would
you calibrate it to [0,1] with 129 parseable archived fixes as the only data? What is
the failure mode of getting this wrong? CC1's position: measure it, never show it to
the generating model, and rely on nu sitting in Phase 3 (after resolution) so the
ordering is enforced by the equation rather than by a prompt. Attack that.

# MEASUREMENT 3 — THE BUGZILLA DEFICIT FOR STEM

Yesterday all five panellists endorsed preserving duplicate records and all five
independently broke the Bugzilla analogy at the same joint: a software bug is a defect
in an artefact the maintainer controls, whereas a STEM claim can be wrong about the
world. Bugzilla has no state for "the reported behaviour is correct and the
SPECIFICATION is wrong."

The founder has ruled the bulletin-board / discussion-forum layer OUT of scope - it is a
UX build, expensive, and unnecessary to demonstrate the theory. It is marked as future
work. Also dropped entirely: any notion of a model monitoring discussion boards and
deriving its own theories.

What remains on the table is narrow and structural:
  (a) the missing epistemic state - a finding that was RIGHT about a target that was WRONG;
  (b) keying claims on their structure rather than their location. The code documents the
      limitation this addresses: location-only keying "cannot see a SECOND distinct defect
      in an already-flagged function" (reference_runner_v2.py:4331).

**Q4. What is the minimum change** to close (a) and (b) that is worth doing BEFORE BR2,
and what should be deferred? Is a new status value sufficient for (a), or does it need
more? For (b), is there anything cheaper than full schema enforcement that gets most of
the benefit? Answer in terms of what BR2 needs to be publishable, not what a finished
platform would want.
