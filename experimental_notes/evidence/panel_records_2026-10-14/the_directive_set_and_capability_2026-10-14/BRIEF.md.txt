## ITEM 3 — MEASURED CAPABILITY AND A STABLE MODEL IDENTITY, NOT VENDOR NAMES

**His ruling, 2026-10-14:** *"This is why giving models 'names' rather than hash values derived from measured capability is a very bad practice ... basing performance and building 'caps' that nobody asked for based purely on model names is clearly nonsensical!"* And earlier, four times: *"the only thing that should impact on capability is measured capability. A models name should have little to do with it, beyond recording this."* His scaling argument: *"what would happen if the schema had access to more than 5 or 7 models ... What if we had 10, or 15, or 500 models."*

**Measured.** Four name-keyed structures decide things — `PHENOTYPE_TRANSFORMS`, `MODEL_COHERENCE_BUDGETS`, and `COMPOSER_MODEL_MAP` (imported into the runner) — plus `DEFAULT_FALSIFIER_STRENGTH = ("Codex", "CC2", "ChatGPT", "Gemini", "DeepSeek", "Fable")` in `bench/routing.py:97` deciding ladder order. Resolved against the live roster: `CC2 -> opus_4_6` (cap 12,000), `Codex -> codex_5_3` (6,000), `ChatGPT -> chatgpt_5_4` (8,000), `Gemini -> gemini_3_1_pro` (8,000), `DeepSeek -> deepseek_v3` (4,000), and **`Fable -> no entry`, silently inheriting the 8,000 default**. Two live models are mapped onto stale phenotypes: Opus 4.7 onto a 4.6 entry, DeepSeek V4 Pro onto a v3 entry.

**AND IT HAS ALREADY COST MORE THAN A WRONG CAP.** `bench/reference_runner_v3.py:11633` records a panel finding of 2026-08-30: *"Fable had no COMPOSER_MODEL_MAP entry and no .toml, so it composed to FileNotFoundError -> 0 chars"* — **a model ran on an EMPTY system prompt and nothing said so**, while CC2 got 6,008 characters and Gemini 5,559. The failure is realised, not hypothetical.

**MY PROPOSED FIX.** Key every capability decision on a STABLE IDENTITY — a hash over (provider, model identifier, version) — with the vendor name recorded as PROVENANCE and deciding nothing. Capability itself comes from measured falsification outcomes per identity. Cold start is UNIFORM, not a frozen vendor order, and the uniform fallback retires itself per identity once a stated number of falsification outcomes exists. Retire `DEFAULT_FALSIFIER_STRENGTH`. An identity absent from the tables must be LOUD, never defaulted.

**What I want from you, and this is the item I most expect to be wrong about.** Both of you previously declined to delete the frozen tuple, on the ground that with no data every model scores identically and the tuple is the only ordering with a validated end-to-end result behind it. That reasoning is on the record and the founder has rejected its conclusion four times. So: say what breaks the cold-start tie if not a name, state the number of outcomes at which the fallback retires and how you derived it, and say what measured statistic capability should be — noting that the heterogeneity figure previously offered (chi-square 280.0138, df 4, p = 2.213221e-59) is computed on FINDING RATE, which ranks a model first that Exp 55 showed confirming 2 of 2 with detached falsifiers.
