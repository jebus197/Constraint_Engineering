# PANEL BRIEF — Is the harness yet a general-purpose STEM calculator, and what would "a fix" mean outside code?

## REQUIRED SECTION 1 — The question

**Is this harness as useful against a wide range of STEM topics as it currently is for code, and if not, what specific modifications would bring it meaningfully closer?**

The founder's words, which this brief exists to answer: *"there is also a separate question of what 'fixes' should mean in a setting that is not coding related, but is specific to any other given STEM topic?... CDSFL has been envisaged from the outset as a general purpose STEM calculator, operating on strict Popperian falsification principles. But in order for that to be true the entire harness has to be as equally useful against a wide range of STEM related topics, as it currently is for code."* He directs it to a free panel *"not only asking if we are there already or not, but if any improvements or modifications may be required in order to bring us meaningfully closer to that goal?"*

- `bench/tests/fixtures/stem/stem_fixtures.py` — the ground-truth corpus
- `scripts/stem_generality_brief_figures_2026-10-29.py` — the producer for every figure below

## REQUIRED SECTION 3 — Produce a fix, and test it

**A finding without a fix is incomplete, and a fix without a runnable falsifier that you have EXECUTED is a hypothesis.** Report the command you ran and the output you saw.

**Write your fix INTO the sandbox repository tree at its real path, as a file.** A fix described in prose is not delivered, and a fix written to scratch space is destroyed when the sandbox is torn down.

The substantive question to fix, rather than the count: **in code and mathematics a fix is an edit whose falsifier can be re-run, so admissibility is decidable by execution.** In an experimental domain a defective claim may be repairable only by narrowing it, by restating its boundary conditions, or by declaring it unfalsifiable on available evidence — none of which a falsifier can execute against. So the open question is not whether the harness can REVIEW non-code STEM, which it demonstrably can, but **whether its notion of a RESOLVED finding survives when no executable repair exists.**

Note before you answer that the corpus already encodes a case close to this: among its harm classes is a genuine metric improvement that carries a correctness regression, where the listing gets faster and stops de-duplicating. That is a repair which passes the obvious test and is still wrong.

## REQUIRED SECTION 4 — What would refute you

**State, before you conclude, what evidence would overturn your own answer.** If you judge the harness already general, name the measurement that would show it is not. If you judge it not general, name the measurement that would show it is — and say whether that measurement can be taken from the existing corpus or needs a new one.

Return these fields, so replies can be compared without a parser guessing:

## REQUIRED SECTION 6 — Termination

**Stop at diminishing returns**, which is this project's own criterion: stop when a further pass produces no new above-threshold findings, and say how many passes you ran. A finding is above threshold if missing it could cause real-world failure.

**NO COMPELLED CONVERGENCE.** Return your own independent verdict and your strongest falsification of the framing above. Disagreement is information and will be preserved, not smoothed. If you think the premise is wrong — that a general-purpose STEM calculator is not a coherent target, or that the code case is not the right baseline to generalise from — say so and show the measurement that makes the case.
