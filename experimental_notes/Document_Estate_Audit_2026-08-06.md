## The recommendation in one paragraph

Adopt **pace layering** (Brand's shearing layers, as Gartner formalised it for systems) as the placement rule, **Diátaxis** as the splitting rule *inside* the live surface only, **Nygard ADRs** as the decision spine — formalising a practice you already run informally — and a **records-management retention schedule** whose disposition action is always "transfer to archive", never "destroy". Use **Adair's ladder upward move as the retirement test and nothing else**. Then, crucially: **stop treating documents as entry points.** Reduce four document entry points to one *executable* entry point (`scripts/cdsfl_recover.py`), whose job is to name the documents and to report what it could not check. The taxonomy is the cheap part. The part that actually buys you anything is the rule that **no organisational rule is adopted unless a probe can check it**, added in the same commit as the rule.

## Testing your half-formed idea

**Working memory versus long-term memory is correct, and it has an industry name.** You do not need to invent it. Stewart Brand's *shearing layers* (1994) — the observation that a building's site, structure, skin, services and contents change on wildly different timescales and must therefore be decoupled — was adopted into systems architecture as Gartner's **pace-layered application strategy** (systems of record / differentiation / innovation). It is the same idea you reached for, and it is the correct one, because it is the only one of the four candidates that predicts *what a recovering agent needs to load first*. The measurement above is the test, and it passes.

**Jacob's Ladder is an audit instrument, not a placement rule.** I tested this and I agree with the assessment you were given. Adair's ladder sorts by abstraction, and abstraction does not correlate with load order here — the mathematical appendix and the resume pointer sit at the same rung and at opposite ends of the volatility spread. But the **upward move is genuinely valuable and should be adopted for exactly one job**: given any artefact, ask which live aim it serves; if the chain to a live aim breaks, that is a principled, non-arbitrary reclassification trigger. That is a real answer to "what should be dispensed with", and it is better than a date cutoff because it does not retire a two-year-old document that is still load-bearing as provenance. Use the ladder to decide *what is still live*. Use pace layers to decide *where live things sit*. They are orthogonal; do not choose between them.

## The four standards: verdicts

**Diátaxis — ADOPT, but narrowly. Reject as a directory taxonomy.** The diagnosis is confirmed by measurement (above), and the prescription — never mix modes in one document — is exactly the cut both big files need. But Diátaxis is a framework for *documentation*, and it has no quadrant for a **dated immutable record**, which is your single largest class (237 notes plus 389 TTS files). Forcing records into "explanation" would commit the very sin it warns against. So: Diátaxis governs the live surface only, and you need a fifth class it does not supply. That fifth class is what records management is for. The two standards are complementary, not competing, and applying each to the thing it was designed for is the opposite of inventing your own.

**Architecture Decision Records — ADOPT, and note you are already 80% there.** `CDSFL_Agent_Operational_Plan.md` carries two sections headed "Superseded resume pointer … retained for trail". `RECOVERY.md` carries inline dated correction blocks. `Rulings_Needed_2026-08-05.md` opens with a thirteen-row disposition table giving question, ruling and status. That *is* an ADR set; what it lacks is a stable identifier, a machine-readable status field, and an index. Nygard's format supplies all three at near-zero cost, and it addresses your stated pain word-for-word — deciding canonicality currently requires digging back through months of rationale. **Formalising an existing practice is far cheaper than importing a foreign one, and it is the same judgement in both directions.** Do not retro-fit ADRs to historical decisions; that is the expensive, low-value half. Number forward, and back-fill only decisions that are currently contested.

**Retention schedules — ADOPT, with one substitution.** Class rules are the only tractable answer to 227 uncounterparted files; per-file judgement does not scale and you should not spend a funded week on it. The substitution: records management distinguishes *retention* (how long you keep it) from *disposition* (destroy / transfer / permanent). Your constraint that rewriting history is a fault does not conflict with the standard — it simply fixes the disposition action at "transfer to archive" for every class. **The schedule then governs what loads, not what exists**, because your scarce resource is not disk, it is the reader's attention and the agent's context window. That reframe makes the standard fit your constraints without bending it.

**Volatility tiering — ADOPT as pace layering, derived not declared.** Four layers, assignment computed from `git log`, a file with no history reported as `UNKNOWN` loudly rather than guessed:

- **L0 Pointer** — changes every session. Resume pointer, active queue head, last accepted decisions. Always in context.
- **L1 State** — changes every arc. Current-state block, session states, run outcomes. Read on entering a phase.
- **L2 Reference and Explanation** — rarely. Architecture, glossary, mathematical appendix, reproducing, paper. Retrieved on demand.
- **L3 Record** — never again. Dated notes, superseded states, TTS mirrors. Archived, searchable, cited by name, never edited.

## What this does about brittleness — the honest answer

**No taxonomy fixes brittleness, and I will not pretend otherwise.** A beautifully layered set of documents that lie is worse than a messy set that announce their age, and the last two days are the proof.

The evidence is in your own source. `scripts/cdsfl_recover.py` carries this, verbatim, in its header comments: the previous probe searched for headings that had been deleted from RECOVERY.md in April 2026, so the pending-work section printed "(No pending work section found)" **for 113 days while the block was 1,575 lines long.** No reorganisation would have caught that. What caught it was writing down what the probe was actually looking for, and what it did when it failed to find it.

So the anti-brittleness content of this proposal is three rules, and they matter more than the taxonomy:

1. **No rule without a probe.** Any organisational rule adopted here ships with a check in `cdsfl_qc.py` in the same commit, or it is not adopted. The layer assignment is computed, so layer drift is detectable. Procedure purity is checkable — a numbered recovery step containing the token `superseded` is a finding.
2. **Probe coverage is itself reported.** `check_file_references` already prints its suppression tally rule by rule, with the stated reason that an unaudited filter is how ten real defects become indistinguishable from 173 false ones. Extend that discipline: a structural rule with no probe appears in the QC output as an uncovered rule, not as silence.
3. **The successor to a brittle document is a probe, not another document.** This is the direct answer to your observation that all four entry points grew because their predecessors proved brittle. Each new document was an attempt to fix a lying document by writing a truer one — and the new one aged at the same rate. Breaking that loop requires the entry point to be executable, so that it recomputes its claims every time it is read and marks what it could not verify. You already have the mechanism: `cdsfl_recover.py`'s three-state convention where a real absence, a failed lookup and a live finding render differently. **Documents become destinations. One executable becomes the door.**

**One measured hole to close now, as the first instance of rule 1.** `cdsfl_utils.check_file_references` suppresses every `~`-prefixed path from becoming a finding — reasonably, since home paths are outside the repository. But `resources/README.md` promises that everything needed is in this repository, and `RECOVERY.md` promises context rebuildable "from the repository alone". I counted **40 distinct `~/Desktop/CDSFL_tts/*.txt` citations across the live recovery surface**, of which the retention audit found 29 have no repository counterpart. Every one of them is, by construction, invisible to the checker. The rule: *a citation inside the live recovery surface must resolve inside the repository* — and a Desktop path there is a broken promise even when the file exists on your machine. That is roughly ten lines of code and it converts a class of 29 silent defects into 29 visible findings.
