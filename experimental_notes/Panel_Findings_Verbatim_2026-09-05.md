# Q4. Should “reductions must be discharged, not sampled” be adopted?

Yes, for reduction claims.

## Benefit

It prevents sampled agreement from being mistaken for identity. A finite grid can refute an equality, but it cannot prove equality over a symbolic or continuous domain.

This directly addresses the failure mode described in the appended tests: substitution tautologies and sampled perturbations can certify garbage models if the test checks the wrong residual.

## Cost

It forbids a currently useful practice: recording sampled numerical agreement as confirmation.

That has real cost:

- algebraically difficult but true reductions may remain `UNDISCHARGED`;
- review becomes slower;
- empirical sanity checks lose status as confirmations;
- numerical regression tests can no longer close a mathematical claim by themselves.

But this is the right asymmetry.

## Vocabulary rule

- Symbolic proof or exhaustive enumeration over declared finite scope: `CONFIRMED`.
- Counterexample by proof, enumeration, or sample: `REFUTED`.
- Sampled agreement only: `UNDISCHARGED`.
- Prose claim of verification without executable artifact: `UNDISCHARGED`.

**Evidence I would accept that I am wrong:** a class of reductions important to the project where symbolic proof and exhaustive enumeration are impossible, sampling is the only feasible method, and treating them as `UNDISCHARGED` causes worse decisions than treating them as provisionally confirmed.

# Q4. Should “reductions must be discharged, not sampled” be adopted?

## Yes.

Adopt it as a verdict-vocabulary asymmetry:

- symbolic proof or exhaustive enumeration may yield `CONFIRMED`;
- sampling may yield `REFUTED`;
- passing samples may yield only `UNDISCHARGED`, not `CONFIRMED`.

It prevents the central failure mode exposed in the pinned tests: substitution tautologies and sampled agreements masquerading as proof.

It forbids a useful current practice: using sampled agreement as provisional confirmation for broad symbolic claims.

That means more claims will remain `UNDISCHARGED`, especially where:

- the expression is too large for symbolic simplification;
- the domain is huge but finite;
- the model is empirical rather than algebraic;
- exhaustive enumeration is expensive.

The fix is not to discard sampling. The fix is to downgrade its verdict:

```text
