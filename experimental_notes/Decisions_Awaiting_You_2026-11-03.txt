PART 15. WHAT ADDITIONAL PASSES AND ADDITIONAL SEATS ACTUALLY BUY, DERIVED RATHER THAN ASSERTED

WHAT IT IS. You asked how much adding additional passes, and additional seats, reduces the risk that an answer is wrong, and whether the mathematics of probability gives insight into how low is low enough. It does, the answer is already implicit in the appendix, and it has never been stated this way. You also asked today whether this is the same question as the severity question. It is not, and the difference IS the answer.

PASSES AND SEATS ACT ON DIFFERENT TERMS, SO THEY ARE NOT SUBSTITUTES. Residual risk updates once per pass, and the composite update has exactly 2 fixed points, 1 and the re injection rate divided by the detection power. The second is the one the recursion actually reaches, so it is the floor. Passes iterate the map and carry residual risk DOWN TOWARD that floor, with each pass buying less than the one before, and the change falls to exactly 0 at the floor. That decay is gamma, and it is why gamma gates convergence rather than being reported beside it.

Seats enter somewhere else entirely. Detection power is built by multiplying together the probability that each seat misses, so adding a seat raises the detection power, and since the floor is the re injection rate divided by the detection power, at a re injection rate of 0.05 and a detection power of 0.45 that floor is 0.111111, so adding a seat LOWERS THE FLOOR ITSELF. A solver confirms that a second seat can never fail to raise detection power. Worked through with a detection rate of 0.9 and a per flaw probability of 0.5 and a re injection rate of 0.05, the floor falls from 0.111111 with 1 seat to 0.071685 with 2, 0.059979 with 3 and 0.051423 with 6, with the gain per seat shrinking each time.

So: no number of passes can cross the floor, and only a seat can move it. That is the plain answer to what a seat buys that a pass does not.

AND THIS IS WHERE VENDOR DIVERSITY GENUINELY MATTERS, which is the only part of your question that really needs paid models. The multiplication assumes the seats miss INDEPENDENTLY. 2 copies of the same model share their blind spots, and the arithmetic shows a second identical seat adds exactly 0 to detection power while a second independent seat adds 0.247500 at those same values. So a different vendor buys independence, and a second instance of the same model buys nothing. That is the biodiversity hypothesis written as an equation. The detection power, around 0.45 at the worked values, and the re injection rate, 0.05 at those values, can both be estimated from the existing archive with no money at all. The INDEPENDENCE of architectures cannot be, because all 6 simulated seats share 1 architecture by construction.

HOW LOW SHOULD LOW BE. There is no universal constant, and that is a result rather than a gap. The floor is a property of the particular experiment, so the acceptance bound has to be derived for each experiment, which is what both panel seats concluded independently on 1 November. The defensible criterion is relative: accept when residual risk is within a small margin of its own derived floor, and report the floor beside it so a reader can see how much headroom there was. A fixed target below the floor is unreachable at any number of passes.

A DEFECT FOUND WHILE DERIVING THIS, AND IT IS A DOCUMENTATION HAZARD RATHER THAN A LIVE FAULT. The appendix also carries an expected improvement formula. At the floor, that formula returns a strictly positive number, and a solver confirms it can never be 0 or negative there. So a stopping rule that read the expected improvement formula would never stop, at the exact point where the recursion has already stopped moving. No live code reads it, which is why this is raised rather than fixed, and the 2 expressions are measuring different things so the disagreement is not automatically an error. It needs a ruling on which is canonical.

RECOMMENDATION AND WHY. Estimate the detection power and the re injection rate, the 2 terms whose ratio is the floor, from the existing archive first, because it is free, and report the derived floor beside residual risk in every run. That is simplest sufficient. It is additive because it adds a reported bound and removes nothing. On composability, no claim is made: the archive estimate alone has not been measured against any alternative.

DECISION 10. WHETHER THE SEVERITY FORMULA OR THE EVIDENCE RULE SHOULD CHANGE
Section 7.7 of the mathematical appendix states that a finding's severity is the weight of its constraint class, times confidence, times a verification factor. The verification factor is the harness's own falsifier verdict and is recorded. The other 2 are not, and for opposite reasons, which is the whole of this item.

Confidence is refused by your own operational directive. Its gate on detection probability reads that the figure must come from actual tool execution and not from self assessed confidence, and that a claim based on being certain is not admissible while a figure derived from 9 of 10 relevant tests catching the flaw class is admissible. So confidence appearing on 0 of 3,311 archived findings, with a Wilson 95 percent interval from 0.000000 to 0.001159, is the directive working as designed rather than a gap.

The hard or soft classification is the opposite case: required and homeless. Section 11 of the same directive says that before producing any output, every constraint must be classified as hard or soft. Of 98 distinct fields across those 3,311 findings, 0 hold such a classification. The only near candidate is an integer class identifier from section 4, which the shadow's own documentation records as a different quantity.

So the specification cannot be implemented as written without accepting self reported confidence, and the directive forbids exactly that. The shadow's substitution of the asserted severity for the first 2 factors is therefore not a shortcut. It is the only admissible route that exists, which reframes the severity question you have asked 3 times.

What you decide. Whether section 7.7's second factor changes, or the directive's refusal does, or the reduced form becomes the official implementation.

The recommendation, and why. Narrow the directive's refusal to permit a confidence that is itself a function of tool output. It is the smallest change that leaves both documents true: the directive's own gate on detection probability already draws its line at self assessed confidence rather than at confidence as such, so a confidence built from tool receipts satisfies it and the formula becomes implementable unchanged. Amending the mathematics would edit a formula to fit a collection accident. Adopting the reduced form leaves the appendix stating something nothing implements.

Adding the hard or soft field is separable and is not a decision, because the directive already requires the classification, so recording it carries out an instruction rather than changing it.

DECISION 13. WHETHER V BAR SHOULD COUNT A MODEL'S OWN CLAIM AND WHAT THE 4 SIBLING RUNNERS DO

DECISION 19. WHETHER A LIST THAT MAY NOT TERMINATE IS ACCEPTABLE, AND IT WAS HIDDEN FROM YOU

There are 2 things in this decision and the second is arguably worse than the first.

The first is a ratio that stood at 0.8986 when this decision was written and at 0.9034 when it was last checked. It moves with every entry added or closed, so take it from its producer, the task list burndown script, rather than from this sentence. Treating each closed entry as producing some number of new entries, a ratio below 1 means the list finishes with certainty and a ratio at or above 1 means it need never finish. On the 11th of September it was 0.4444 with the whole interval below 1, so the list demonstrably terminated. On the 17th of October it was 0.8148 with an interval from 0.6155 to 1.0787, and an entry was raised then to tell you the upper bound had crossed 1. Tonight, from 186 entries added against 207 closed, it is 0.8986 with an interval from 0.7374 to 1.0950, and an exact test against a ratio of 1 returns 0.3130.

The point estimate is still below 1. What has gone is the ability to assert termination at 95 percent confidence, and the evidence against a non terminating list has got weaker rather than stronger, because that test value rose from 0.0873 to 0.3130.

Tonight's 5 new entries are not the cause, and that was checked before it was said. Immediately before them the ratio was 0.8786 with an upper bound of 1.0725, already above 1. The crossing happened during the burst of entries raised between the 1st and the 3rd of November, while 4 long investigations were running at once.

The second thing is that this entry was marked as done while its own first line says it needs your ruling, and while the field that records your ruling was empty. The morning report builds its decisions section from entries that are still open, so a decision you had never made was invisible on the only surface that exists to show you decisions. That is the same suppression found tonight in 2 other entries, where a ruling of yours from the 17th of October silences questions added weeks later. Repetition is visible to you. Suppression is not. The marker is corrected to open, which is a record fix and not a reopening of anything you decided.

The test that reports that 0.8986 is deliberately left red, because its own author wrote that silencing it would be the one forbidden action. It is also not among the checks that run before a commit, which is why it has been saying this since at least the 1st of November with nobody reading it. That is the cost question in decision 16.

What you decide. Whether a list whose termination is no longer demonstrated is acceptable as it stands.

The recommendation, and why. Accept it and keep closing. The point estimate is below 1, no test rejects termination, and the rise is explained by a 3 day burst of findings raised while several investigations ran together. The honest position is that termination is not currently demonstrated, which is a weaker claim than non termination and does not by itself call for a change of plan. The alternative that would force 0.8986 down is a cap on how fast new entries may be raised, and that works by suppressing findings, which is the opposite of what this project is for. If instead you want the ratio treated as a stopping condition, so that the runway does not resume until the whole interval sits below 1, say so, because that is a materially different plan from the current one.

DECISION 20. WHETHER TO PRODUCE THE ERROR FIGURE OR RETIRE IT

Both your instruction files state, in 2 places each, that of 12 errors on the 11th of October, 10 were catchable by more careful reasoning before running anything. They give that as 0.8333, with a 95 per cent interval from 0.5520 to 0.9530 and a binomial probability of 0.0193 against an even split.

0 scripts reproduce any of those 4 figures. It is also the figure cited whenever the depth over breadth instruction is discussed, including in the answer written for you tonight, which is how it came to light.

The denominator cannot be recovered. The 11th of October carries 14 memory files, 6 of them lessons and 8 of them findings, and which 12 of those 14 the figure counted is recorded nowhere. Reconstructing it would mean choosing a population to fit a number that is already written down, which is the shape this project refuses.

The single existing producer classifies errors on a different axis. It asks whether an error was in the measuring instrument, in the subject being measured, or in how the work was sequenced, rather than whether it was catchable before running anything. So generalising it by date would answer a different question and would not reproduce the figure.

Meanwhile 2 producers do exist for the same underlying concern, and both are sharper than the figure they would replace. The first finds that 16 of 18 of a night's recorded errors were in the measuring instruments rather than in the code being measured, which is 0.888889 with a Wilson interval from 0.672002 to 0.968980 and a Clopper Pearson interval from 0.652880 to 0.986249. What you decide. Whether to spend the work producing the original figure, to retire it in favour of the 2 that have producers, or to leave it flagged where it stands.

The recommendation, and why. Retire it, under the additive standard's removal clause. That clause admits a removal only on a committed measurement showing the replacement dominates on a named property, and the named property here is reproducibility: the replacements have producers and the incumbent cannot have any. Nothing is deleted meanwhile. The flag is already in place in both files, naming the 2 producers that do exist, so the figure cannot be quoted again innocently.
