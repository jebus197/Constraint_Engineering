MATHS REVISION REVIEW, SYNTHESIS OF 6 PAID PANEL ROUNDS

20 September 2026, 23:05 BST

WHAT THE REVIEW DECIDED

A proposed revision to the project's mathematical model, produced by an unbounded frontier model asked to improve it, was reviewed across 6 paid panel rounds by 7 seats. The founder's question was whether the project needs the revision, or whether it is an example of unbounded model churn.

The answer is neither, and the review's own framing turned out to be wrong. The revision is largely a correction list, which every returning seat concluded from round 3 onward. But the falsifiability defect the review spent 5 rounds charging to the revision is already in this project's own appendix, in section 7.12, and has been there since 31 March 2026. The revision did not introduce it. It made it visible.

THE ROUNDS

6 rounds were dispatched. Round 1 returned 6 seats of 7 and 69,131 characters. Round 2 returned 5 and 67,500. Round 3 returned 5 and 78,653. Round 4 returned all 7 and 101,895. The blind round returned all 7 and 103,307. The open round returned all 7 and 118,234. The total is 538,720 characters of seat output.

Rounds 1 to 3 lost seats to defects in the project's own dispatch machinery, all since repaired. The paid seats had no tool that could read a file. An empty-content retry existed in one tool loop and not in the one the paid seats actually ran. And a forced-synthesis call carried a fixed temperature that one seat's endpoint refuses.

The seats were cc2 and fable on the founder's existing subscription, cx, cgpt, ge and ds through OpenRouter, and kimi on Moonshot credits. The authorised ceiling was 12 pounds and spend across all 6 rounds stayed well inside it.

FINDING 1. THE DEGENERACY WAS ALREADY OURS

The cc2 seat, working blind, established that the project's own appendix section 7.12 carries the identical degeneracy the review had been charging to the revision. Under a stated substitution, section 7.12's recursion is the revised action step term for term, and the 2 fixed points coincide exactly with a symbolic residual of 0.

In the seat's own words, the 2 are one object in 2 coordinate systems. It added that rejecting the revision on those grounds would require rejecting the project's own section 7.12 on identical grounds, and that the revision's real contribution is to make visible a cost the project was already paying silently.

5 further seats adopted this in the open round, and every one of them re-derived it rather than taking it on trust. The fable seat confirmed the substitution was an exact identity before accepting it. The kimi seat adopted the conclusion while attacking the proof of it in the same finding.

FINDING 2. THE ASSISTANT'S HEADLINE CLAIM WAS FALSE

CC1 reported to the founder, and wrote into a paid brief sent to 7 seats, that 191 archived fixes had been refused on the strength of a constant nobody measured.

Measured on both refusal paths, the unmeasured constants decided nothing at all. All 191 refusals are structural: the proposed fix parsed no usable blocks, scored 0, and was refused before any constant was read. Of the 902 scored fixes that reached the gate, 0 were refused, with a confidence interval from 0 to 0.4241 percent.

The repository already recorded this, pinned by a committed test, in a docstring inside the live runner stating that the threshold flag reads false 0 times under every method and every corpus tried, and that the gate has never rejected a fix. CC1 contradicted a test-pinned measurement without consulting it. The kimi and cc2 seats both caught it first.

Worse, the producing script continued printing the withdrawn claim on a clean run until the kimi seat found it by executing a figure the brief itself had declared.

FINDING 3. THE FOUNDER'S FRAMING HOLDS, 7 SEATS OF 7

All 7 blind seats agreed that treating the decay measure as a ruler rather than a verdict dissolves 3 of the 4 tensions the review had uncovered. They are not concessions but correct descriptions of what a measuring instrument can license.

A gate that reads observations cannot fire on a class of flaw that is never observed, which is a scope boundary rather than a defect. A discovery curve flat at a small constant is indistinguishable from exhaustion at any realistic noise floor, which is a researcher's judgement under bounded recursion. And residual risk sitting behind a flat curve is the model reporting an introduction rate above 0, not concealing it.

The 4th tension, that the model can be parameterised to hit any target risk, does not dissolve. It becomes a calibration requirement.

FINDING 4. THE ASSISTANT WAS REFUTED ON THE 4TH TENSION BY 4 SEATS OF 7

On 4 independent grounds. The ge seat showed that fixing one parameter leaves the other free, making the claim mathematically false. The fable seat showed the calculation had used an invalid domain: under the settled parameter map the reachable band is narrower and carries a floor. The cx seat observed that parameters fitted on the outcomes they are meant to explain remain unfalsifiable, so calibration must be done in advance. The ds seat called a calibration requirement that has never been executed a promissory note rather than a defence.

CC1's own script had printed that measuring the parameter does not shrink the interval but translates it, before CC1 told the founder it restores the model's power to forbid outcomes.

FINDING 5. THE SCORING GATE IS THE LIVE PROBLEM

All 7 blind seats independently found that the fix score does not discriminate inside the band it guards.

672 of 902 scored fixes are exactly 1.0, a rate of 74.5011 percent with a confidence interval from 71.5570 to 77.2374 percent. The admissibility term is binary across all 1,247 archived decisions, taking the value 1 on 902 of them and 0 on 345, never anything in between. The cc2 seat measured roughly 2.93 effective levels across the whole score.

The consequence is that the entire over-refusal debate, which consumed most of 3 rounds and produced 3 competing figures, is operationally inert. The lowest score any archived fix received is 0.740000. Both disputed thresholds sit below the entire observed distribution, and 0 of 902 archived fixes fall below either. Moving the threshold between them changes 0 archived decisions.

FINDING 6. BOUNDED RECURSION IS A THEOREM, AND THE ASSISTANT'S SKETCH WAS WRONG

CC1 put a 3-step proof sketch into the open brief. 2 of the 3 steps are false, confirmed independently by symbolic algebra and by a constraint solver.

The first false step claimed a strictly positive floor whenever either the novel-defect rate or the re-injection rate is positive. At a novel-defect rate of 0 the floor is 0 for any re-injection rate, and the solver returns unsatisfiable on the conjunction, so the second half of the disjunction does no work at all.

The third false step claimed the marginal gain tends to 0 as risk approaches the floor. At a positive floor it does not: at the stated parameters it equals 4 over 45, and the solver shows it is strictly positive everywhere on the open unit square. CC1 had substituted the detection-phase gain for the composite map's net change per cycle.

The theorem that survives is stronger than the one attempted. The appendix's own composition is a strictly increasing continuous self-map of the unit interval, so every orbit is monotone and bounded and therefore converges, needing no contraction argument. The per-cycle gains telescope to at most 1, which gives a finite bound on the number of cycles worth running.

It also inverts the premise. The load-bearing assumption is that the marginal value of a cycle is bounded by a constant times the gain. With no re-injection and a logarithmic utility, every cycle is worth the same fixed amount forever and the theorem fails. So a positive re-injection rate is not needed for convergence; it is what makes the economic step robust.

A second condition emerged that nobody had held: above a critical re-injection rate the only fixed point in the unit interval is certain failure, and no number of rounds helps.

FINDING 7. THE OPTIMAL NUMBER OF ROUNDS IS CALCULABLE, AND CAME OUT AT 9.37

From the project's own decay law and its own stopping rule, the optimal number of rounds is the round-1 finding rate divided by the researcher's stopping threshold, raised to the power of 1 over the decay parameter. The round-1 rate stands in for the complexity of the artefact, and the threshold is the researcher's own cost and value judgement. At a decay parameter of one half this reduces to the inverse square root law of precision, stated in this project in March 2026.

Fitted to the project's own March data the parameters come out at 4.89 and 0.709, and at a threshold of 1 finding per round the optimum is 9.37 rounds. The founder recorded that experimental runs had been capped at around 9 to 15. CC1 called that a coincidence. The founder corrected it, and the founder was right: it had been calculated.

The gap is that nothing in the harness uses it. The fit runs on every round and feeds only the convergence gate, while the round budget remains a typed integer.

FINDING 8. CONTAINMENT, AND WHY IT KEPT FAILING

The dispatcher builds a sandbox copy for every seat, and the round logs record 7 copies for 7 seats in every round. The tool module used by the seats reached over a network consulted the panel working directory 0 times, resolving every path against the live repository instead, so 5 of the 7 copies were built and never entered.

In round 4 that allowed one seat to read 4.7945 percent of another seat's reply verbatim, with a confidence interval from 2.8772 to 7.8858 percent, by running a recursive search across a directory that accumulates each answer as its seat finishes.

Read-only confinement could never have prevented this. The kernel-enforced wrapper governs what a seat may write, and this defect was entirely about what a seat could read. The repair roots tool paths in the calling seat's own copy and is held by 7 tests, 5 of which fail against the previous code. Blindness then arrived at no extra cost, because a copy taken before any reply exists cannot contain one.

FINDING 9. WHAT THE BLIND AND OPEN ROUNDS MEASURED ABOUT THE PANEL ITSELF

The blind brief carried CC1's own position and invited attack on it. Measured afterwards, only 2 of the 7 seats worked genuinely independently of that position, 4 were mixed, and 1 refuted nothing CC1 had said anywhere. The anchoring bought 4 useful refutations and cost 5 partly captured seats, so the open brief contained no CC1 position at all.

The open round produced selective incorporation rather than deference. 2 seats reversed their central blind position on evidence. 5 re-derived an adopted finding rather than accepting it. And 1 seat held its position alone against 6 others, supported by 12 executed symbolic checks, and did not fold.

One artefact contaminates the adoption measurement and is recorded rather than hidden. The cc2 seat reviewed its own blind reply without recognising it, treating its own finding as another seat's, which makes 1 of its 2 reported adoptions a self-adoption. It also improved the work: believing the finding to be external, the seat obeyed the brief's instruction not to verify its own paraphrase, re-derived the result with a negative control, and refuted 2 of its own earlier claims.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN

The scoring gate repair, which is where 7 seats independently converged, and where the threshold debate turns out not to matter.

Wiring the optimal round count as an advisory projection available from round 3. This is proposed and not built, and advisory by design, so that the ruler reports and does not decide.

Whether the bounded recursion theorem, in its corrected form, belongs in the appendix's own table of falsifiable claims. 6 seats say it does and 1 dissents.

Whether a further round is warranted, decided by the project's own stopping rule rather than committed to in advance.
