## PART 2. THE ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION

The collapsed equation is correct. It was never in doubt after checking: at a prior of 0.5 with a single flaw class, the recursion gives exactly the closed form the appendix states, confirmed by SymPy, by mpmath at 40 decimal places, and by Wolfram.

Your instinct that a written-down derivation must trace both ways is right, and your own condition for it, that each form is made equally apparent, turns out to be exactly the right condition. Where nothing is hidden, the map between the 2 families is an exact bijection: per flaw class, and across a vector of flaw classes, a detection fraction converts to a residual risk and back and returns the original identically. A detection fraction of 0.271 maps to a risk of 0.421631 and back to 0.271 exactly.

Where it stops being a two-way street is at the aggregate, when the model sums over flaw classes with weights. There, 2 different sets of per-class coverages can give the same overall coverage but different overall risk. CC2 found that and was right.

But CC2's framing of it was too strong, and this is the part worth keeping. The overall coverage figure is already many-to-one over its own inputs: 2 different class vectors both give a coverage of 0.5. And the overall risk figure is many-to-one too. So the information is not destroyed by converting between coverage and risk. It is destroyed by the weighted sum, identically in both coordinate systems. Neither is privileged. Your symmetry holds everywhere information is preserved, and fails only where the model deliberately throws information away.



## PART 3. WHAT THE DOCUMENT SWEEP FOUND AND FIXED

The appendix claimed that all 5 links in the 6-stage model lineage are strict generalisations. Executed link by link, that is wrong in 1 place and understated in another.

Four links are genuine nestings, where the earlier model is the later one with parameters fixed.

One link, the recursive collapse, is stronger than a nesting. It is an exact identity: the recursion unrolls into the batch Bayesian form for every number of passes, because a single pass multiplies the odds by exactly 1 over 1 minus the detection probability. That is why the prior drops out of the update rule.

One link is not a generalisation at all. One family reports what fraction of flaws were caught, a number between 0 and 1. The other reports how much risk remains. The risk figure can only range from 0 up to the prior, so with a prior of 0.5 it can never exceed 0.5, so for any prior below 1 it cannot even produce the numbers the coverage model produces. The prior that would make them agree depends on the very quantity being measured, and a parameter that has to be retuned at every point is not a parameter setting.

Nine places carried the over-broad claim and all 9 are corrected: 2 in the white paper, 1 in the README, and 6 in the appendix, including an off-by-one where the text said 5 stages above a table with 6 rows.

Two of those sites carried a subtler error worth naming. Both called the risk expression the white paper's simple model. It is the simple model remapped, not the simple model. At a detection rate of 0.1 over 3 passes the coverage is 0.271 and the risk is 0.421631, a gap of 0.150631.

I checked the 3 remaining places that still say strict generalisation, and all 3 are true, so all 3 were left alone. I did not over-correct.
