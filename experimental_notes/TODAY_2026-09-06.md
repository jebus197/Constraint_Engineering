## PART 2. WHAT I READ, SINCE YOU ASKED ME TO READ BEFORE WRITING

The most important thing I found is that our lead citation would have been the wrong one.

Thomas Nickles, 1973, Two Concepts of Intertheoretic Reduction, Journal of Philosophy, volume 70, issue 7, pages 181 to 201. Nickles noticed that philosophers and physicists use the word reduction in opposite directions. Philosophers say the older theory reduces to the newer one, by being derived from it. Physicists say the newer, richer theory reduces to the older one in a limit, as when relativity becomes Newtonian mechanics as velocity over the speed of light squared goes to 0. Nickles allowed the operations to include limits and approximations, not only logical deduction.

Ours is the physicist's sense throughout. Our combined model becomes the machine only model as the human term vanishes. Nothing is derived from anything with a translation manual. So Nickles is the anchor, and this is the correction that matters.

Ernest Nagel, 1961, The Structure of Science. This is the broad tradition and deserves a citation as such. Nagel's reduction derives the laws of one science from another together with bridge laws that connect the 2 vocabularies. That is not what we do. Our reductions never leave the same vocabulary. One parameter goes to a limit and the formula becomes a simpler formula already in the paper. Citing Nagel as our lead would name a tradition our work does not belong to.

Robert Batterman, 2002, The Devil in the Details. This is the sharp objection our claims have to survive, and it is the reason the reading was worth doing. Batterman distinguishes regular from singular limits. A limit is singular when the behaviour at the limit is of a fundamentally different character from the behaviour on the way to it. Phase transitions are his standard case. Singular limits block reduction outright: the simpler theory you claim to recover exists only exactly at the limit and never on approach.

We pass this test, and I checked rather than assumed. All 4 reduction branches of the combined model are regular limits, meaning the value at the point equals the 2 sided limit approaching it. Verified today with SymPy, and the result is committed as an executable check rather than a claim in prose.

Kemeny and Oppenheim, 1956, and Kenneth Schaffner are the other 2 names worth a line. Schaffner extends Nagel to cover cases where the newer theory corrects rather than merely absorbs the older one, using limiting assumptions, which is nearer to us than Nagel is.

The Bohr correspondence principle is the obvious historical ancestor of the physicist's sense, but I did not find it treated that way in the sources I read, so I am not citing it as one until I have read more.

## PART 4. WHAT WAS COMPLETED

The canonical formal statement in the white paper is corrected. Executing its 5 reduction claims rather than reading them refuted 2 of them.

The first said the model reduces to the simple corroboration model under uniform assumptions. It omitted the independence condition. With independence the reduction does hold, and it is a better result than the paper claimed: the human passes simply add to the machine's, so 3 machine passes plus 2 human passes behave exactly like 5 machine passes. Without independence no whole number of passes reproduces the value at all. At 20 percent detection per pass, 3 machine passes, 2 human passes and correlation 0.5, the model gives 0.58016, and the number of passes that would be needed to match it is 3.889. Wolfram confirmed this by exhaustive proof rather than by example.

The second said every simpler model in the paper is a special case of the combined model. That is a universal asserted without anyone enumerating the set, which is this project's recurring failure shape. Enumerated: 3 of the 4 nest, and the recursive state equation does not. It carries a pole and a novelty floor that the combined model has no parameter for, and the paper elsewhere says it supersedes the simple model, so it was never underneath.

Both corrections are discharged by a committed script, 19 assertions across 7 checks, using SymPy and mpmath, and independently cross checked in Wolfram Language.
