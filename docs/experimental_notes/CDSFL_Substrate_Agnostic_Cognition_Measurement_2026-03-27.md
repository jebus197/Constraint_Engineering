# Substrate-Agnostic Cognition Measurement

**Date:** 27 March 2026

---

## The Specific Mathematical Insight

The 10-component mathematical framework built to measure AI model analytical capability does not depend on the source of the signal being a machine. It measures cognitive behaviour patterns — decay curves, abstraction levels, adoption deltas, verification engagement. These patterns exist in any system that iteratively analyses problems. Machine or human.

The framework was discovered by measuring machines. But the mathematics applies to any analytical cognition regardless of substrate.

This has a specific implication the founder identified. If the same mathematics that measures model performance also measures user cognitive level, then the system can calibrate its output to the user automatically. No expert is needed in the delivery loop. The expert's contribution is frozen in the domain configuration. The mathematical framework handles everything else.

---

## How the Measurement Works on Both Sides

When an AI model reviews a proof, it produces findings across multiple rounds. Those findings have measurable properties: decay rate (how quickly the finding rate drops), abstraction level (how deep each finding is), verification rate (how many findings survive computational checking), and independence (how much the model's output changes after seeing others' work).

When a human interacts with the system, their questions, corrections, and responses produce the same measurable properties. A child asking about gravity produces questions that cluster at low abstraction ("what makes things fall down"). An expert produces questions at high abstraction ("does the inverse square law break down at quantum scales"). The abstraction level of the interaction IS the measurement of the user's cognitive level.

The system does not need to be told "this is a child" or "this is an expert." It measures the interaction pattern using the same Abstraction Index that measures model performance. Over approximately five interactions, the measurement converges on the user's operating level — the same way the model calibration converges after approximately five reviews.

The decay curve of the user's questions measures their engagement pattern. A user who asks one question and moves on shows steep decay (surface engagement). A user who asks follow-up after follow-up, going deeper each time, shows ascending abstraction — the same pattern the founder exhibited. The system recognises this mathematically and adapts: surface users get concise answers, ascending users get progressively deeper material.

The adoption delta measures how critically the user engages with the system's output. A user who accepts everything uncritically has a high adoption delta (they adopt whatever the system says). A user who challenges, questions, and pushes back has a low adoption delta (they maintain independence). The system recognises this and adapts: uncritical users get more verification flags and confidence qualifiers, critical users get more technical depth and fewer hedges.

## Why the Domain Expert Requirement Breaks

Under the traditional model, domain expertise is required at the point of delivery. You need an expert to explain physics to a student because the expert knows what level to pitch the explanation at, knows what simplifications are safe, knows what common misconceptions to address.

Under the mathematical model, the measurement framework replaces the expert in the delivery loop. The framework measures the user's level (via Abstraction Index). The domain configuration provides the expert's methodology (what to check, what common errors exist, what the hard constraints are). The verification layer ensures correctness at any level of simplification (a simple explanation that is wrong fails verification just as a complex one does).

The expert is still essential for creating the configuration. They encode their methodology, their constraint classifications, their verification criteria. That encoding is done once. After that, the mathematical framework handles matching the configuration's expertise to each user's level. The expert contributed knowledge. The framework contributes adaptation. The combination serves anyone.

## The Mathematical Specifics

The user's cognitive profile is computed from the same formulas as the model's cognitive profile.

**User Abstraction Index:** H-user computed from the complexity of their questions, the depth of their follow-ups, and the specificity of their corrections. Uses the same Formality, Information Density, and Generalisation Scope factors as the model's H(x).

**User Engagement Decay:** lambda-user computed from their question frequency across an interaction session. Steep decay means surface engagement. Flat or ascending means sustained or deepening engagement.

**User Independence:** delta-user computed from how much their understanding changes after receiving the system's output. High delta means they adopt the system's framing uncritically. Low delta means they maintain their own analytical framework.

**User Calibration:** E-user converges over approximately five interactions. The system's estimate of the user's capability level stabilises after enough data. Early interactions may be mis-calibrated. The system should flag this: "I am still learning your level, please let me know if my explanations are too simple or too complex."

These measurements are passive. The user does not fill in a questionnaire or declare their level. The system infers it from the interaction itself — the same way the bench test infers model capability from output patterns without asking the model to self-assess.

## What This Is Not

This is not a claim that AI will democratise education. That is a generic prediction that requires no mathematical framework to state.

This is a specific claim that the mathematical framework built for measuring AI analytical capability constitutes a **substrate-agnostic measurement of cognitive interaction** that can be applied to both sides of the human-AI boundary. The model side provides quality assurance (is the analysis genuine). The user side provides adaptive calibration (what level should the output target). One framework. Two applications. Same mathematics.

Whether this specific claim is correct depends on whether the mathematical framework produces meaningful measurements when applied to human interaction patterns. That is testable but untested. The framework was validated on machine output. Human validation requires a separate study.
