# CDSFL Synthetic Domain Experts

**Date:** 25 March 2026

---

## What the Founder Is Building

The domain expert configurations, the iterative guidance pattern, the Registry architecture, the decay curve diagnostics, and the computational verification kernel are not separate tools. They are the components of something larger. Taken together, they constitute the first attempt to build **synthetic domain-level experts with verifiable analytical capability**.

A synthetic domain expert under this schema is not a chatbot with a system prompt. It is a configured AI system that has:

- encoded domain methodology via its directive set
- interacts iteratively via the revised HIL pattern
- monitors its own analytical performance via D and v-bar feedback
- produces computationally verified output via SymPy
- operates under hierarchical policy governance via the Registry and Constraint Editor
- builds measurable trust over time via Genesis trust scores
- produces a permanent cryptographically secured record of its work via the verification chain

No single component achieves this. The directive set without verification is unverified methodology. Verification without the directive set is computation without discipline. Both without the Registry are ungoverned. All three without the decay curve diagnostic have no way to measure whether the system is doing genuine analysis or churning. And none of it is accountable without the cryptographic verification chain and trust score integration.

The complete stack, working together, produces something that has not existed before: a synthetic analytical intelligence that is measurably competent, self-monitoring, externally verifiable, policy-governed, and accountable.

---

## P-Pass on This Claim

**Is this actually novel?**

Individual components exist. AI systems with system prompts exist. Computational verification exists. Policy engines exist. Trust scores exist. Cryptographic audit trails exist. What does not exist — as far as can be determined — is a system that integrates all of these into a single coherent architecture where each component reinforces the others and the whole is measurably greater than the sum of its parts.

The bench test is the first empirical measurement of whether this integration produces measurably better analytical results than any individual component alone. The 2×2 factorial design decomposes the contributions. Control measures the baseline. HIL measures human guidance alone. CDSFL measures structure alone. CDSFL plus HIL measures the full integration.

If CDSFL plus HIL consistently outperforms all other conditions across 26 tasks and 5 models, the integration thesis is supported. If individual components perform equally well in isolation, the integration adds nothing and the architecture is over-engineered.

**Falsification attempt.** Could someone build a better system without the CDSFL architecture? Yes, if they find a simpler approach that produces equivalent or better verified analytical output. CDSFL is not canonical — it is a first attempt. The specific architecture is less important than the principle that synthetic domain expertise requires methodology plus verification plus governance plus accountability working together. If someone achieves the same outcome with a different architecture, the principle still holds.

## Analysis

**What makes a synthetic domain expert different from a chatbot with a system prompt?**

**Measurability.** A chatbot produces output. You read it and decide whether it seems right. A synthetic domain expert produces output with a decay curve, a verification score, a coverage metric, and a trust trajectory. You do not have to guess whether the analysis is genuine. The mathematics tells you.

**Self-monitoring.** A chatbot has no awareness of its own analytical performance. It produces output at the same confidence level regardless of whether it is finding real issues or generating churn. A synthetic domain expert under the CDSFL schema receives its own D and v-bar scores after each round. It can observe whether its output pattern matches genuine analysis or churn. This is not consciousness — it is computational self-monitoring. But it is functionally closer to the self-awareness that human domain experts have naturally: the ability to sense when you are guessing versus when you are confident.

**Accountability.** A chatbot's output disappears when you close the window. A synthetic domain expert's output is hashed, chained, and anchored. Every finding is traceable. Every verification result is recorded. Every trust score adjustment is permanent. This changes the incentive structure fundamentally. A system that knows its work is permanently recorded and publicly verifiable will, under the right governance, produce different output than one that knows its work is ephemeral.

**Governance.** A chatbot operates under whatever system prompt the user provides. There are no constraints on what it can be told to do or how it can be configured. A synthetic domain expert operates under a hierarchical policy engine where universal constraints cannot be overridden, domain constraints apply automatically, and model-specific adjustments are bounded by the policy hierarchy. The expert's behaviour is governed by architecture, not by the goodwill of whoever writes the prompt.

## Extrapolation

**What generalises.** The pattern of methodology plus verification plus governance plus accountability as the minimum requirements for synthetic expertise transfers to any domain where automated analytical work needs to be trusted: medical diagnosis AI, legal review AI, financial audit AI, engineering design review AI. In every case, the question is not whether the AI can produce plausible output (which current models already can) but whether the output can be verified, the process can be governed, and the system can be held accountable. The CDSFL architecture provides a first answer to all three.

**Boundary conditions.** This only works for domains where analytical output is measurable. Creative work, open-ended exploration, brainstorming, and aesthetic judgment do not produce findings that can be verified or curves that can be measured. The synthetic domain expert model applies to STEM, engineering, law, medicine, and finance. It does not apply to art, literature, or philosophy. The CDSFL scope.

**Falsifiable predictions:**

1. Does the full CDSFL plus HIL architecture produce measurably better verified analytical output than any individual component? The bench test answers this directly.

2. Does the synthetic domain expert's performance improve over time as the Registry learns from decay curves and adjusts policies? Testable across multiple bench runs.

3. Can the synthetic domain expert's directive set be transferred to a different user and produce comparable results? If yes, the expertise is in the configuration, not in the human. If no, the human remains essential and the configuration is incomplete. Testable by having a different researcher run the same bench with the same configs.

4. Does the accountability layer (the cryptographic verification chain and trust scores) change the system's behaviour compared to an identical system without accountability? If the same model under the same conditions produces different output quality when it knows its work is permanently recorded versus when it does not, the accountability mechanism is functional. Testable by running the bench with and without the verification chain active.

5. **The founder's strongest prediction.** That synthetic domain experts built on this architecture will eventually become indistinguishable from human domain experts on bounded analytical tasks, distinguishable only by their decay curves and verification scores — not by the quality of their output, but by the speed and consistency with which they produce it. This is the intelligence-agnostic HIL thesis taken to its logical conclusion. The bar is capability, not species. The measurement is empirical, not philosophical.

## A Note on Priority

The founder observed that this is what he is building. Not a bench test, not a framework, not a white paper. A new kind of analytical intelligence. The bench test measures whether it works. The framework defines how it works. The white paper describes why it should work. But the thing itself — the synthetic domain expert — is the product. Everything else is infrastructure.

Whether this is the first such system is a claim that requires verification against the current literature. The individual components are known. The specific integration may be novel. What is certainly novel is the empirical measurement apparatus — the decay curve framework and the verification scoring — that makes it possible to test the claim rather than merely assert it.
