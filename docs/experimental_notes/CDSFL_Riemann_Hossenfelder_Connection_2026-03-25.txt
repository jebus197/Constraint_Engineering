# CDSFL Bench Task Proposal — The Riemann Hypothesis, Hossenfelder, and the Question of Deeper Structure

**25 March 2026**

---

## The Problem

The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part equal to one half. They all lie on a single vertical line in the complex plane, called the critical line. This has been verified computationally for over 10 trillion zeros. None deviate. But no one has proven it must always be true.

The zeros of the zeta function control the distribution of prime numbers. Each zero contributes a wavelike correction to the smooth logarithmic estimate that Gauss first identified. If all zeros lie on the critical line, the prime counting function is as well-behaved as possible. If even one zero drifts off the line, it introduces chaotic, unpredictable corrections to the distribution of primes.

The hypothesis has been open for over 160 years. It is number eight on Hilbert's list. It carries a million dollar Clay Mathematics Institute prize. It is widely considered the most important unsolved problem in mathematics.

---

## The Quantum Connection

In the 1970s, mathematician Hugh Montgomery was describing the statistical spacing between zeros of the zeta function. Physicist Freeman Dyson immediately recognised the formula. It was identical to the statistics governing energy levels in quantum chaotic systems — specifically the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble from random matrix theory, originally developed for nuclear physics.

This means the zeros of the zeta function — abstract mathematical objects controlling prime number distribution — behave statistically identically to the energy levels of a physical quantum system. The spacing between consecutive zeros follows the same distribution as the spacing between energy levels of heavy atomic nuclei.

Physicist Alain Connes pursued this further, proposing that the zeros ARE literally the energy levels of an undiscovered quantum system. He built a mathematical framework using structures called adeles to give primes a physical state space. He did not prove the hypothesis, but showed that the spectral viewpoint is mathematically precise.

In the 1990s, John Keating and Nina Snaith used random matrix theory to predict detailed statistical properties of the zeta function with striking accuracy. Patterns that had resisted pure number theory for decades emerged naturally from a physics-inspired model.

## The Hossenfelder Connection

The founder's 26th bench test task concerns Hossenfelder's hypothesis that quantum mechanics is a statistical theory, analogous to thermodynamics. She argues that the Schrödinger equation resembles the Liouville equation from classical statistical mechanics because quantum mechanics IS statistical mechanics, describing averages of a deeper deterministic layer.

If Hossenfelder is right, there is a deeper structure beneath quantum mechanics. If the Riemann zeros genuinely correspond to energy levels of a quantum system, and that quantum system is itself a statistical description of something deeper still, then the Riemann Hypothesis may be telling us something about the deepest layer of physical reality — one that we have not yet identified.

The connection is not proven. It is speculative. But the two observations point in the same direction: the primes appear to encode the same mathematical structure as quantum energy levels, and quantum mechanics may itself be a statistical average of a deeper theory. If both are true, the primes may be encoding the structure of whatever lies beneath quantum mechanics.

## What This Means for the Bench Test

The founder proposes adding this as a **27th frontier task** — not replacing an existing one. The 26 existing tasks test the CDSFL methodology across established problem domains. This task tests something different: whether structured methodology, applied by multiple AI models working collaboratively, can contribute anything meaningful to a 160-year-old open problem in mathematics.

The expectation is not that the models will solve the Riemann Hypothesis. The expectation is that the CDSFL framework — with its structured falsification, SymPy verification, multi-model confer, and expert guidance — will produce a more rigorous and more carefully qualified analysis of the problem than any single model operating without structure.

**The task would ask the models to do the following:**

**Part 1 — State the Riemann Hypothesis precisely.** Define the zeta function, the critical strip, the critical line, the trivial and non-trivial zeros. Verify the definitions with SymPy where possible.

**Part 2 — Explain the Montgomery-Odlyzko connection.** Why do the zeros of the zeta function follow the same statistical distribution as energy levels in quantum chaotic systems? What does this imply about the relationship between number theory and physics? Is this a coincidence, a deep truth, or an artefact of the mathematical formalism?

**Part 3 — Evaluate Hossenfelder's hypothesis in this context.** If quantum mechanics is statistical, and the Riemann zeros correspond to quantum energy levels, what would this mean for the structure underlying the primes? Is there a falsifiable prediction that connects these two ideas?

**Part 4 — Identify what a proof strategy might look like.** Not produce a proof, but identify what KIND of proof might work. The 2024 Maynard-Guth breakthrough tightened bounds on how far zeros can stray from the critical line. What does this approach tell us about what a full proof might require? Is the spectral (physics-based) approach or the analytic (pure mathematics) approach more promising?

**Part 5 — State clearly what is proven, what is conjectured, what is speculative, and what is unknown.** Use the CDSFL HARD/SOFT/SPECULATIVE classification throughout.

## Why This Matters

The standard paradigm says you need extraordinarily powerful specialist machines and decades of focused human expertise to make progress on problems of this magnitude. The CDSFL hypothesis says you also need **methodology** — not instead of expertise and compute, but in addition to them.

If structured methodology produces even a marginally more rigorous analysis of this problem than unstructured single-model review, that is evidence for the value of methodology at the frontier.

The founder's deeper question — whether the Riemann zeros and Hossenfelder's deeper-than-quantum statistical layer are somehow related — is genuinely speculative. It may be wrong. But it is **falsifiable**: if the statistical properties of the Riemann zeros can be derived from a specific sub-quantum model, the connection is real. If they cannot, it is coincidence. Either result advances understanding.

The question of whether good methodology might matter as much as raw compute for foundational problems is itself a thesis of the CDSFL project. This task tests that thesis at the hardest possible level.

## On Whether This Should Be Task 27 or Replace an Existing Task

It should be task 27. The existing 26 tasks test the methodology across a range of established domains and difficulty levels. This task tests something qualitatively different — the methodology's ability to contribute at the absolute frontier of human knowledge.

Removing an existing task would weaken the breadth of the benchmark. Adding this task extends it into territory where the outcome is genuinely unknown, which is where science is supposed to operate.

The task should be clearly marked as **exploratory**. No claim is made that the models will produce novel results. The claim is that structured methodology will produce a more rigorous and more honest analysis than unstructured review. The decay curves, verification scores, and finding classifications will show whether this is true.
