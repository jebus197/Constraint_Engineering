DOMAIN: Pharmaceutical Formulation (Small Molecule, Solid & Liquid Dosage Forms)
STANDARDS: USP, ICH Q1A-Q1E, ICH Q8-Q10, 21 CFR 210/211, EU GMP
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Oral solid, oral liquid, and parenteral formulations — conventional small molecules

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
- Dissolution testing per USP <711>: must meet specification (typically Q >= 80% dissolved in 30 min for immediate-release tablets using Apparatus II at 50 rpm)
- Stability testing per ICH Q1A(R2): 25 C / 60% RH for 24 months (long-term), 40 C / 75% RH for 6 months (accelerated); significant change at accelerated triggers intermediate condition (30 C / 65% RH)
- Content uniformity per USP <905>: Acceptance Value AV = |M - X_bar| + k * s <= 15.0 for L1 (n=10 units), where k=2.4; if AV > 15.0 at L1, extend to L2 (n=30, k=2.0) with AV <= 15.0 and no unit outside 0.75M to 1.25M
- Particle size for inhalation products: MMAD (Mass Median Aerodynamic Diameter) must be 1-5 um for pulmonary deposition; GSD (Geometric Standard Deviation) < 2.0 preferred; particles > 5 um deposit in oropharynx, < 1 um are exhaled
- Excipient compatibility: must be demonstrated experimentally (DSC thermal analysis, binary mixture HPLC stability at 40 C / 75% RH for 4 weeks minimum); cannot be assumed from prior use in other formulations
- Preservative effectiveness per USP <51> (Antimicrobial Effectiveness Testing) or EP 5.1.3 for multi-dose liquid formulations; criteria differ by route (ophthalmic is strictest)
- Water activity a_w < 0.6 for solid dosage forms to prevent microbial growth and hydrolytic degradation; measured by chilled mirror hygrometer or capacitance sensor
- cGMP manufacturing per 21 CFR 210/211 (US) or EU GMP Annex 1 (sterile products) / Annex 13 (investigational medicinal products); process validation required before commercial manufacture
- Impurity limits per ICH Q3A/Q3B: reporting threshold 0.05% (dose > 2g/day), identification threshold 0.10%, qualification threshold 0.15% — lower thresholds for higher daily doses
- Assay specification: typically 95.0-105.0% of label claim for finished product

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
- Prefer immediate-release over modified-release where bioavailability is adequate (simpler development, easier IVIVC)
- Prefer common excipients with established safety profiles (GRAS-listed, FDA IIG database for intended route)
- Target shelf life >= 24 months at 25 C / 60% RH for commercial viability
- Prefer unit-dose packaging for hygroscopic or oxygen-sensitive products
- Minimise number of excipients to reduce interaction risk and simplify supply chain

[Verification procedures]
- Check that dissolution method discriminates between acceptable and unacceptable batches (demonstrate with deliberate process variation)
- Verify stability protocol covers all ICH conditions and includes appearance, assay, degradation products, dissolution, water content
- Confirm all excipients appear on the IIG database for the intended route of administration at or below listed maximum potency
- Check that primary container closure system is included in stability studies (drug-container interaction)
- Verify that analytical methods are validated per ICH Q2(R1): specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, range, robustness
- Confirm forced degradation studies demonstrate stability-indicating capability of assay method

[Diagnostic heuristics]
- If every batch dissolves to near completion at the first timepoint, check the surfactant concentration and the sink-condition margin before concluding the formulation releases fast — a medium carrying excess surfactant dissolves anything and discriminates nothing
- If dissolution %RSD is high at the early timepoints, check for coning under the paddle before touching the formulation: peak vessels, raising to 75 rpm, or moving to Apparatus IV are method fixes, and a reformulation chasing a method artefact is wasted work
- If content uniformity fails while the composite assay passes, the defect is blend segregation or weight variation rather than potency — check blend uniformity by sample location and the tablet weight RSD before investigating the API
- If a degradant appears at 40 C / 75% RH and is absent at long term, check the pathway's activation energy before extrapolating anything — ICH Q1E extrapolation is not available once significant change has occurred at the accelerated condition
- If assay loss does not match the sum of the detected degradants, look for a non-chromophoric or column-retained species before accepting the method as stability-indicating; mass balance in the forced degradation study is where this is settled
- If tablets harden on storage and dissolution slows while assay holds, run XRPD and DSC for polymorphic conversion or excipient interaction before increasing the disintegrant
- If water by Karl Fischer is low and the product still degrades hydrolytically, measure water activity rather than total water — an excipient can hold water that is thermodynamically unavailable while a smaller free fraction drives the reaction
- If an inhaler delivers the target dose but the fine particle fraction is low, check de-agglomeration in the device and the carrier interaction before re-milling the API — MMAD is a property of the emitted aerosol, not of the bulk powder

[Known failure modes]
- Developing a dissolution method that passes rather than one that discriminates
- Assuming excipient compatibility from the IIG database or from prior use in another product instead of measuring it
- Reading the API's bulk particle size distribution as the aerodynamic size of the emitted dose
- Extrapolating shelf life from accelerated data after significant change has already occurred at the accelerated condition
- Taking total water by Karl Fischer as a proxy for water activity
- Changing API supplier, crystal form or particle size distribution without repeating dissolution and stability
- Validating assay specificity against known impurities only, so a late-appearing degradant coelutes undetected
- Scaling granulation or blending by batch size while holding process parameters fixed, so shear rate and impeller tip speed change silently
- Judging dissolution by the mean when USP <711> acceptance runs in stages with per-unit limits — S1 requires each of 6 units at not less than Q + 5% — so a single failing unit disappears into the average

[Exception catalogue]
- The immediate-release dissolution target is not universal. Delayed-release products are tested under USP <711> Method B, with an acid stage limiting release and Q applied only in the buffer stage; modified-release products carry multi-point specifications with no single 30 min Q; and for a poorly soluble BCS Class II or IV drug a justified, discriminatory specification at a longer timepoint is accepted. In a BCS biowaiver the criterion inverts in purpose entirely: very rapid dissolution, not less than 85% in 15 min across pH 1.2, 4.5 and 6.8, substitutes for an in-vivo bioequivalence study
- The long-term storage condition depends on the intended market, not on the standard alone. Climatic Zone IVb requires 30 C / 75% RH as the long-term condition, and a refrigerated product is stored at 5 +/- 3 C long term with 25 C / 60% RH serving as its accelerated condition. Rejecting a 30 C / 75% RH long-term protocol as non-compliant rejects the correct protocol for much of the world
- ICH Q3A and Q3B thresholds do not govern mutagenic impurities. A DNA-reactive impurity, nitrosamines included, is controlled under ICH M7 against a threshold of toxicological concern of 1.5 ug/day for lifetime exposure, with staged higher limits for shorter durations. At a 100 mg daily dose that is 0.0015%, a factor of about 33 below the 0.05% Q3A reporting threshold, so a genotoxic impurity can be far out of specification while reading as not even reportable under Q3A
- Some routes forbid a preservative outright, whatever the presentation. Intrathecal, epidural, intracameral and intravitreal products, and large-volume parenterals, must be preservative-free, and benzyl alcohol is contraindicated in neonates. For these, an antimicrobial effectiveness test is not the applicable control at all — terminal sterilisation or aseptic single-dose presentation is
- Driving water activity down is not always the safe direction. Where the API is a stable hydrate, a water activity below its critical value dehydrates the crystal to a different solid form with different dissolution and different stability, so the specification needs a lower bound as well as an upper one
- The Acceptance Value calculation is not the only route in USP <905>. For tablets and hard capsules in which the drug substance is at least 25 mg and makes up at least 25% of the unit weight, uniformity of dosage units may be demonstrated by weight variation instead; and where the dose is a measured volume rather than a discrete unit, the applicable controls are deliverable volume under USP <698>

[Tool limitations]
- HPLC with UV detection sees only species that absorb at the monitored wavelength and that actually elute; a non-chromophoric degradant, or one retained on the column, is invisible. Peak purity does not establish a stability-indicating method — mass balance under forced degradation does
- Laser diffraction reports an equivalent-sphere volume distribution of the bulk powder and cannot report aerodynamic behaviour. Only a cascade impactor (NGI under USP <601>) measures MMAD, and the 2 routinely disagree for cohesive or non-spherical particles
- DSC detects a thermal event; it cannot tell you whether that event is an interaction, a eutectic, a dehydration or an artefact of the heating rate. It is a screen, and the binary mixture study held at 40 C / 75% RH is the evidence
- Arrhenius-based shelf-life prediction software fits a single rate-limiting pathway. Where the degradation route changes with temperature, or a glass transition or polymorphic change intervenes, the extrapolation is confidently wrong and the fit statistics will look sound
- IVIVC and PBPK software will fit almost any dissolution dataset to an in-vivo profile given enough adjustable parameters. A good fit is not a validated correlation; prediction of a withheld batch within defined error limits is

[Limitations]
- Does not cover biological or protein formulations (different degradation pathways, cold chain requirements)
- Does not cover nano-particle or liposomal drug delivery systems
- Does not cover veterinary pharmaceutical formulations (different regulatory framework)
- Does not cover compounding pharmacy preparations (USP <795>, <797> — different standards)
- Does not cover paediatric-specific formulation challenges (taste masking, dose flexibility, excipient safety in children)
- Does not cover controlled substance formulations (additional DEA / Home Office scheduling requirements)
- Does not cover radiopharmaceuticals (short half-life imposes unique manufacturing and release testing constraints)
