[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Allometric scaling: physiological rates scale with body mass to a non-unit
  power, so a per-kilogram dose or rate extrapolated across a large mass
  difference is wrong by a factor that grows with the ratio. Linear
  per-kilogram scaling across species is the standard error.

Species differences are not quantitative adjustments. A pathway present in one
  species may be absent in another, and extrapolation requires the pathway to
  be established, not assumed.

Welfare constraints are HARD where a regime governs the work, and the regime
  must be named. Treating an unnamed regime as absent is not a neutral
  default.

Behavioural observation requires a stated protocol: the sampling rule, the
  observation window and the operational definition of each behaviour. Without
  them, 2 observers measure different things.

Observer effects are real. The presence of an observer changes behaviour, and
  a design that does not account for it measures the observation.

Individual identification must be reliable before any repeated-measures claim.
  Misidentification turns a within-individual comparison into a between-
  individual one silently.

Sample independence: littermates, cage-mates and animals sharing an enclosure
  are not independent, and this is the dominant source of overstated
  confidence in the field.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer reporting both the absolute and the mass-specific quantity.
Prefer blind scoring of behaviour where the hypothesis is known to the scorer.
State the housing, diet and light cycle; most physiological measures depend on
  all 3.
Prefer non-invasive measurement where it answers the question.
Prefer reporting individual trajectories alongside group means.

[Verification procedures]

pint for every dose, rate and concentration, and an explicit allometric
  exponent rather than an implicit linear scale.
scipy.stats and statsmodels for the analysis, with the clustering unit stated
  and the interval computed on the effective sample size.
Inter-observer agreement computed and reported for any behavioural measure,
  because a behavioural metric is bounded by its own reliability.
Check every physiological value against a species-specific range by execution.
For repeated measures, verify identification by execution against the
  identification record, not by assumption.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a cross-species dose scales linearly with mass, it is probably wrong; check
  the allometric exponent.
If inter-observer agreement is unreported, the behavioural result has an
  unknown ceiling.
If variance between cages exceeds variance within them, the cage is the unit.
If a behaviour appears only when observed, the observation is the treatment.
If a physiological value matches the human range closely, check whether a
  human reference range was used by default.

[Known failure modes]

Linear per-kilogram cross-species extrapolation.
Pseudoreplication at the cage, litter or enclosure level.
Unblinded behavioural scoring.
Applying a human or model-species reference range to another species.
Ignoring circadian and seasonal effects on physiological measures.
Treating a welfare regime as absent because it was not named.
Misidentifying individuals in a repeated-measures design.

[Exception catalogue]

Hibernation, torpor, diapause and diving produce physiological values far
  outside the active range, and a plausibility check calibrated on active
  animals will refuse correct data.
Some species have no established reference ranges, and the honest report is
  that none exists.
Social species housed singly are stressed by the housing, so a single-housed
  control is not a neutral baseline.
Neonates and juveniles differ qualitatively, not merely in scale.

[Tool limitations]

Statistical libraries will analyse pseudoreplicated data without complaint.
pint cannot catch a wrong allometric exponent, because the expression remains
  dimensionally consistent.
Automated behaviour-tracking software has systematic failure modes by species
  and lighting, and its error rate must be measured rather than assumed.
No tool can adjudicate a welfare judgement.
