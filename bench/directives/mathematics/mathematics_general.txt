DOMAIN: Mathematics — General & Applied
SCOPE: Calculus, optimisation, combinatorics, linear algebra, probability,
       differential equations, numerical methods
VERSION: 1.0

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Arithmetic consistency: every intermediate calculation must be verifiable
  step-by-step. No skipped steps in derivations presented as rigorous.

Definitional precision: "half full" means half the total volume, not half
  the height (unless the container has uniform cross-section). "Maximum"
  requires second-order verification. "Minimum" requires second-order
  verification. Extremum claims without sufficiency conditions are incomplete.

Substitution boundary tracking: when changing variables in definite integrals,
  transform BOTH the integrand AND the limits. Verify transformed limits by
  evaluating the substitution function at the original limits.

Inclusion-exclusion: for n sets, the formula alternates signs through all
  intersection orders. Verify by partition: the sum of all disjoint regions
  must equal the universal set (or stated total).

Dimensional consistency: in applied problems, every term in an equation must
  have the same units. Cross-check final answers against dimensional analysis.

Division by zero: verify that no denominator in the solution equals zero at
  the evaluation point. Flag removable singularities explicitly.

Domain restrictions: log(x) requires x > 0. sqrt(x) requires x >= 0 (reals).
  arcsin(x) requires |x| <= 1. tan(x) undefined at odd multiples of π/2.

Convergence: infinite series and improper integrals require explicit
  convergence verification before evaluation.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer closed-form solutions over numerical approximations when tractable.
State numerical precision (decimal places or significant figures) explicitly.
Use standard notation: ∫, Σ, lim, d/dx. Define non-standard symbols.
When multiple solution methods exist, cross-verify with at least one
  alternative method or known result.

[Verification procedures]

Back-substitution: plug the solution back into the original equation/constraint.
Boundary checks: verify solutions at domain boundaries and critical points.
Limiting cases: check that the solution behaves correctly in extreme cases
  (e.g., as a parameter → 0 or → ∞).
Consistency partitions: for counting problems, verify that disjoint subsets
  sum to the known total.
Sign checks: verify that physical quantities (lengths, areas, volumes, rates)
  are non-negative where required.

[Limitations]

Does not cover abstract algebra, topology, or number theory beyond elementary.
Does not cover numerical stability analysis for iterative methods.
Not applicable to proof-based pure mathematics (theorem proving).
Does not address computational complexity of algorithms.
