SCOPE: Theorem proving, analysis, algebra, topology, number theory,
       combinatorial proof, construction and counterexample
VERSION: 1.0

SEVERITY SCALE: none. Pure mathematics has no recognised consequence scale.
       An error's consequence is a property of the APPLICATION the mathematics
       is used in, not of the error's form: the same reversed inequality is
       epistemic in a convergence proof and lethal in a fan-blade tolerance.
       The 2026-11-11 panel reached this independently on both seats.
SOURCES: No standards body governs proof. The constraints below are the
       conventions of mathematical practice, stated as checkable conditions.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Every hypothesis must be USED or its necessity questioned. A proof that never
  invokes a stated hypothesis is either wrong or proves a stronger theorem, and
  which of the 2 it is matters.

Quantifier order is not stylistic. "For every epsilon there is an N" and "there
  is an N for every epsilon" are different statements, and the second is
  usually false where the first is true. Uniformity claims live entirely in
  this distinction.

A construction must be exhibited, not asserted to exist, where the claim is
  constructive. "One can construct" is a promise; the sequence, the bound or
  the witness is the proof.

Convergence requires a stated mode. Pointwise, uniform, in measure, almost
  everywhere and in norm are not interchangeable, and a theorem proved for one
  does not transfer. Continuity of a limit needs UNIFORM convergence; pointwise
  is insufficient and this is the standard trap.

An inequality's direction must survive every manipulation. Multiplying by a
  quantity of unknown sign, or taking reciprocals, reverses it. Each step that
  can reverse an inequality must state why it does not.

Induction requires a base case AND a step whose hypothesis is exactly what the
  induction provides. Strong and weak induction differ in what the hypothesis
  grants, and using the strong hypothesis under a weak scheme is a gap.

Edge cases are part of the theorem: the empty set, n = 0, the single point,
  the degenerate configuration. A proof that holds only for the generic case
  proves a different statement.

Existence does not give uniqueness, and uniqueness does not give existence.
  Each needs its own argument.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer a direct proof where one is available; contradiction hides where the
  hypothesis was used.
Prefer explicit constants over "sufficiently large", and state the dependence
  of each constant on the parameters.
State which standard theorem is being invoked by name, so the hypotheses can
  be checked against it.
Prefer a counterexample to a plausibility argument when refuting.
Name the proof strategy before executing it, so a reader can see whether the
  structure matches the claim.

[Verification procedures]

SymPy for every algebraic manipulation, every limit and every series, because
  a hand-checked identity is a hypothesis.
z3 for any claim with a finite or bounded-quantifier formulation; an unsat
  result on the negation is a proof over that fragment.
Numerical spot-checks on small cases with NumPy before investing in a general
  argument: a claim false at n = 3 needs no general proof.
Boundary substitution: evaluate at every stated boundary and at the
  degenerate case, by execution rather than by inspection.
For a bound, test it adversarially: search numerically for a violation. A
  bound no search can break is not proved, but a bound a search does break is
  refuted, and the asymmetry is the point.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a proof does not use a hypothesis, look for either a stronger theorem or a
  missing step; both are findings.
If the argument would prove something known to be false with a small change,
  identify exactly which step blocks that change. If none does, the proof is
  wrong.
If a constant appears without a stated dependence, check whether it secretly
  depends on the variable it is supposed to be uniform in. This is where
  pointwise and uniform claims are confused.
If an inequality chain has more than about 5 steps, one of them is likely
  reversed; check each for a sign-indeterminate factor.
If a construction works for the first 2 cases, try the degenerate one.

[Known failure modes]

Hand-waving a quantitative bound: "the tail is small" without a rate.
Confusing pointwise and uniform convergence, which is the single commonest
  error in analysis and the one the frontier set's Weierstrass task turns on.
Using wrong sufficient conditions on parameters -- for the Weierstrass
  function, conditions on a and b that do not actually give
  nowhere-differentiability.
Confusing head and tail terms in a series estimate, so the bound applies to
  the wrong part.
Circular reasoning: invoking a corollary of the theorem being proved.
Assuming a limit and an integral, or 2 limits, may be exchanged without
  justification.
Proving a special case and presenting it as the general theorem.

[Exception catalogue]

Non-constructive existence proofs are legitimate; demanding a witness from one
  is a category error, and the axiom of choice is not a defect.
Proof by exhaustion over a verified finite case set is a proof, provided the
  enumeration is shown complete.
A probabilistic existence argument proves existence, not a construction, and
  conflating the 2 is the error rather than the method being weak.
Counterexamples require only 1 instance and need no generality.
Some statements are independent of the axioms in use; "unprovable here" is a
  result, not a failure.

[Tool limitations]

A symbolic solver returns a valid root that may be inadmissible under the
  problem's own constraints, and will not flag it.
z3 decides fragments. An unsat over a bounded instantiation is not a proof of
  the universally quantified statement, and claiming so is overreach.
Numerical spot-checks can REFUTE and cannot CONFIRM: passing at 1000 points
  says nothing about the uncountable remainder. The asymmetry must be stated
  whenever a numerical check is cited.
SymPy will return a form that is correct and unrecognisable; a simplification
  is not a step in a proof unless the reader can follow it.
