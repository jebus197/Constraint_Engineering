DIRECTIVE: Automotive Components
DOMAIN: Product Engineering
SCOPE: Tier 1/2/3 supplier components for passenger vehicle integration
STANDARDS: IATF 16949, AIAG PPAP 4th ed., AIAG-VDA FMEA Handbook,
           AEC-Q100/Q200, ISO 16750, FMVSS 302, IMDS

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. IATF 16949 quality management system certification required for all automotive
   supply chain participants. ISO 9001 alone is not accepted by OEMs.
2. PPAP submission and customer approval required before mass production.
   18 elements including: design records, DFMEA, process flow diagram, PFMEA,
   control plan, MSA studies, dimensional results, material/performance test results,
   initial process study (Cpk >= 1.67 for special characteristics),
   qualified lab documentation, sample parts, master sample, PSW.
3. FMEA required per AIAG-VDA methodology: both DFMEA and PFMEA.
   Action Priority (AP) replaces RPN. AP = High: mandatory action with documented
   completion. AP = Medium: action with justified timeline. AP = Low: review required.
4. Material specification per OEM standard (VW TL, BMW GS, GM GMW, Ford WSS-M).
   Generic material grades ("ABS", "PA66") not acceptable without OEM qualification.
5. Environmental testing: temperature cycling per AEC-Q100 Grade 1 (-40 to +125 C)
   for electronics. Location-dependent ranges:
   Under-hood: -40 to +125 C. Cabin: -40 to +85 C. Trunk: -40 to +85 C.
6. Vibration testing: random profiles per OEM spec or ISO 16750-3. All resonance
   frequencies must be identified and addressed. Resonance amplification can exceed 10x.
7. Flammability: FMVSS 302 (or ISO 3795) for interior trim materials.
   Burn rate must not exceed 102 mm/min, or material must be self-extinguishing.
8. IMDS: full material composition declaration mandatory for all components.
   No "trade secret" blanket exemptions for restricted substances.
9. Change management: any change to material, process, sub-supplier, or manufacturing
   location requires customer notification and re-PPAP.
   Unapproved changes are a containment-level quality escape.
10. Traceability: safety-critical and special-characteristic parts must be traceable
    to raw material lot. Traceability records retained per OEM requirement (typically 15 years).

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
1. Prefer DFMA principles — reduce part count, minimise tool changes on assembly line.
2. Prefer standard fasteners to minimise tool count at OEM assembly stations.
3. Target 15-year / 200,000 km design life for structural and safety-relevant components.
4. Prefer suppliers with VDA 6.3 process audit scores >= 90% (grade A).
5. Prefer single-source avoidance — dual-source strategy for critical components.

[Verification procedures]
1. Check component temperature rating covers worst-case vehicle installation location.
   Engine bay is most severe; do not assume cabin conditions near the firewall.
2. Verify all materials declared in IMDS with complete substance data —
   incomplete entries block OEM release.
3. Confirm vibration testing covers correct axis orientation and frequency range
   for actual installation location and mounting method.
4. Check PPAP documentation complete for all special characteristics (CC/SC)
   on control plan and drawing.
5. Verify Cpk meets OEM requirement (typically >= 1.67 special, >= 1.33 standard).
6. Confirm DFMEA and PFMEA reflect current design/process revision.

[Diagnostic heuristics]
1. If a part passes component validation and fails on the vehicle, check the bracket
   and the harness routing before the part. ISO 16750-3 profiles are applied at the
   component's own fixing points, and a bracket resonance changes the spectrum the
   part actually sees.
2. If Cpk is high while the customer still reports variation, check whether the
   subgroups were rational. A Cpk from consecutive pieces within a single shot
   measures within-shot variation and excludes the shift-to-shift variation the
   customer experiences.
3. If a DFMEA produces no AP = High rows, check the severity ratings against the
   vehicle-level effect rather than the component-level effect. A loss of function the
   driver cannot detect at component level can still be severity 9 or 10 in the
   vehicle.
4. If a thermal failure appears in the field but not in the chamber, check junction
   temperature rather than ambient. AEC-Q100 Grade 1 rates ambient, and
   T_j = T_ambient + P * R_thja, so a Grade 1 part can exceed its own maximum junction
   temperature in a +125 C under-hood location.
5. If an IMDS submission is rejected with no substance named, check the material tree
   for unclosed nodes and for node weights that do not sum to the part weight. IMDS
   rejects on arithmetic and completeness before it rejects on content.
6. If a warranty return shows no fault found, check the terminal and connector before
   the electronics. Fretting corrosion at a tin-plated terminal under micro-motion is
   intermittent, recovers on disassembly, and is the dominant no-fault-found cause.
7. If a capability study passes on 30 pieces, check the sample plan against the PPAP
   requirement. An initial process study needs at least 25 subgroups or 100
   consecutive pieces, and a Cpk from 30 pieces carries a confidence interval wide
   enough to contain 1.0.
8. If a change was judged like-for-like and a field issue followed, check the
   sub-supplier and the manufacturing location before the drawing. Those 2 are the
   changes most often judged not to need notification, and both require re-PPAP.

[Known failure modes]
1. Severity rated at component level rather than at vehicle level.
2. Cpk computed from irrational subgroups, capturing within-shot and not
   between-shift variation.
3. An ambient temperature rating treated as a junction or surface temperature limit.
4. Vibration input applied on the wrong axis, or at the part rather than at its
   mounting interface.
5. Tin-plated terminals in a micro-motion joint, producing fretting corrosion and
   no-fault-found returns.
6. A sub-supplier or plant change released without customer notification and re-PPAP.
7. A generic material grade left on the drawing in place of the OEM specification.
8. Validation run on prototype tooling and reported as production-intent.
9. Traceability broken at a rework or sort operation, where lot identity is not
   carried forward.

[Exception catalogue]
1. A time-boxed and quantity-boxed deviation, or an interim approval PSW, legitimately
   authorises shipment before full PPAP approval. "No production without approved
   PPAP" is therefore not absolute, and the correct response to an urgent build is a
   deviation request with containment, not a refusal.
2. Lower AEC-Q100 grades are correct, not deficient, where the installation supports
   them. Grade 2 (-40 to +105 C) and Grade 3 (-40 to +85 C) suit cabin and luggage
   compartment electronics, and specifying Grade 1 everywhere buys cost with no
   reliability gain.
3. FMVSS 302 applies to materials within 13 mm of the occupant compartment air space.
   Components wholly outside that envelope — under-body, engine bay, sealed internal
   housings — are outside its scope, and the 102 mm/min burn rate is not a requirement
   for them.
4. Independent aftermarket and accessory parts not supplied into an OEM assembly plant
   fall outside IATF 16949 and need no PPAP. Demanding a PSW for an independent
   aftermarket part refuses correct work.
5. Cpk is not required, and often not computable, for every characteristic. Attribute
   characteristics, characteristics with a single specification limit bounded at 0, and
   error-proofed or 100 per cent inspected operations are accepted by alternative means
   in the control plan, and forcing a Cpk onto a pass/fail gauge produces a number with
   no referent.
6. A carry-over part already in production elsewhere can be released on PPAP-on-file
   where design, process, plant and sub-suppliers are all unchanged. A new submission
   is triggered by the change, not by the programme.

[Tool limitations]
1. A capability index describes a stable process. Cpk computed on an out-of-control
   process is arithmetically valid and meaningless, and no statistics package refuses
   to compute it.
2. FEA predicts stress for the mesh, material model and boundary conditions supplied.
   It does not predict the weld line, knit line or fibre orientation that actually sets
   the strength of a moulded or welded part.
3. Accelerated life models — Arrhenius, Coffin-Manson — assume 1 dominant mechanism
   with a known activation energy. Where the field failure is a different mechanism,
   the acceleration factor is a number with no physical referent.
4. IMDS validates tree arithmetic and flags declarable substances against GADSL. It
   cannot verify that the declared composition matches the material actually used,
   which only analytical testing establishes.
5. A pre-compliance EMC scan with a near-field probe shows relative change only. A
   pass against a limit line requires the standard's measurement distance, antenna and
   accredited site.

[Limitations]
1. Does not cover powertrain (engine, transmission, exhaust) — additional durability applies.
2. Does not cover EV battery systems (UN ECE R100, UL 2580) or HV components (>60V DC).
3. Does not cover ADAS/autonomous driving (ISO 26262 ASIL classification).
4. Does not cover exterior lighting (ECE R48 series) or glazing (ECE R43).
5. OEM-specific CSR requirements vary significantly and override generic standards.
