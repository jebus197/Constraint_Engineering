DIRECTIVE: Automotive Components
DOMAIN: Product Engineering
SCOPE: Tier 1/2/3 supplier components for passenger vehicle integration
STANDARDS: IATF 16949, AIAG PPAP 4th ed., AIAG-VDA FMEA Handbook,
           AEC-Q100/Q200, ISO 16750, FMVSS 302, IMDS

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. IATF 16949 quality management system certification required for all automotive
   supply chain participants. ISO 9001 alone is not accepted by OEMs.
2. PPAP submission and customer approval required before mass production.
   18 elements including: design records, DFMEA, process flow diagram, PFMEA,
   control plan, MSA studies, dimensional results, material/performance test results,
   initial process study (Cpk >= 1.67 for special characteristics),
   qualified lab documentation, sample parts, master sample, PSW.
3. FMEA required per AIAG-VDA methodology: both DFMEA and PFMEA.
   Action Priority (AP) replaces RPN. AP = High: mandatory action with documented
   completion. AP = Medium: action with justified timeline. AP = Low: review required.
4. Material specification per OEM standard (VW TL, BMW GS, GM GMW, Ford WSS-M).
   Generic material grades ("ABS", "PA66") not acceptable without OEM qualification.
5. Environmental testing: temperature cycling per AEC-Q100 Grade 1 (-40 to +125 C)
   for electronics. Location-dependent ranges:
   Under-hood: -40 to +125 C. Cabin: -40 to +85 C. Trunk: -40 to +85 C.
6. Vibration testing: random profiles per OEM spec or ISO 16750-3. All resonance
   frequencies must be identified and addressed. Resonance amplification can exceed 10x.
7. Flammability: FMVSS 302 (or ISO 3795) for interior trim materials.
   Burn rate must not exceed 102 mm/min, or material must be self-extinguishing.
8. IMDS: full material composition declaration mandatory for all components.
   No "trade secret" blanket exemptions for restricted substances.
9. Change management: any change to material, process, sub-supplier, or manufacturing
   location requires customer notification and re-PPAP.
   Unapproved changes are a containment-level quality escape.
10. Traceability: safety-critical and special-characteristic parts must be traceable
    to raw material lot. Traceability records retained per OEM requirement (typically 15 years).

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
1. Prefer DFMA principles — reduce part count, minimise tool changes on assembly line.
2. Prefer standard fasteners to minimise tool count at OEM assembly stations.
3. Target 15-year / 200,000 km design life for structural and safety-relevant components.
4. Prefer suppliers with VDA 6.3 process audit scores >= 90% (grade A).
5. Prefer single-source avoidance — dual-source strategy for critical components.

[Verification procedures]
1. Check component temperature rating covers worst-case vehicle installation location.
   Engine bay is most severe; do not assume cabin conditions near the firewall.
2. Verify all materials declared in IMDS with complete substance data —
   incomplete entries block OEM release.
3. Confirm vibration testing covers correct axis orientation and frequency range
   for actual installation location and mounting method.
4. Check PPAP documentation complete for all special characteristics (CC/SC)
   on control plan and drawing.
5. Verify Cpk meets OEM requirement (typically >= 1.67 special, >= 1.33 standard).
6. Confirm DFMEA and PFMEA reflect current design/process revision.

[Limitations]
1. Does not cover powertrain (engine, transmission, exhaust) — additional durability applies.
2. Does not cover EV battery systems (UN ECE R100, UL 2580) or HV components (>60V DC).
3. Does not cover ADAS/autonomous driving (ISO 26262 ASIL classification).
4. Does not cover exterior lighting (ECE R48 series) or glazing (ECE R43).
5. OEM-specific CSR requirements vary significantly and override generic standards.
