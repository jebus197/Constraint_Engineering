DIRECTIVE: Consumer Products — CE/UL Compliance
DOMAIN: Product Engineering
SCOPE: Consumer electronics and electrical products for EU and North American markets
STANDARDS: LVD 2014/35/EU, EMC 2014/30/EU, RoHS 2011/65/EU, REACH EC 1907/2006,
           IEC 62368-1, UN 38.3, IEC 62133-2, IEC 60529

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. CE marking mandatory for EU market: product must comply with ALL applicable directives
   (LVD 2014/35/EU, EMC 2014/30/EU, RoHS 2011/65/EU, WEEE, RED 2014/53/EU if wireless).
   Missing a single applicable directive invalidates the CE mark entirely.
2. UL/CSA listing required for North American market — product must be tested and certified
   by an NRTL (Nationally Recognized Testing Lab) to the applicable standard.
3. RoHS compliance in homogeneous materials:
   Pb <= 1000 ppm, Cd <= 100 ppm, Hg <= 1000 ppm, Cr(VI) <= 1000 ppm,
   PBB <= 1000 ppm, PBDE <= 1000 ppm.
   Four phthalates (DEHP, BBP, DBP, DIBP) each <= 1000 ppm per 2015/863/EU amendment.
4. REACH: any SVHC present at > 0.1% w/w in any article must be declared to ECHA
   and communicated down the supply chain. Candidate list updates biannually.
5. IEC 62368-1 replaces IEC 60065 and IEC 60950-1 for audio/video/IT equipment safety.
   Hazard-based safety engineering: classify energy sources (ES1/ES2/ES3), apply safeguards.
6. Drop test per IEC 60068-2-31 or product-specific standard: product must survive specified
   drop height onto specified surface without creating a safety hazard.
7. Battery safety: UN 38.3 transport testing mandatory for all lithium cells and batteries
   (8 tests: altitude, thermal, shock, short circuit, impact, overcharge, forced discharge,
   external fire). IEC 62133-2 for lithium battery safety in the finished product.
8. Product labelling: manufacturer name and registered address, CE mark (min 5 mm height,
   correct proportions per EU regulation), power ratings, safety warnings in official
   language(s) of each target market.
9. Declaration of Conformity (DoC) must exist per product type — a legal document signed by
   the manufacturer or authorised representative, listing all applicable directives and
   harmonised standards applied. Must be available to market surveillance authorities on request.
10. EMC: product must not cause electromagnetic interference exceeding limits (emissions)
    and must operate correctly in the presence of expected interference (immunity).
    Radiated emissions limits per CISPR 32: Class B (residential) tighter than Class A.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
1. Prefer IP54 minimum for outdoor/wet-environment products (IEC 60529).
2. Design for disassembly in anticipation of EU ESPR requirements.
3. Target product design lifetime >= 1.5x warranty period.
4. Prefer recyclable or mono-material packaging for EPR obligations.
5. Prefer third-party verified recycled content declarations for plastics.

[Verification procedures]
1. Confirm all applicable directives identified for every target market —
   missed directives are the most common compliance failure.
2. Verify material declarations (RoHS/REACH) from all suppliers.
   Self-declarations alone insufficient for high-risk materials — request third-party reports.
3. Check test reports cover actual mass-production configuration, not prototype.
4. Confirm labelling per market: language, symbols, font size, durability over product life.
5. Verify DoC lists correct edition of each harmonised standard —
   citing a withdrawn standard invalidates the presumption of conformity.
6. Confirm EMC test setup matches intended installation conditions
   (cable lengths, peripheral configuration, operating mode).

[Limitations]
1. Does not cover toys (EN 71), cosmetics, or food-contact materials.
2. Does not cover machinery (2006/42/EC) or PPE (EU 2016/425).
3. Does not cover medical devices (MDR 2017/745) or IVDs (IVDR 2017/746).
4. Does not address requirements outside EU and North America (CCC, BIS, KC, PSE, etc.).
5. REACH candidate list and RoHS exemptions are time-dependent.
