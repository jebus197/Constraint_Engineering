DOMAIN: Chemistry — Process Chemistry / Scale-Up
CODES: ATEX 2014/34/EU, DSEAR 2002, ASME BPV VIII, PED 2014/68/EU, DIERS
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Batch/semi-batch organic synthesis, 1 L to 10 m3, non-cryogenic (>-78C),
       non-polymerisation, conventional solvents

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Exothermic reactions require semi-batch operation with metered addition.
  Bulk-addition removes dose control — the sole safeguard against runaway.
Heat removal must exceed heat generation at maximum rate:
  Q_removal = U * A * dT_lm >= Q_rxn,max = r_max * (-dH_rxn) * V
  U = overall HTC (W/m2K), A = wetted jacket area, dT_lm = log-mean delta-T.
Adiabatic temperature rise per exothermic step:
  dT_ad = (-dH_rxn * C_0) / (rho * C_p)
  dT_ad > 50 K = high hazard (Stoessel criticality >= 3); requires independent
  secondary protection (bursting disc, quench system).
Scale-up of mixing — geometric similarity does NOT preserve mixing regime:
  Re = (N * D^2 * rho) / mu; constant tip speed gives decreasing Re/V.
  Constant P/V = N_p * rho * N^3 * D^5 / V gives different blend times.
  Mixing-sensitive reactions require CFD or empirical correlation at each scale.
Gas evolution rate must not exceed condenser/scrubber capacity:
  V_gas(STP) = n_gas * R * T / P. Vent sizing per DIERS two-phase methodology.
Solvent flash point vs process temperature: >= 10C margin (T_proc < T_flash - 10),
  or inert atmosphere mandatory (N2 blanket, O2 < 5 vol%). Ref: NFPA 30.
ATEX/DSEAR zone classification mandatory for flammable solvents (FP < 60C).
  Equipment must carry Ex rating for the classified zone (0, 1, or 2).
MAWP of reactor must exceed maximum expected pressure including runaway:
  P_max,runaway <= MAWP / 1.5. If exceeded, relief sizing per API 520/521.
Solvent-material compatibility: HCl corrodes SS; HF attacks glass lining;
  strong bases degrade borosilicate. Ref: DECHEMA corrosion tables.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer continuous flow for highly exothermic reactions (dT_ad > 100 K).
Prefer aqueous or bio-derived solvents where performance allows.
Minimise isolation steps (filtration, drying) — yield loss + exposure risk.
Target E-factor < 25 (fine chemicals), < 100 (pharma) per Sheldon.

[Verification procedures]

Calculate dT_ad for every exothermic step; flag any > 50 K.
Verify heat transfer area sufficient at scale — recalculate U with fouling.
Check vent sizing for worst-case gas evolution (DIERS two-phase flow).
Confirm solvent-material compatibility vs pH, temperature, halide content.
Verify mixing: blend time < 5% of reaction half-life for kinetic control.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If the batch runs hotter at scale on the same jacket setpoint, check A/V before
  suspecting U. Geometric scale-up from 1 L to 1000 L multiplies volume by 1000
  and jacket area by about 100, so specific cooling falls roughly 10-fold and
  chemistry that was isothermal in glass approaches adiabatic in steel.
If the reaction mass stops following the jacket, check wall fouling and the
  agitator's actual power draw before concluding the reaction has finished. Lost
  U and lost mixing both present as a reaction that has stopped.
If an exotherm arrives late in a metered addition rather than at the start, stop
  the dose. An induction period has been accumulating reagent, and the
  unreleased heat is already in the vessel.
If calorimetry and the observed temperature rise disagree, check whether the
  measurement used the real reagent order, concentration and dosing profile. A
  DSC pan gives a decomposition onset, not the accumulation that a slow dose
  creates; reaction calorimetry run on the actual profile is the number that
  bears on dT_ad.
If a step is slower at scale at the same temperature and concentration, check
  the mixing regime and the interfacial area before suspecting a reagent lot. A
  mass-transfer-limited rate is a function of the stirrer, not of the kinetics.
If a quench overshoots on pH or temperature at scale but never did in glass,
  check where the addition enters relative to the impeller. A 10 m3 vessel can
  hold a local composition for tens of seconds that the bulk never sees.
If a solid fouls the vessel at scale and not in the laboratory, check the WALL
  temperature rather than the bulk. Crystallisation starts at the coldest
  surface, and a large jacket delta-T puts that surface well below the measured
  batch temperature.
If a solvent or mass balance closes above 100.0%, suspect a recovered or
  recycled stream counted both as an input and as a recovery before crediting
  the yield.

[Known failure modes]

Setting the addition rate in proportion to volume rather than to the achievable
  heat removal, so the dose out-runs the reaction at scale and the reagent
  accumulates.
Taking a DSC onset as a safe operating ceiling. Onset is scan-rate dependent,
  and the temperature at which time to maximum rate is 24 hours can sit 50 K
  below it.
Using the jacket's nameplate duty instead of the dT_lm achievable at process
  temperature. A jacket served by a -10C chiller removes very little heat from a
  mass held at 0C.
Resting the safety case on the agitator running, with no independent basis for
  that assumption. Loss of agitation with accumulated reagent is the canonical
  runaway.
Judging inerting by nitrogen flow rather than by measured headspace oxygen.
Sizing the condenser for the stoichiometric gas and ignoring the solvent vapour
  carried with it.
Treating filtration and drying as scale-neutral. Cake resistance and drying time
  scale with bed depth, not with volume, and this is where campaign time is
  actually spent.
Distilling to the same pot temperature at scale, where the longer time at
  temperature degrades a thermally labile product that survived a laboratory
  rotary evaporator.
Omitting heel volumes, line rinses and hold times from the mass and energy
  balance, then finding the cycle does not fit the vessel or the shift pattern.

[Exception catalogue]

Metered addition is not always the safer mode, and mandating it can create the
  hazard it is meant to prevent. Dosing a reagent into a mass held at a
  temperature where the reaction does not proceed accumulates the full charge
  with no dose control at all; for such chemistry the correct mode is an all-in
  charge followed by a controlled ramp, so heat release tracks the temperature
  the operator holds. Dose control is a safeguard only where consumption keeps
  pace with addition.
At reflux the jacket calculation does not govern. The solvent's latent heat is
  the heat sink and the vessel is temperature-clamped, so binding constraints
  move to condenser duty and vent capacity, and a U * A * dT_lm shortfall
  against Q_rxn,max is not a reason to refuse a refluxing step.
dT_ad above 50 K does not by itself require independent secondary protection.
  Stoessel's criticality class depends on where the maximum temperature of the
  synthesis reaction sits relative to the boiling point and to the 24-hour
  time-to-maximum-rate temperature. A large adiabatic rise whose MTSR stays
  below the boiling point and far below T_D24 is class 1 or 2, and the solvent
  and the jacket control it.
No explosive atmosphere means no hazardous zone. A flammable solvent handled
  wholly in a closed, inerted system, or present at a concentration that cannot
  reach the LFL in the headspace, generates no zone to classify — and
  conversely, a solvent with a flash point above 60C handled ABOVE its flash
  point does need assessment, which a flash-point threshold alone would miss.
Mixing scale-up is not a risk where the kinetics are slow relative to blend
  time. A reaction with a half-life of hours against a blend time of seconds is
  kinetically controlled at every scale in range, and CFD or a mixing
  correlation for it is effort spent on a question already answered.
Material compatibility verdicts are condition-bound. Anhydrous HCl in an organic
  stream is routine service for 316 stainless, because chloride stress corrosion
  cracking needs water, chloride and tensile stress together, so refusing a dry
  HCl step on a wet-service corrosion rule is wrong. The same table entry is
  correct for aqueous HCl and must not be relaxed there.

[Tool limitations]

A structure file carries no thermochemistry. RDKit parses SMILES and validates
  syntax, not chemical plausibility — a structure can be syntactically valid and
  physically impossible — and it returns no enthalpy of reaction, no
  decomposition onset and no gas stoichiometry, so nothing drawn from a SMILES
  bears on dT_ad or on vent sizing. Those are calorimetric measurements.
DSC reports a scan-rate-dependent onset, not a safe temperature. The quantity
  that sets an operating limit is the adiabatic time to maximum rate, from
  adiabatic or isothermal calorimetry on the real mass.
A simulator's heat-transfer coefficient is a correlation fitted to a clean wall
  and an idealised Newtonian fluid. Fouling, a slurry and a shear-thinning mass
  fall outside its validity, and U on a specific vessel is measured, not
  predicted.
CFD resolves the hydrodynamics of the geometry and boundary conditions supplied
  to it. It carries no kinetics unless kinetics are supplied, so it cannot
  predict a mixing-sensitive selectivity on its own.
A vent-sizing code applies the methodology to the flow regime selected for it.
  Choosing all-vapour where the real case is 2-phase returns a confidently
  undersized device, and the regime comes from a closed-vessel adiabatic test
  rather than from the code.

[Limitations]

Does not cover polymerisation (distinct runaway kinetics, viscosity effects).
Does not cover biotransformations, photochemistry, or electrochemistry.
Does not cover cryogenic reactions below -78C or supercritical fluid processes.
