DOMAIN: Chemistry — Analytical Method Validation
CODES: ICH Q2(R2) (Analytical Validation), USP <1225>, USP <621> (Chromatography),
       Ph. Eur. 2.2.46, 21 CFR Part 11 / Annex 11 (Data Integrity)
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: HPLC and UV-Vis methods for pharmaceutical release and stability testing,
       small-molecule analytes, GMP-validated methods

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Method validation per ICH Q2(R2) must demonstrate: accuracy, precision
  (repeatability + intermediate), specificity, linearity, range, LOD, LOQ.
  Robustness evaluated during development; validated if critical.
Linearity: R^2 >= 0.999 across validated range (typically 80-120% nominal for
  assay, LOQ-120% of spec limit for impurities). y-intercept bias <= 2% of
  target response.
System suitability per USP <621>:
  Precision: RSD of >= 5 replicate injections <= 2.0% (area).
  Tailing factor: T = W_0.05 / (2 * f) <= 2.0.
  Resolution: R_s = 2 * (t_R2 - t_R1) / (W_1 + W_2) >= 2.0.
  Theoretical plates: N = 16 * (t_R / W)^2, typically N >= 2000.
Detection and quantitation limits:
  LOD = 3.3 * sigma / S; LOQ = 10 * sigma / S
  where sigma = SD of response (blank or regression residual), S = calibration
  slope. LOQ verified by accuracy (80-120% recovery) and precision (RSD <= 10%).
Mass balance in forced degradation:
  assay + degradation products + residual >= 95% of initial material.
  Target 5-20% degradation per stress condition (acid, base, oxidative,
  thermal, photolytic per ICH Q1B). Over-degradation obscures the profile.
Reference standard purity characterised by independent absolute method
  (DSC, qNMR, or mass balance). Purity factor applied to all calculations:
  True_conc = weighed_conc * (purity / 100).
Beer-Lambert law: A = epsilon * l * c. Linear range valid only in dilute
  solution. Deviations expected above A ~ 1.5 (stray light) and below
  A ~ 0.1 (noise-dominated). Verify linearity experimentally.
Data integrity: ALCOA+ principles. Electronic records per 21 CFR Part 11 /
  Annex 11. Audit trail enabled and reviewed; no data deletion permitted.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer validated pharmacopoeial methods (USP, EP, JP) where available.
Prefer HPLC over GC for non-volatile analytes (no derivatisation needed).
Target run time < 15 min for routine release testing. UHPLC acceptable.
Prefer gradient for impurity methods, isocratic for simple assay.

[Verification procedures]

Confirm calibration range brackets all expected sample concentrations —
  no extrapolation beyond validated range permitted.
Verify forced degradation blanks (stressed matrix without analyte) to
  exclude artefact peaks from the degradation profile.
Confirm sample preparation does not introduce bias: test filter adsorption,
  extraction recovery, solution stability over the analytical window.
Verify intermediate precision: different analyst, day, instrument where
  possible. RSD <= 2.0% (assay), <= 10% (at LOQ).

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If area RSD fails system suitability while retention-time RSD passes, the fault
  is in the injection — partial draw, a cored septum, an air bubble in the
  needle — not in the chromatography. Check the autosampler before touching the
  column or the mobile phase.
If early peaks split or shoulder while late peaks stay symmetric, check the
  sample diluent strength before suspecting a voided column. A diluent stronger
  than the mobile phase distorts only weakly retained peaks; a channel at the
  column inlet distorts all of them.
If tailing rises for basic analytes while neutral peaks hold T <= 2.0, check
  mobile-phase pH against the analyte pKa and the buffer concentration before
  replacing the column. A base buffered within about 1 pH unit of its pKa tails
  for a chemical reason, not a packing one.
If retention times drift steadily earlier across an isocratic sequence, check
  the premixed mobile phase for evaporative loss of the organic component
  before investigating the pump.
If mass balance in forced degradation falls short of 95%, check the response
  factor assumption and whether a degradant has lost its chromophore before
  concluding that an unknown degradant is unaccounted for. A degradant with a
  response factor of 0.3 relative to the parent reports at 30% of its true
  level.
If R^2 >= 0.999 but recovery is biased at the ends of the range, plot the
  residuals and the response factors before accepting linearity. R^2 is
  insensitive to the curvature implied by 120% recovery at the top of the range
  and 90% at the bottom.
If a UV method is non-linear at LOW absorbance, check adsorption onto the
  cuvette, the filter and the vial before blaming detector noise. Adsorptive
  loss is concentration-dependent; noise is not.

[Known failure modes]

Quantifying an impurity against the parent's response without measuring the
  relative response factor. RRFs of 0.2 to 3 are ordinary, so an uncorrected
  area ratio can be wrong by a factor of 5.
Reporting area percent as an assay. Area percent assumes every component
  responds equally and silently excludes anything that does not elute.
Manual reintegration to bring a result into specification. The arithmetic is
  defensible and the practice is a data-integrity finding.
Identifying a peak by retention time alone, then carrying that identity into a
  specification.
Preparing standard and sample in different diluents, so the injection solvent
  differs between them and the comparison is not like for like.
Running system suitability only at the head of a sequence, so nothing
  establishes that the system was still suitable at injection 60.
Reporting assay on an as-is basis against a specification written on an
  anhydrous, solvent-free basis, or the reverse.
Inferring sample solution stability from the standard's stability. The standard
  is pure; the sample carries matrix, excipients and degradants that catalyse.
Reading carryover from a preceding high-level standard as a low-level impurity.

[Exception catalogue]

LOD and LOQ are not required validation characteristics for an assay. ICH Q2
  requires them for impurity and degradation-product methods; a potency assay
  operating at nominal concentration has no quantitation limit to establish,
  and demanding one is over-validation.
A method taken unchanged from a pharmacopoeial monograph is VERIFIED under USP
  <1226>, not validated under ICH Q2. The user demonstrates suitability under
  the actual conditions of use; repeating the full validation package duplicates
  work the pharmacopoeia has already done.
A pharmacopoeial reference standard (USP RS, EP CRS) carries no independent
  purity determination by the user. It is used as directed, and applying a
  purity factor to a standard whose label already accounts for content is an
  error that biases every result derived from it.
The system-suitability numbers belong to the monograph or to the validated
  method, not to chromatography in general. USP <621> derives the permitted
  replicate RSD from the number of injections and the limit being tested, and a
  single-analyte assay with nothing eluting nearby has no resolution criterion
  to meet at all.
A mass balance below 95% is a correct result where the degradant is
  non-chromophoric at the detection wavelength, volatile (CO2, formaldehyde, a
  low-boiling amine), or does not elute. The obligation is to explain the gap
  with evidence, not to close it.
Early-phase and pre-clinical methods are qualified rather than validated. The
  full ICH Q2 package is due before the method supports a GMP release decision
  or a stability-indicating claim, not before it supports a development
  decision.

[Tool limitations]

Photodiode-array peak purity can exclude gross co-elution and cannot establish
  peak homogeneity. A co-eluting impurity with a similar spectrum, or present
  below roughly 0.5% relative, returns a passing purity angle.
The chromatography data system reports the area the processing method asks for.
  Baseline placement and integration parameters on a shoulder or a tailing peak
  move the result by more than the method's own precision, so 2 analysts can
  disagree on the same raw file.
Regression software returns R^2 and a p-value for curved data as readily as for
  linear data. It fits the model it is given; it does not test whether the
  linear model is the right one.
Published spectra and predicted extinction coefficients do not supply a
  response factor. Epsilon at the detection wavelength must be measured against
  a characterised standard in the actual mobile phase.
RDKit parses SMILES and validates syntax, not chemical plausibility: a
  structure can be syntactically valid and physically impossible. It predicts
  neither a chromophore nor a retention time, so a structure proposed for an
  unknown degradant peak stays a hypothesis until an orthogonal measurement
  supports it.

[Limitations]

Does not cover LC-MS/MS (distinct selectivity/matrix effect requirements).
Does not cover qNMR, biological assays (ELISA, cell-based), or ICP-MS/OES.
Does not cover environmental analysis (EPA methods) or dissolution (USP <711>).
