DOMAIN: Structural Engineering — Building Design
CODES: BS EN 1993-1-1 (Steel), BS EN 1992-1-1 (Concrete), BS EN 1990 (Basis),
       BS EN 1991-1-1 (Actions), AISC 360 (US steel alternative)
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Multi-storey steel/concrete buildings up to ~10 storeys, conventional
       framing, non-seismic regions

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

ULS load combination (Eurocode, Eq. 6.10):
  F_d = 1.35 * G_k + 1.5 * Q_k,1 + SUM(1.5 * psi_0,i * Q_k,i)
  AISC LRFD alternative: F_d = 1.2 * D + 1.6 * L + 0.5 * (L_r or S or R)
  Both expressions are non-negotiable — code selection determines which applies.

Moment capacity (steel, Class 1 or 2 sections):
  M_Ed <= M_c,Rd = W_pl,y * f_y / gamma_M0
  where gamma_M0 = 1.00 (BS EN 1993-1-1, 6.2.5). Class 3: use W_el,y.

Shear capacity (steel, unstiffened web):
  V_Ed <= V_c,Rd = A_v * (f_y / sqrt(3)) / gamma_M0
  where A_v = A - 2*b*t_f + (t_w + 2*r)*t_f for rolled I/H sections.

Combined bending + shear: if V_Ed > 0.5 * V_c,Rd, reduce moment capacity
  by factor (1 - rho), where rho = (2*V_Ed/V_pl,Rd - 1)^2.
  V_pl,Rd = plastic shear resistance = A_v * (f_y / sqrt(3)) / gamma_M0
  (equals V_c,Rd for Class 1/2 sections; BS EN 1993-1-1, 6.2.6/6.2.8).

Deflection limits (SLS, BS EN 1993-1-1, NA.2.23):
  Total: delta <= L / 250
  Beams supporting brittle finishes (glazing, plaster): delta <= L / 360
  Imposed load only (variable action): delta <= L / 360

Column buckling (BS EN 1993-1-1, 6.3.1):
  N_Ed <= N_b,Rd = chi * A * f_y / gamma_M1
  chi = 1 / (Phi + sqrt(Phi^2 - lambda_bar^2)), chi <= 1.0
  Phi = 0.5 * (1 + alpha*(lambda_bar - 0.2) + lambda_bar^2)
  alpha from buckling curve (a0=0.13, a=0.21, b=0.34, c=0.49, d=0.76).

Lateral stability: complete load path from point of application to foundations.
  Every floor must be braced or have rigid frame action in two orthogonal directions.

Fire resistance rating must match building use classification (Approved Doc B
  or BS EN 1991-1-2). Minimum 30 min (houses) to 120 min (high-risk occupancy).

Steel yield strength must match specified grade:
  S235: f_y = 235 MPa (t <= 16mm), S275: f_y = 275 MPa, S355: f_y = 355 MPa.
  Reduce f_y for thickness > 16mm per BS EN 10025 Table 7.

Connection capacity >= member capacity for ductile design (capacity design).
  Bolt shear: F_v,Rd = alpha_v * f_ub * A_s / gamma_M2 (gamma_M2 = 1.25).

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer standardised rolled sections (UB, UC, UBP catalogues) over fabricated.
Minimise distinct section sizes on a project — aids procurement and reduces error.
Design connections considering construction sequence and erection tolerances.
Rationalise column grid to suit typical spans (6m-9m steel, 5m-8m RC).

[Verification procedures]

Self-weight consistency: verify that the self-weight used in analysis matches
  the sections selected (iterate if section sizes change significantly).
Load path completeness: trace every load from application to foundation —
  gravity, lateral (wind/notional), and torsional (asymmetric plans).
Connection assumptions: pinned connections in analysis must be detailed as pins;
  moment connections must have stiffness compatible with analysis model.
Check both ULS and SLS: a design that passes strength may fail deflection,
  and vice versa. Natural frequency check if span > ~10m (f >= 4 Hz typical).

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a beam passes moment capacity with margin and still fails, check the lateral-torsional buckling
  verification and the effective length between restraints before increasing the section: M_b,Rd
  with an unrestrained compression flange can be a small fraction of M_c,Rd.
If utilisation rises when the section is made larger, check flange thickness against BS EN 10025
  Table 7. Crossing 16 mm reduces f_y for S355 from 355 to 345 MPa and crossing 40 mm reduces it
  again, so a deeper section can be weaker in yield terms than the one it replaced.
If a frame analysis returns less sway than expected, confirm that notional horizontal loads were
  applied and that second-order effects were considered: alpha_cr below 10 means they are not
  negligible, and the frame must be amplified or analysed second-order.
If a floor satisfies L/360 and occupants still report movement, check the fundamental frequency,
  modal mass and damping rather than adding stiffness: a long-span lightweight floor can pass
  deflection and fail a vibration response factor.
If column base reactions do not balance the applied vertical load, look for load applied to elements
  the model treats as unloaded — cladding rails, secondary beams released at both ends — before
  suspecting the solver.
If a connection is reported adequate while the member is at full capacity, check the assumed pin or
  fixity against the detail. A nominally pinned end plate detailed stiff attracts a moment the beam
  was never designed for; a nominally fixed connection that is flexible sheds moment into the span.
If a punching shear check fails at a flat-slab column, examine the column head detail and whether
  the control perimeter has been reduced by a slab edge or a nearby opening before adding shear
  reinforcement.
If a fire period is satisfied by a section-factor calculation for a beam carrying a compartment wall,
  confirm which criteria apply: protecting the steel for load-bearing capacity R 60 does not by
  itself give the wall it supports integrity and insulation for the same period.

[Known failure modes]

Lateral-torsional buckling omitted because the beam carries a slab. Composite action requires shear
  connection, and a slab resting on the top flange restrains nothing during construction.
Effective length factors taken for an idealised end condition the connection does not provide, most
  often treating a nominal 4-bolt base plate as fixed.
Notional horizontal loads for equivalent imperfections left out of the lateral design, or applied in
  a single direction and a single combination.
Robustness and tying-force requirements assumed satisfied, with no check that the horizontal tie
  forces can pass through the connections actually detailed.
Composite beams designed for the composite section throughout, with no verification of the bare
  steel beam under wet concrete and construction load, and no propping specified.
Transfer structures and discontinuous columns introduced by a layout change without re-tracing the
  vertical load path, so a column lands on a slab.
Deflection checked as a total when the brittle-finish limit applies to deflection occurring after the
  finish is installed, and precamber credited against a limit it does not relieve.
Fire protection specified as a generic thickness rather than by section factor and required period,
  leaving slender members under-protected and stocky ones over-protected.
Bolt groups verified for bolt shear while the governing mode is unchecked: bearing on a thin plate,
  block shear at an end connection, or the long-joint reduction on a line of bolts.

[Exception catalogue]

The deflection limits of NA.2.23 are recommendations for conventional construction, not absolute
  requirements, and the governing criterion is fitness for the finishes and services actually
  present. A documented relaxation is legitimate for an exposed steel roof with no brittle finish,
  or for a member whose movement is taken up by a joint — and the limit tightens below L/360 where a
  facade, a lift guide or a crane rail demands it.
Eq. 6.10 is 1 of 3 permitted routes. BS EN 1990 also offers the pair 6.10a and 6.10b, and the UK
  National Annex permits either, so a verification using 6.10b with the reduction factor on the
  permanent action is code-compliant rather than under-designed. Mixing expressions within a single
  verification is the error, not choosing the less conservative pair.
Capacity design — connection stronger than member — is required where ductility is relied on for
  force redistribution or for robustness. For a non-dissipative member in a conventional non-seismic
  frame, a connection designed for the applied effects with its own partial factors is sufficient and
  is normal UK practice; forcing full-strength connections throughout adds cost and fabrication risk
  with no mechanism that needs them.
Class 4 slender sections are not excluded by the expressions quoted above, which cover Class 1 and 2
  with W_pl and Class 3 with W_el. A Class 4 member is verified with effective section properties to
  BS EN 1993-1-5, so a cold-formed or slender plate-girder design is not non-compliant merely for
  falling outside those equations.
The yield strengths listed are specified minima for the grade and thickness. Where a design relies on
  tested material — an assessment of an existing structure using coupon results, or steel certified
  to a higher minimum — the tested characteristic value governs under its own evaluation rules; and
  where overstrength is unwelcome, as in a deliberate fuse element, it is the upper characteristic
  value that matters rather than the lower.
A fire resistance period of 0 is legitimate for elements whose failure affects neither means of
  escape, nor compartmentation, nor the stability of the structure — single-storey elements under the
  boundary-distance rules, and members inside a sacrificial non-load-bearing assembly. The period
  follows from use, height and compartmentation, not from a minimum applied to every member.

[Tool limitations]

Analysis packages report utilisation against the checks requested. A member with no
  lateral-torsional buckling check configured, or a column with no second-order amplification
  applied, returns a figure below 1.0 that says nothing about the omission.
A linear elastic analysis cannot reveal a mechanism. Acceptable capacity ratios throughout do not
  establish stability, because a model's restraints or rigid-diaphragm assumptions can supply in a
  given direction what the detailing does not.
Model-based quantity take-off reports what is modelled. Connections, stiffeners, fire protection and
  non-structural restraint usually sit outside the structural model, so self-weight derived from it
  is systematically light and the restraint it implies is not the restraint that will be built.
Section property libraries hold nominal dimensions. Rolling tolerances, corrosion loss on existing
  steel, and flange or web material removed for a notch or a service penetration are absent, so a
  check against library properties overstates the capacity of the member that exists.
Fire engineering tools compute a limiting temperature and a protection thickness from the section
  factor and load ratio supplied. They do not verify that the load ratio reflects the fire
  combination, nor that protection is continuous across connections and penetrations, which is where
  it actually fails.

[Limitations]

Does not cover seismic design (BS EN 1998 / ASCE 7 required separately).
Does not cover soil-structure interaction or foundation design.
Not applicable to specialist structures: long-span (>30m), high-rise (>10
  storeys), tension structures, shell structures.
Does not cover non-standard materials (aluminium, stainless steel, timber).
Does not address progressive collapse or disproportionate collapse beyond
  basic tie-force requirements.
