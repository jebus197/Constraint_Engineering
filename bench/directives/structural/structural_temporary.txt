DOMAIN: Structural Engineering — Temporary Works
CODES: BS 5975 (Code of practice for temporary works), BS EN 12811 (Scaffolding),
       BS EN 12812 (Falsework), BS EN 12813 (Load-bearing towers),
       BS EN 1991-1-4 (Wind), CIRIA C660 (Formwork pressure)
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Falsework, formwork, scaffolding, propping, temporary foundations.
       Conventional building and civil works, non-marine.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Partial safety factors DIFFER from permanent works (BS 5975, Table 1):
  gamma_f (loads): 1.35 (dead), 1.50 (imposed/construction), 1.50 (wind).
  gamma_m (materials): 1.0 (steel to BS EN 1993), but 1.25 for reused/
  ungraded timber. For proprietary systems, use manufacturer's stated capacity
  with appropriate gamma — never exceed catalogue rated load.

Design life: typically <= 2 years. This affects:
  Wind return period: 2-year NOT 50-year (V_2yr approx 0.78 * V_50yr per
  BS EN 1991-1-4, Note to 4.2). Still non-trivial — a common error is
  ignoring wind entirely on scaffolding and falsework.
  Material degradation: negligible for steel, but check timber moisture content
  and corrosion on reused components.

Wind loading on scaffolding/falsework (BS EN 12811-1, BS EN 1991-1-4):
  Sheeted scaffold: C_f * q_p(z) * A_ref — wind force can govern design.
  Open scaffold: force coefficients per BS EN 12811-1, Annex A.
  Site-specific wind speed: V_b = V_b,map * c_dir * c_season * c_alt.
  CRITICAL: wind on partially completed structures often exceeds wind on the
  finished structure (no cladding to share load, incomplete bracing).

Wet concrete pressure on formwork (CIRIA C660 / BS EN 12812):
  Hydrostatic: p = rho * g * h, where rho_concrete = 2400 kg/m^3 (reinforced),
  giving p = 23.5 kN/m^3 * h (depth in metres). For deep pours (walls/columns),
  p_max depends on pour rate R, concrete temperature T, and cement type.
  CIRIA C660 formula: p_max = rho*g*(C_1*sqrt(R) + C_2*K*sqrt(H) - C_1*sqrt(R))
  Simplified conservative: assume full hydrostatic up to 25 kN/m^3 * h.
  NEVER assume concrete is self-supporting before initial set.

Propping loads — construction sequence (BS 5975, Clause 15):
  Slab propping must account for the load of freshly poured concrete above
  PLUS construction live loads BEFORE the supporting structure has reached
  design strength. Backpropping through multiple floors:
  F_prop = w_slab * A_trib * n_levels_supported (worst case).
  Common failure: assuming the slab below can carry the load of the slab
  above when the lower slab is only 7 days old (approx 65-75% of f_ck,28).

Ground bearing under temporary foundations / sole plates:
  Bearing pressure: sigma = F / A_sole <= q_a (allowable bearing capacity).
  q_a must be verified for ACTUAL ground conditions — not assumed.
  Sole plate size: A_sole >= F / q_a. Timber sole plates: check bending
  capacity of the plate itself (simply supported or cantilevered from prop).

Scaffold tie spacing (BS EN 12811 / NASC TG20 Compliance Sheet):
  Maximum tie pattern depends on scaffold configuration, cladding, height.
  TG20 standard solutions: typically 2.4m x 4.0m or 2.4m x 2.0m pattern.
  Every lift must be tied at the prescribed pattern — missing ties is a
  common cause of scaffold collapse. Ties must resist tension AND compression.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Reusability of components: prefer standard proprietary systems (Peri, Doka,
  SGB, Layher) over bespoke fabrications — easier inspection, known capacity.
Speed of erection and striking: influences programme but not safety.
Minimise crane lifts: modular pre-assembly reduces crane dependency.
Access and working platforms: minimum 600mm clear width (BS EN 12811).

[Verification procedures]

Construction load analysis: verify that the temporary works design accounts
  for the full construction load sequence — not just the permanent state.
  Worst case is often mid-pour or mid-erection, not the completed state.
Ground conditions: verify bearing capacity under sole plates by inspection
  (as minimum) or testing. Pad foundations on made ground or soft clay need
  specific geotechnical input. Record actual ground vs assumed ground.
Strike times: confirm that concrete has reached adequate strength before
  striking formwork or removing props. Typically >= 70% of f_ck,28 for
  soffit formwork, >= 100% before full design load applied.
  Maturity testing or cube results — do not rely on age alone in cold weather.
Wind assumptions: check assumed wind speed against Met Office data for the
  site and season. Two-year return period is appropriate for design life
  <= 2 years but must use correct seasonal factor (winter exposure is worse).
Temporary works coordinator: BS 5975 requires a named TWC on every project.
  Design check category (1, 2, or 3) determines level of independent review.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a falsework design is adequate on paper but the erected towers are out of plumb, address lateral
  stability before vertical capacity: proprietary tower capacities are quoted at a stated verticality
  tolerance, and a lean of 1 in 100 converts vertical load into a lateral demand the bracing may
  never have been designed for.
If wall formwork deflects more than predicted, check the achieved pour rate and concrete temperature
  against the CIRIA C660 assumptions before the form stiffness. A faster pour or a colder mix raises
  p_max because less of the placed concrete has set.
If props show distress at a floor above the one being poured, count the levels of backpropping the
  sequence actually leaves in place. Load sheds back to the last slab at full strength, so the
  worst-loaded prop is often not the one beneath the pour.
If sole plates settle unevenly, assess the ground as it is now rather than as the site investigation
  found it: made ground, a service trench, a stripped haul road or a saturated formation after rain
  all change the allowable bearing pressure the design assumed.
If a scaffold is reported compliant to a TG20 standard solution but carries sheeting, debris netting,
  a loading bay or a banner that was not in the original configuration, treat it as a designed
  scaffold. The standard solution's tie pattern is valid only for the configuration it was derived
  for.
If a prop's capacity looks higher than expected, check the extension and the eccentricity. Published
  capacity falls sharply with extended length, and load applied off the axis or through an unseated
  head plate reduces it further than the length curve alone.
If a striking decision rests on concrete age, ask for cube or maturity results before agreeing it:
  7-day strength at 5 degC is far below 7-day strength at 20 degC, and age is not evidence in cold
  weather.
If a tower or prop layout was moved on site to clear an obstruction, re-check the load path down to
  the sole plates before accepting it. Shifting a standard off the plate centre, or spanning a bearer
  further than designed, changes both the prop load and the bending in the plate itself, and neither
  appears on the drawing that was altered.

[Known failure modes]

Permanent-works partial factors and material grades applied to reused or ungraded components, timber
  sole plates and bearers most often, where gamma_m is higher and the grade is unknown.
Wind taken at the 50-year return period and then relaxed by judgement instead of by the stated
  return-period and seasonal factors, so the reduction cannot be audited.
The completed state verified and the construction sequence not, when the worst case is usually
  mid-pour, mid-erection, or the moment a prop is removed.
Ties omitted at the first lift, at a return, or where an opening interrupts the pattern, with the
  scaffold then reported as tied because the pattern is correct elsewhere.
Edge protection, brick guards, sheeting or netting added after design, multiplying the wind reference
  area with no re-check of ties or foundations.
Backpropping designed for a single level when the sequence leaves 2 or 3, or designed for slab
  self-weight alone with no construction live load.
Proprietary systems mixed between manufacturers, or fitted with couplers of unknown provenance,
  voiding the catalogue capacity the design relied upon.
The BS 5975 design check category set too low, so a scheme requiring independent Category 3 review
  receives a Category 1 check from within the designer's own team.
Props struck onto a slab that has reached 28-day strength but was never designed for a construction
  load case at all.

[Exception catalogue]

The 2-year return-period wind is permitted BECAUSE the exposure is short, so it does not apply to
  temporary works that stand longer: a scaffold left through a winter, a propping scheme in place for
  an 18-month refurbishment, falsework across a seasonal shutdown. The return period follows the
  actual duration of exposure, not the word temporary.
Permanent-works deflection and crack-width limits do not apply to temporary works, and importing
  them is the commonest over-design in this domain. Formwork deflection is governed by the specified
  surface tolerance of the finished concrete, falsework deflection by the as-cast level and the
  precamber allowed for it, and a working platform by serviceability of the walking surface — not by
  span over 250 or span over 360.
The converse also holds and is the more dangerous direction: temporary works carrying the public, or
  standing over a live highway, railway or occupied building, rise in consequence class. The design
  check category rises with it, and the loading may become that of a permanent structure — crowd
  loading on a temporary grandstand or public footbridge, a protective deck over a running line to
  the infrastructure owner's standard.
Full hydrostatic pressure is the safe simplification, and it legitimately does not govern a pour
  that is slow, warm and deep enough for the lower lifts to have set: CIRIA C660 returns a lower
  p_max in those conditions, and designing a 10 m wall form for full hydrostatic head is a large
  unnecessary cost. The condition is that pour rate, temperature and admixture regime are specified
  and controlled on site — a retarded or self-compacting mix removes the reduction.
Dynamic and impact terms absent from permanent-works design do apply here, so the permanent design
  is no precedent for omitting them: a skip or kibble landing on a working platform, the reaction of
  a concrete placing boom, a prop struck by plant, and the asymmetric load of a partially discharged
  pour are temporary-works load cases with no permanent-state equivalent.
Allowable bearing pressure from the site investigation legitimately does not apply at sole-plate
  level. The investigation reports the founding stratum at design depth, whereas a sole plate bears
  on whatever is present after stripping, filling, trafficking and rain, so a lower verified pressure
  or a ground improvement is the correct input, and quoting the geotechnical figure for a sole plate
  is a misapplication rather than a conservative choice.

[Tool limitations]

Manufacturers' catalogues and system design software (Peri, Doka, Layher, SGB) give capacities for
  the system assembled as tested, within stated verticality, extension and bracing configurations.
  They cannot tell you whether the frame erected on site matches that configuration, and they are
  silent on mixed components, bent or worn members and non-original couplers.
TG20 compliance software issues a sheet for the configuration entered. A scaffold that later gains
  sheeting, a loading bay, a hoist tower or an unplanned return stands outside the sheet it holds,
  and the sheet carries no record of what was actually built.
Analysis of falsework models the joints as specified, while proprietary tower and tube-and-fitting
  connections are semi-rigid and slip. A model with pinned or fixed joints and no slip understates
  lateral deflection, which is the quantity the stability of the scheme depends on.
Maturity meters and temperature-matched curing report the strength of the concrete at the sensor. A
  thin section, a soffit near a cold external edge, or a late pour can lag the monitored location,
  and the striking decision applies to the weakest part of the pour.
Visual inspection of ground conditions, which BS 5975 accepts as a minimum, cannot detect a void, a
  backfilled trench, a buried service surround or a perched water table. Where a sole-plate failure
  would collapse the works, a plate bearing test, a dynamic probe or a trial pit is the instrument.

[Limitations]

Does not cover deep excavation support (sheet piles, secant piles, diaphragm
  walls — requires specialist geotechnical design to BS EN 1997 / CIRIA C760).
Does not cover marine temporary works (cofferdams, jack-up barges).
Does not address demolition temporary works (temporary stability during
  sequential demolition — requires specialist methodology to BS 6187).
Does not cover specialist access systems (mast climbers, suspended platforms,
  rope access) — separate regulations and competence requirements.
Does not address temporary traffic management structures or signs.
