DOMAIN: Structural Engineering — Temporary Works
CODES: BS 5975 (Code of practice for temporary works), BS EN 12811 (Scaffolding),
       BS EN 12812 (Falsework), BS EN 12813 (Load-bearing towers),
       BS EN 1991-1-4 (Wind), CIRIA C660 (Formwork pressure)
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Falsework, formwork, scaffolding, propping, temporary foundations.
       Conventional building and civil works, non-marine.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Partial safety factors DIFFER from permanent works (BS 5975, Table 1):
  gamma_f (loads): 1.35 (dead), 1.50 (imposed/construction), 1.50 (wind).
  gamma_m (materials): 1.0 (steel to BS EN 1993), but 1.25 for reused/
  ungraded timber. For proprietary systems, use manufacturer's stated capacity
  with appropriate gamma — never exceed catalogue rated load.

Design life: typically <= 2 years. This affects:
  Wind return period: 2-year NOT 50-year (V_2yr approx 0.78 * V_50yr per
  BS EN 1991-1-4, Note to 4.2). Still non-trivial — a common error is
  ignoring wind entirely on scaffolding and falsework.
  Material degradation: negligible for steel, but check timber moisture content
  and corrosion on reused components.

Wind loading on scaffolding/falsework (BS EN 12811-1, BS EN 1991-1-4):
  Sheeted scaffold: C_f * q_p(z) * A_ref — wind force can govern design.
  Open scaffold: force coefficients per BS EN 12811-1, Annex A.
  Site-specific wind speed: V_b = V_b,map * c_dir * c_season * c_alt.
  CRITICAL: wind on partially completed structures often exceeds wind on the
  finished structure (no cladding to share load, incomplete bracing).

Wet concrete pressure on formwork (CIRIA C660 / BS EN 12812):
  Hydrostatic: p = rho * g * h, where rho_concrete = 2400 kg/m^3 (reinforced),
  giving p = 23.5 kN/m^3 * h (depth in metres). For deep pours (walls/columns),
  p_max depends on pour rate R, concrete temperature T, and cement type.
  CIRIA C660 formula: p_max = rho*g*(C_1*sqrt(R) + C_2*K*sqrt(H) - C_1*sqrt(R))
  Simplified conservative: assume full hydrostatic up to 25 kN/m^3 * h.
  NEVER assume concrete is self-supporting before initial set.

Propping loads — construction sequence (BS 5975, Clause 15):
  Slab propping must account for the load of freshly poured concrete above
  PLUS construction live loads BEFORE the supporting structure has reached
  design strength. Backpropping through multiple floors:
  F_prop = w_slab * A_trib * n_levels_supported (worst case).
  Common failure: assuming the slab below can carry the load of the slab
  above when the lower slab is only 7 days old (approx 65-75% of f_ck,28).

Ground bearing under temporary foundations / sole plates:
  Bearing pressure: sigma = F / A_sole <= q_a (allowable bearing capacity).
  q_a must be verified for ACTUAL ground conditions — not assumed.
  Sole plate size: A_sole >= F / q_a. Timber sole plates: check bending
  capacity of the plate itself (simply supported or cantilevered from prop).

Scaffold tie spacing (BS EN 12811 / NASC TG20 Compliance Sheet):
  Maximum tie pattern depends on scaffold configuration, cladding, height.
  TG20 standard solutions: typically 2.4m x 4.0m or 2.4m x 2.0m pattern.
  Every lift must be tied at the prescribed pattern — missing ties is a
  common cause of scaffold collapse. Ties must resist tension AND compression.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Reusability of components: prefer standard proprietary systems (Peri, Doka,
  SGB, Layher) over bespoke fabrications — easier inspection, known capacity.
Speed of erection and striking: influences programme but not safety.
Minimise crane lifts: modular pre-assembly reduces crane dependency.
Access and working platforms: minimum 600mm clear width (BS EN 12811).

[Verification procedures]

Construction load analysis: verify that the temporary works design accounts
  for the full construction load sequence — not just the permanent state.
  Worst case is often mid-pour or mid-erection, not the completed state.
Ground conditions: verify bearing capacity under sole plates by inspection
  (as minimum) or testing. Pad foundations on made ground or soft clay need
  specific geotechnical input. Record actual ground vs assumed ground.
Strike times: confirm that concrete has reached adequate strength before
  striking formwork or removing props. Typically >= 70% of f_ck,28 for
  soffit formwork, >= 100% before full design load applied.
  Maturity testing or cube results — do not rely on age alone in cold weather.
Wind assumptions: check assumed wind speed against Met Office data for the
  site and season. Two-year return period is appropriate for design life
  <= 2 years but must use correct seasonal factor (winter exposure is worse).
Temporary works coordinator: BS 5975 requires a named TWC on every project.
  Design check category (1, 2, or 3) determines level of independent review.

[Limitations]

Does not cover deep excavation support (sheet piles, secant piles, diaphragm
  walls — requires specialist geotechnical design to BS EN 1997 / CIRIA C760).
Does not cover marine temporary works (cofferdams, jack-up barges).
Does not address demolition temporary works (temporary stability during
  sequential demolition — requires specialist methodology to BS 6187).
Does not cover specialist access systems (mast climbers, suspended platforms,
  rope access) — separate regulations and competence requirements.
Does not address temporary traffic management structures or signs.
