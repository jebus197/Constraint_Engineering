DOMAIN: Structural Engineering — Bridge Design
CODES: BS EN 1991-2 (Traffic loads), BS EN 1992-2 (Concrete bridges),
       BS EN 1993-2 (Steel bridges), BS EN 1993-1-9 (Fatigue),
       BS EN 1991-1-5 (Thermal), CD 350 (DMRB geometric design)
VERSION: 1.0
SCOPE: Highway bridges (road traffic), steel/composite/concrete, single and
       multi-span, non-seismic regions, spans up to ~60m

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Load Model 1 (LM1) per BS EN 1991-2, Table 4.2:
  Lane 1: tandem axle = 2 x 300 kN (alpha_Q1 = 1.0), UDL = 9.0 kN/m^2
  Lane 2: tandem axle = 2 x 200 kN, UDL = 2.5 kN/m^2
  Lane 3: tandem axle = 2 x 100 kN, UDL = 2.5 kN/m^2
  Remaining area: UDL = 2.5 kN/m^2, no tandem.
  Tandem axle spacing = 1.2m, wheel contact = 0.4m x 0.4m.

Dynamic amplification: included within LM1 characteristic values (no separate
  DAF for LM1). For LM2 (single axle = 400 kN), DAF is also included.
  Braking/acceleration: F_braking = 0.6 * alpha_Q1 * 2*Q_1k + 0.1 * alpha_q1 *
  q_1k * w_1 * L, bounded 180 kN <= F <= 900 kN.

Fatigue verification mandatory for steel and composite bridges:
  BS EN 1993-1-9, detail categories (e.g. Category 71 for fillet welds,
  Category 160 for base metal). Fatigue strength at 2*10^6 cycles:
  Delta_sigma_R = Delta_sigma_C * (2*10^6 / N)^(1/m), m=3 for N<=5*10^6.
  Damage equivalent factor lambda per BS EN 1993-2, Annex A.

Durability — exposure class per BS EN 1992-1-1, Table 4.1:
  XD3 (cyclic wet/dry with de-icing salts) typical for UK highway bridges.
  XC3/XC4 for sheltered/exposed concrete surfaces.
  Minimum cover: c_min = c_min,b + c_min,dur + Delta_c_dur.
  Typical: 45mm (XD3 with C40/50, 100-year design life per BS EN 1992-2 NA).

Thermal actions (BS EN 1991-1-5):
  Bearing design must accommodate expansion: Delta_L = alpha_T * Delta_T * L
  alpha_T = 12 x 10^-6 /degC (steel), 10 x 10^-6 /degC (concrete).
  Uniform temperature range: T_max - T_min from isotherm maps (UK NA).
  Differential temperature: nonlinear profiles per BS EN 1991-1-5, 6.1.4.

Deck drainage: crossfall >= 1:40 (2.5%). Longitudinal gradient >= 0.5% at sag
  points. Drainage capacity for 1-in-5-year storm (or per client requirement).

Parapet containment level per CD 377 (DMRB):
  N1 (low containment): minor roads, low speed.
  N2 (normal): standard highways.
  H2/H4a (high): motorways, high-speed dual carriageways.
  Parapet must be crash-tested to the specified level — analysis alone is
  insufficient. Anchorage must resist the test load without structural failure.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Aesthetic considerations: depth-to-span ratio, parapet transparency, pier shape.
Construction method: precast segmental vs cast-in-situ vs steel-concrete composite.
  Choice affects temporary works, programme, and traffic management.
Maintenance access: inspection walkways, bearing replacement clearance, painting
  access for steel. Prefer weathering steel (Corten) to reduce repainting needs.
Joint minimisation: integral bridges eliminate expansion joints where feasible
  (typically spans < 60m, skew < 30 degrees).

[Verification procedures]

Load combination completeness: check all groups (gr1a through gr5) including
  thermal + traffic, wind + traffic, accidental (vehicle impact on piers).
Fatigue life: verify that cumulative damage D = SUM(n_i / N_i) <= 1.0 under
  Fatigue Load Model 3 (or 4 for refined analysis), using detail categories
  appropriate to actual weld/connection details — not parent metal defaults.
Bearing travel: confirm specified bearing movement range >= alpha_T * Delta_T * L
  plus construction tolerance. Check rotational capacity at bearings.
Parapet certification: confirm parapet system holds valid crash test certificate
  to the required containment level. Anchorage forces from test report, not
  from simplified analysis.
Reinforcement detailing: verify cover, spacing, lap lengths, and anchorage
  against exposure class and specified design life (typically 120 years).

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a bearing is found near the end of its travel, check the setting temperature recorded at
  installation against the design range before suspecting creep or shrinkage: a bearing set at
  20 degC on a deck designed for -20 to +40 degC must accommodate 20 degC of expansion and 40 degC
  of contraction, and the 2 are not equal.
If a fatigue check fails by a wide margin, confirm the detail category assigned to the connection
  before refining the traffic model. Category 36 for a transverse non-load-carrying attachment
  against Category 160 for plain base metal is a factor of 4.4 in permissible stress range, and at
  m = 3 a factor of about 88 in life.
If a continuous deck shows transverse cracking at regular spacing over a pier, examine the
  differential temperature profile and the restraint moments it generates before the live load
  model; the nonlinear gradients of BS EN 1991-1-5, 6.1.4 commonly govern top-fibre tension there.
If rust staining appears at a joint or a drainage outlet and nowhere else, reconsider the exposure
  class applying at that location rather than the cover specified generally: a leaking joint turns a
  local XC surface into XD3, and the general-surface cover is then the wrong figure for it.
If reactions in a grillage or deck model do not reflect the applied load, check the notional lane
  division and the tandem positions before the element properties. LM1 lanes are notional and are
  positioned to produce the worst effect, so a lane layout copied from the road markings understates
  the load effects.
If a steel or composite bridge returns an unexpected first natural frequency, check the section
  assumptions before adjusting mass: short-term against long-term modular ratio, and whether the
  deck slab was taken as cracked over the pier.
If a parapet anchorage check passes under a simplified static load, obtain the anchorage forces from
  the crash test report. Impact loads at the specified containment level are dynamic and routinely
  exceed the equivalent static figure used for preliminary sizing.
If a pier is governed by accidental vehicle impact, establish whether the client and the relevant
  code require the pier to be designed for impact or to be shielded by a certified barrier; the 2
  routes give very different sections, and choosing wrongly wastes a design cycle.

[Known failure modes]

Fatigue assessed on parent metal while the governing detail is a welded attachment, a cope hole or a
  shear stud — detail category chosen by material rather than by geometry.
Bearing schedules issued without the installation temperature and the construction-stage movements,
  so available travel is consumed before the deck is complete.
Integral construction adopted outside its range of span and skew, with the cyclic ratcheting of
  earth pressure behind the abutment left out of the assessment.
Joint and drainage details that discharge chloride-laden water onto bearing shelves, pier tops and
  half-joints, creating a durability liability no element check reports.
Half-joints and comparable inaccessible details specified at all: they cannot be inspected, and the
  assessment record of existing structures is largely a record of their consequences.
Construction-stage analysis omitted for a composite deck, so the beam is verified only as the
  completed composite section while the critical case is the bare steel beam under wet concrete with
  partial restraint.
Supports taken as rigid in a continuous deck, understating hogging moments, bearing uplift and the
  sensitivity of the design to differential settlement.
Load groups applied selectively: gr1a verified while the horizontal-force group and the
  thermal-with-traffic case are not.
Crossfall achieved by varying surfacing thickness, so both the as-built dead load and the drainage
  path differ from the design.

[Exception catalogue]

The alpha adjustment factors on LM1 are national and route-dependent, and the UK National Annex
  permits reduced values where the traffic is demonstrably lighter — an accommodation bridge with a
  physically enforced weight limit, a private or restricted-access road, a footbridge taking an
  occasional maintenance vehicle. Applying alpha_Q1 = 1.0 with the full 2 x 300 kN tandem there is
  over-design, not a safety requirement. The restriction must be physical and enforced, not advisory.
Fatigue is governed by stress range and cycle count, so it legitimately does not govern a
  reinforced-concrete member held in permanent compression under the fatigue load model, or a detail
  whose computed stress range falls below the constant-amplitude fatigue limit. The verification is
  still performed; the finding that it does not govern is a result, not an omission.
Dynamic amplification is already inside the LM1 and LM2 characteristic values, so adding a separate
  factor double-counts. The real exception runs the other way: a separate dynamic assessment is
  required outside that calibration — very short spans under a single axle, expansion joints and
  bearings taking wheel impact directly, and assessment of existing structures under abnormal
  indivisible loads.
Minimum cover is a provision against the exposure a surface actually sees, so 45 mm at XD3 is a
  typical figure for an exposed UK highway deck and not a floor for every surface on the bridge. A
  soffit permanently enclosed within a box girder, a surface under bonded waterproofing with
  protected drainage, or stainless or galvanised reinforcement each change c_min,dur legitimately.
Bearing travel provision does not apply where there is no bearing to travel: an integral bridge
  accommodates expansion by flexing the abutments and mobilising the backfill, which is why the span
  and skew limits exist. The requirement transfers to abutment flexure and backfill cycling rather
  than disappearing.
Crash-test certification to a containment level applies to vehicle parapets. A pedestrian or cycle
  parapet on a structure with no vehicular access, and a parapet standing behind a separately
  certified vehicle restraint system that takes the impact, are both legitimately outside the CD 377
  containment levels — the geometric, loading and infill requirements for pedestrian protection
  still apply.

[Tool limitations]

A grillage or shell model returns the load effects of the structure as modelled. It cannot establish
  that the lane and tandem positions searched were the governing ones, and an automated
  influence-surface search is only as complete as the set of positions it was given.
Load-combination generators apply code factors to the load cases as named. A thermal case entered as
  a variable action without its psi factors, or a construction-stage case left in the permanent
  group, yields a combination that is arithmetically correct and code-non-compliant, and no check in
  the package detects it.
Fatigue post-processors compute damage from the stress histories at the points requested. They
  cannot see the detail: the same node is Category 160 or Category 36 depending on whether a
  stiffener is welded there, and that is a drawing decision the model does not hold.
Capacity checkers use the section class, effective lengths and restraint conditions supplied to
  them. Values carried through from a template rather than taken from the bracing actually detailed
  are the commonest silent non-conservatism, and the reported utilisation looks precise regardless.
Peak stresses at re-entrant corners and point supports in a finite element model are mesh-dependent
  and do not converge. Quoting one as a fatigue or yield demand reports a property of the mesh; the
  code's detail categories and effective-width rules already embed the real behaviour.

[Limitations]

Does not cover cable-stayed or suspension bridges (different load paths,
  cable fatigue, wind-induced oscillation — specialist analysis required).
Does not cover moveable bridges (bascule, swing, lift).
Not applicable to rail bridges — different load models (LM71, SW/0, SW/2
  per BS EN 1991-2, 6.3) and dynamic factors.
Does not address seismic design (BS EN 1998-2 required separately).
Does not cover substructure/foundation design beyond bearing reactions.
Does not address assessment of existing bridges (CS 454, BD 21/01).
