SOURCES: SI Brochure 9th edition (BIPM, 2019) for units and constants.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Free-body completeness: every force acting on the body must appear, including
  constraint reactions, friction and body forces. An omitted force is the
  single most common source of a wrong answer here, and it is invisible in the
  algebra because the remaining equations are self-consistent.

Newton's third law pairs act on DIFFERENT bodies. A third-law pair never
  appears in the same free-body diagram, and treating it as a cancellation
  within one body is an error that yields zero net force on everything.

Inertial frames: F = ma holds in an inertial frame only. In a rotating frame
  the centrifugal and Coriolis terms are not optional corrections, they are
  part of the equation of motion.

Energy accounting must be closed: kinetic plus potential plus dissipated plus
  work done by external agents. A derivation in which energy appears or
  vanishes has an error, and the magnitude of the imbalance bounds it.

Small-angle approximation (sin θ ≈ θ) must be declared where used and is not
  valid beyond roughly 0.1 rad for 1 per cent accuracy. Carrying it into a
  large-amplitude pendulum is a regime error, not an approximation.

Continuum assumption: the Knudsen number must be small for continuum mechanics
  to apply at all. Below that, the equations do not merely lose accuracy, they
  lose meaning.

Material frame-indifference: a constitutive relation must be independent of
  the observer's frame. A stress-strain law that is not objective will produce
  spurious stresses under rigid rotation.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer the Lagrangian route where constraints are holonomic and numerous; it
  removes the constraint forces from the algebra rather than from the physics.
Prefer dimensionless groups for any scaling argument.
State whether a deformation is treated as small-strain or finite-strain; the
  two disagree by a factor that grows with strain.
Prefer energy methods for equilibrium problems and momentum methods for
  collision problems; the reverse works but costs more algebra.

[Verification procedures]

SymPy for the symbolic equations of motion, with conserved quantities checked
  by differentiating them along the solution and confirming zero.
pint on every force, stress and modulus expression.
Limiting cases: zero friction, infinite mass, zero stiffness. Each should
  reduce the result to a known textbook form.
Numerical integration (scipy.integrate) as a second route against any closed
  form, with energy drift reported -- an integrator that loses energy is
  telling you about the integrator, not the physics.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a reaction force comes out negative, the assumed direction was wrong and
  the magnitude is still right. If it comes out imaginary, a constraint is
  being violated.
A natural frequency that scales as the square root of stiffness over mass is
  almost certainly right in form; check the numerical factor against the
  simplest case.
A result independent of mass in a gravitational problem is usually correct and
  is worth noticing, because it indicates the mass cancelled rather than was
  dropped.
A stress that does not fall off with distance in a 3-dimensional elastic
  problem has a missing geometric factor.

[Known failure modes]

Confusing mass and weight, which balances dimensionally in any expression
  where g appears and so survives a units check.
Using engineering strain where true strain is required, or the reverse, past
  about 5 per cent strain.
Omitting the parallel-axis term when moving a moment of inertia between axes.
Treating a distributed load as a point load at the wrong centroid.
Assuming a pin joint carries no moment and then applying a moment to it.
Applying a static factor of safety to a dynamic or fatigue problem.

[Exception catalogue]

Non-holonomic constraints -- rolling without slipping is the standard case --
  cannot be eliminated by a Lagrangian coordinate choice and need the
  multiplier treatment.
Rigid-body dynamics has no energy dissipation term, so a rigid-body collision
  model must declare its restitution coefficient rather than conserve energy.
Buckling is an instability, not a strength failure: a column can buckle at a
  stress far below yield and no strength check will see it.
Resonance makes a correct steady-state solution useless; a frequency-domain
  result near a natural frequency needs the damping stated.

[Tool limitations]

A symbolic solver will return a valid root that is physically inadmissible --
  a negative length, a speed above c -- and will not flag it. Admissibility is
  the caller's check.
Numerical integrators conserve energy only for symplectic schemes; a
  general-purpose solver will drift and the drift is not a physical effect.
pint cannot distinguish stress from pressure, or energy from torque, because
  each pair shares dimensions. The distinction is physical, not dimensional.

[Limitations]

Does not cover relativistic mechanics beyond flagging the regime boundary.
Does not cover turbulence modelling or any closure problem.
Does not cover fracture mechanics or plasticity beyond yield.
Does not cover finite-element practice; the constraints here are analytical.
