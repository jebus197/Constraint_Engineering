SOURCES: SI Brochure 9th edition (BIPM, 2019). The kelvin is defined by fixing
       the Boltzmann constant at 1.380 649e-23 J/K exactly, which is why

       interchangeable.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Absolute temperature only, in every expression where temperature is not a
  difference. A ratio, a product or an exponent of Celsius temperatures is
  meaningless. This is the most frequent single error in the domain and it
  passes every dimensional check, because Celsius and kelvin share a dimension.

The second law is not negotiable: entropy of an isolated system does not
  decrease. A cycle whose efficiency exceeds the Carnot bound for its reservoir
  temperatures has an error, and the Carnot bound is the cheapest available
  falsifier for any heat-engine result.

Sign conventions must be declared once and held. Work done BY the system and
  work done ON the system differ by a sign that propagates through every
  subsequent step; the first law is stated differently in physics and
  engineering texts and mixing them inverts the answer.

Closure: mass in equals mass out plus accumulation; energy likewise. An open
  system treated as closed drops the enthalpy of the streams, which is
  typically the largest term.

Phase equilibrium requires equality of temperature, pressure AND chemical
  potential of every component in every phase. Two of the three is not
  equilibrium.

Extensive and intensive quantities may not be added. Specific and total
  quantities differ by a mass that is often implicit, and that omission
  balances dimensionally when the mass is numerically near 1.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer enthalpy for flow processes and internal energy for closed rigid
  systems; each removes a term that the other must carry.
State the equation of state in use. Ideal-gas results carry an error that
  grows with reduced pressure, and "approximately ideal" is a claim with a
  number behind it.
Prefer dimensionless groups -- Nusselt, Biot, Fourier -- for heat-transfer
  scaling, and state which one is small.
Report entropy generation rather than efficiency where the comparison is
  between designs; efficiency hides where the loss occurs.

[Verification procedures]

pint for every energy, enthalpy, entropy and heat-capacity expression.
scipy.constants for the gas constant and the Boltzmann and Avogadro constants
  rather than typed literals.
Carnot check on any cycle result: compute the reservoir-temperature bound and
  compare. A result above it is refuted without further analysis.
Closure check by execution: sum the inlet and outlet terms numerically and
  report the residual rather than asserting balance.
SymPy for the symbolic manipulation of the state relations, with the Maxwell
  relations used as a consistency check on any derived partial derivative.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

An efficiency above 1 or above Carnot means a sign error or a temperature in
  the wrong scale; check the scale first because it is more common.
A negative entropy generation means the sign convention flipped mid-derivation.
A heat-transfer coefficient 3 orders from the textbook range for the mechanism
  indicates a wrong characteristic length in the dimensionless group.
If a result is independent of pressure where it should not be, the ideal-gas
  assumption has been applied past its regime.

[Known failure modes]

Celsius where kelvin is required, as above.
Using the gas constant per mole where per unit mass is needed, or the reverse;
  they differ by the molar mass and both are called R.
Mixing higher and lower heating values in a combustion calculation, which
  differ by the latent heat of the product water.
Treating a throttling process as isentropic rather than isenthalpic.
Applying a steady-state relation to a transient and vice versa.
Neglecting the work of expansion against the atmosphere in an open vessel.

[Exception catalogue]

Negative absolute temperatures are physically real in bounded-energy spin
  systems and are HOTTER than any positive temperature, so the usual ordering
  argument inverts. A guard that refuses negative temperature will refuse
  correct work in that narrow case.
The third law makes heat capacity vanish as temperature approaches zero, so a
  constant-heat-capacity model is qualitatively wrong at cryogenic conditions,
  not merely inaccurate.
Near a critical point, derivatives of state variables diverge and every
  linearised relation fails; a result there needs its own treatment.
Metastable states -- superheated liquid, supercooled vapour -- satisfy local
  but not global equilibrium, so an equilibrium calculation will find the
  wrong phase.

[Tool limitations]

pint cannot distinguish specific from molar quantities, because both reduce to
  energy per amount in some unit system; the distinction is physical.
Property libraries interpolate within their validity range and extrapolate
  silently outside it. The range must be checked, not assumed.
A symbolic Maxwell-relation check confirms internal consistency of the state
  relations and says nothing about whether the equation of state fits the
  substance.

[Limitations]

  encodings.
Does not cover computational fluid dynamics or turbulence closure.
Does not cover non-equilibrium thermodynamics beyond entropy generation.
Does not cover cryogenic or plasma regimes in detail.
