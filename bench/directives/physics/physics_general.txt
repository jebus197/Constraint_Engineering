DOMAIN: Physics — General
SCOPE: Measurement, units, dimensional analysis, conservation laws, orders of
       magnitude, error propagation; the constraints common to every physics
       subdiscipline before any specialisation applies
VERSION: 1.0
TIER: DRAFT
SEVERITY SCALE: none. Physics has no single recognised consequence scale across
       its subdisciplines -- a nuclear result inherits INES, an optics result
       may inherit nothing -- so a finding INHERITS the consequence scale of the
       application the physics is being used in. Inventing a scale to fill this
       field is the failure the project calls a magic wand; an honest absence is
       the alternative.
SOURCES: SI Brochure, 9th edition (BIPM, 2019), in force from 20 May 2019.
       Defining-constant values cross-checked against scipy.constants on
       2026-11-12: exact float equality on all 5 and uncertainty 0.0 for each,
       confirming they are exact BY DEFINITION rather than measured.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Dimensional homogeneity: every additive term in an equation must carry the same
  dimensions. A sum of unlike dimensions is not a wrong answer, it is not an
  equation. Check with pint before trusting any derived expression.
  ★ HOMOGENEITY IS NECESSARY AND NOT SUFFICIENT. An expression can balance
  dimensionally and still be wrong -- a missing factor of 2, a sign error, a
  wrong power of a dimensionless ratio all survive the check. A homogeneity
  pass is therefore never evidence of correctness, only the absence of one
  specific disproof.

The 7 SI base quantities are second, metre, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole,
  candela. Any other unit must reduce to these or be declared non-SI
  explicitly with its conversion factor stated.

The 2019 defining constants are EXACT, not measured. Treating any of them as
  carrying uncertainty is a category error:
    c   = 299 792 458 m/s                 (exact)
    h   = 6.626 070 15e-34 J s            (exact, defines the kilogram)
    e   = 1.602 176 634e-19 C             (exact, defines the ampere)
    k   = 1.380 649e-23 J/K               (exact, defines the kelvin)
    N_A = 6.022 140 76e23 /mol            (exact, defines the mole)
  A result quoting an uncertainty on one of these has misunderstood the 2019
  redefinition, in which the kilogram, ampere, kelvin and mole were all
  redefined in terms of fixed constants.

Conservation: energy, momentum, angular momentum, charge and baryon number are
  conserved in every closed system. A derivation that violates one has an
  error upstream of its conclusion, and the violation locates the error.

Significant figures: a result may not carry more precision than its least
  precise input. Stating 7 figures from 2-figure data is a false claim about
  the measurement, not a rounding preference.

Reference frames must be declared. A velocity, an energy or a time interval
  without its frame is incomplete, and quantities from different frames may
  not be combined without an explicit transformation.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer SI throughout; where a field convention differs (electronvolts in
  particle physics, parsecs in astronomy) state the conversion once.
Prefer an order-of-magnitude estimate BEFORE the detailed calculation, so a
  result 3 orders out is caught by arithmetic rather than by review.
Prefer exact symbolic manipulation while the algebra is tractable, and
  substitute numbers once, at the end.
State the regime of validity: non-relativistic, non-quantum, continuum,
  linear-response. A result outside its regime is not approximately right.

[Verification procedures]

pint for every dimensional claim; astropy.units where astronomical units or
  constants are involved. These are the primary route.
scipy.constants for any physical constant, rather than a typed literal. A
  typed constant is a transcription risk with no upside.
SymPy for the symbolic derivation, with limiting cases checked by substitution
  rather than by inspection.
Limiting-case substitution: as a parameter goes to 0 or to infinity, does the
  expression reduce to the known result? A formula that does not is wrong even
  where no step looks wrong.
uncertainties for error propagation, because hand-propagated errors understate
  correlated terms by construction.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

Check the dimensions, then the order of magnitude, then the sign, then the
  limiting cases, and only then the algebra. 3 of those 4 are cheap and catch
  most errors; the algebra is expensive and catches the rest.
A factor of exactly 2, 4 or pi in a discrepancy almost always indicates a
  geometric or averaging convention, not an arithmetic slip -- look for a
  radius against a diameter, an amplitude against an RMS, a solid angle
  against a plane angle.
A discrepancy of exactly 10^3 or 10^-3 is a prefix error, not a physics error.
If a result is independent of a parameter it should depend on, a term has been
  dropped. If it depends on one it should not, a term has been added.

[Known failure modes]

Degrees against radians in any trigonometric argument. Every numerical library
  takes radians; most human statements are in degrees.
Celsius against kelvin in any expression where temperature appears other than
  as a difference. A ratio of Celsius temperatures is meaningless.
Mixing a quantity with its per-unit-mass or per-unit-volume form, which
  balances dimensionally only sometimes and so survives the homogeneity check
  intermittently -- the worst case, because the check passes often enough to
  be trusted.
Treating a defining constant as measured, or quoting a pre-2019 value with an
  uncertainty.
Carrying a small-angle, non-relativistic or linear approximation past the
  point where it holds, without stating that it was used.

[Exception catalogue]

Dimensionless groups -- Reynolds, Mach, Prandtl -- are dimensionless by
  construction, so a homogeneity check on an expression containing them says
  nothing about the physics. Verify the group's own definition instead.
Logarithms and exponentials take dimensionless arguments only. An expression
  with ln(length) has a normalising scale missing, not a units problem.
Angles are dimensionless in SI but not interchangeable with pure numbers in
  every context; a torque per angle and a torque per radian differ by the
  convention, not by the algebra.
Natural-unit systems set c or hbar to 1 deliberately; a dimensional check
  against SI will flag correct natural-unit work as an error.

[Tool limitations]

pint cannot refuse a sum of 2 dimensionless quantities, so it cannot catch a
  nonsensical combination of 2 unrelated dimensionless scales or indices.
  Measured 2026-11-12: a clinical severity grade plus a software severity
  score evaluates happily to a number.
scipy.constants carries CODATA values; where a field uses a convention-fixed
  value instead, they will differ in the last figures and neither is wrong.
SymPy will simplify an expression into a form that is mathematically identical
  and physically opaque; keep the physical grouping when reporting.
A dimensional-analysis pass has nothing to say about a sign error, and sign
  errors are the most common single class in this domain.

[Limitations]

Does not cover any specific subdiscipline's constitutive relations; see the
  classical, thermodynamics and quantum encodings.
Does not cover experimental design, instrument calibration or systematic-error
  budgets.
Does not cover numerical stability of physics simulations; see the numerical
  mathematics encoding.
