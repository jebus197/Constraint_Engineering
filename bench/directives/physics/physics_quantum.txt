[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Normalisation: a state vector must have unit norm, and a density matrix must
  have unit trace and be positive semi-definite. A density matrix with a
  negative eigenvalue is not a state, and the negative eigenvalue locates the
  error.

Hermiticity: an observable is a Hermitian operator. A non-Hermitian operator
  presented as an observable has complex eigenvalues and so no measurement
  outcomes. Anti-Hermitian and non-normal operators have uses, but not as
  observables.

Unitarity: time evolution of a closed system preserves the norm. An evolution
  operator that is not unitary describes an open system, and the environment
  must then be stated rather than ignored.

Commutators determine what is jointly measurable. Asserting simultaneous
  definite values for non-commuting observables is not an approximation, it is
  a contradiction of the formalism.

The uncertainty relation is a theorem about state preparation, NOT a statement
  about instrument disturbance. Conflating the two is the most common
  conceptual error in the domain and it survives every algebraic check.

No-signalling: no local operation on one part of an entangled system changes
  the marginal statistics of the other. Any protocol that appears to send
  information faster than light has an error, and the error is almost always a
  dropped normalisation or a post-selection treated as a marginal.

A Bell inequality violation refutes LOCAL HIDDEN VARIABLES under its own
  stated assumptions. Which assumption is given up -- locality, measurement
  independence, outcome definiteness -- is an interpretational choice and the
  experiment does not make it. A claim that the experiment settles it is
  overreach, and stating the assumption set is mandatory.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer the density-matrix formalism wherever the system may be mixed or open;
  state vectors silently assume purity.
State the basis. A statement about superposition is basis-relative and means
  nothing without one.
Prefer natural units with hbar = 1 for the algebra and restore SI once, at the
  end, where a numerical answer is wanted.
Distinguish interpretational claims from formal ones explicitly. Both are
  legitimate; conflating them is not.

[Verification procedures]

SymPy for the operator algebra, with commutators computed rather than recalled.
NumPy for explicit finite-dimensional cases: build the matrices, check
  Hermiticity, unitarity and the trace numerically. A 2-level or 4-level
  explicit case refutes most algebra errors in seconds.
scipy.constants for hbar and any physical constant.
Eigenvalue check: a Hermitian operator must return real eigenvalues
  numerically. If it does not, it is not Hermitian whatever the algebra said.
No-signalling check by execution: compute the reduced density matrix before
  and after the remote operation and compare. They must be identical.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

A probability above 1 or below 0 means a normalisation was dropped, nearly
  always at a partial trace or a basis change.
A result that depends on the ORDER of 2 commuting operations has an algebra
  error; one that depends on the order of non-commuting operations is correct
  and expected.
If an entanglement measure comes out non-zero for a product state, the partial
  trace was taken over the wrong subsystem.
A Bell parameter above 2*sqrt(2) exceeds the Tsirelson bound and is refuted by
  quantum mechanics itself, not merely by experiment.

[Known failure modes]

Treating the uncertainty relation as a disturbance claim.
Taking a partial trace over the wrong factor, which produces a valid-looking
  density matrix with the wrong physics.
Confusing a mixed state with a superposition; they have identical diagonals in
  one basis and different off-diagonals, so a check that reads only
  probabilities cannot tell them apart.
Post-selecting a subensemble and reporting its statistics as unconditional,
  which manufactures apparent signalling.
Assuming the collapse postulate is dynamical and then asking when it occurs,
  which is an interpretational question presented as a physical one.
Using a tensor-product ordering convention inconsistently between operators
  and states.

[Exception catalogue]

Weak values can lie outside the eigenvalue spectrum of their observable. A
  guard that refuses an out-of-spectrum value will refuse correct weak-value
  work.
Non-Hermitian Hamiltonians are legitimate for open and PT-symmetric systems,
  where the norm is not conserved by design, so a unitarity check will flag
  correct work.
Superdeterminism and retrocausal accounts deny measurement independence rather
  than locality, so a Bell argument stated as "locality is refuted" does not
  address them. This is precisely the frontier task's subject and the
  distinction must be preserved rather than flattened.
Relational and QBist accounts make outcome definiteness observer-relative, so
  a check that assumes a single global outcome set will mislabel them.

[Tool limitations]

A numerical eigensolver returns eigenvalues with a small imaginary part for a
  Hermitian matrix purely from floating-point error. The threshold must be
  stated, not eyeballed, and it is of order the machine epsilon times the
  matrix norm.
Symbolic operator algebra will not enforce domain questions for unbounded
  operators; position and momentum are unbounded and the formal manipulation
  is silent about where it fails.
No tool available here can adjudicate an interpretational claim, and the
  honest response to one is to state the assumption set rather than to score
  it. Marking such a claim as verified would be the project's own
  flattering-measure failure.

[Limitations]

Does not cover quantum field theory or relativistic quantum mechanics.
Does not cover quantum computing circuit practice or error correction.
Does not cover many-body or condensed-matter methods.
Does not adjudicate between interpretations, by design.
