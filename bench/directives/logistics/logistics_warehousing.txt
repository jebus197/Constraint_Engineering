[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Conservation of stock: opening balance plus receipts minus issues minus losses
  equals closing balance. A reconciliation that does not close has an error,
  and the magnitude of the gap bounds it. Unexplained shrinkage is a finding,
  not a rounding.

Units of measure must be consistent across the whole chain: each, case, pallet,
  layer. A quantity without its unit of measure is the dominant source of
  inventory error, and the ratios between them are site-specific.

Capacity constraints are physical: volume, floor loading, rack rating, aisle
  clearance. A plan exceeding any of them is not optimistic, it is not
  executable.

Stock rotation discipline must be stated -- first-in-first-out, first-expired-
  first-out -- and the 2 differ whenever receipt order and expiry order differ,
  which is the normal case for anything dated.

Lead time and demand variability both drive safety stock. A safety stock
  computed from demand variability alone understates by the lead-time
  contribution.

A count is a measurement with an error rate. Inventory accuracy must be stated
  as a measured figure with its method -- full count, cycle count, sample --
  and not asserted.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer reporting accuracy by value and by line, since the 2 diverge sharply.
Prefer cycle counting over annual full counts for anything moving.
State whether a throughput figure is sustained or peak.
Prefer slotting by velocity, and state the velocity window used.
Report capacity utilisation with its denominator: usable, gross, or rated.

[Verification procedures]

Reconcile by execution: compute the balance identity and report the residual
  rather than asserting closure.
pint for any volume, mass or floor-loading expression, with the unit of
  measure carried explicitly.
Check every unit-of-measure conversion against the site's own table, by
  execution, not by recall.
For any accuracy claim, report the count method, the sample size and an
  interval.
Simulate the service level against demand and lead-time distributions rather
  than applying a formula whose assumptions are unstated.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a reconciliation closes exactly, check whether an adjustment was posted to
  make it close.
If accuracy by line is high and by value is low, the errors are concentrated in
  high-value items, which is the expensive direction.
If utilisation exceeds 100 per cent, the denominator is rated rather than
  usable.
If a pick rate is far above the industry range, check whether travel time is
  included.
If demand looks stationary, check for seasonality before sizing stock.

[Known failure modes]

Mixing units of measure between systems.
Treating an inventory count as exact.
Posting adjustments to force a reconciliation, which destroys the evidence of
  the discrepancy.
Sizing safety stock from demand variability alone.
Confusing first-in-first-out with first-expired-first-out for dated stock.
Quoting peak throughput as sustained capacity.
Ignoring floor loading because volume fits.

[Exception catalogue]

Consignment and bonded stock may be physically present and not owned, so a
  physical count legitimately exceeds the balance sheet.
Negative inventory is a system artefact and never physical; it indicates a
  sequencing error in postings rather than a quantity.
Some losses are expected and budgeted -- evaporation, breakage -- and reporting
  them as shrinkage misattributes them.
Kitted and assembled items exist in 2 states, and double-counting components
  and kits is easy.

[Tool limitations]

An inventory system enforces its own unit-of-measure table and cannot detect
  that the table is wrong.
pint cannot catch a case-to-each conversion error, because both are
  dimensionless counts.
A simulation's service level depends entirely on the assumed demand
  distribution, which is usually fitted to too little data.
No tool detects an adjustment posted to force a reconciliation; only the audit
  trail does.

[Limitations]

Does not cover cold chain; see the cold-chain encoding.
Does not cover maritime or freight; see those encodings.
Does not cover transport routing or network design.
Does not cover any jurisdiction's distribution-practice requirements.
