Domain: Maritime Shipping and Container Logistics
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-03-14
References: SOLAS (VGM amendment 2016), IMDG Code, CSS Code, ISM Code, Suez Canal Authority regulations

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. Vessel draft must not exceed channel, port, or berth limits at any point in the
   rotation. Key constraints: Suez Canal new canal max 20.1m/66ft for widest beam
   classes, original canal ~16m/~52ft (beam-dependent — consult SCA Rules of Navigation),
   Panama Canal Neopanamax locks 15.2m TFW, Singapore Strait ~21m, Malacca Strait
   ~20.5m. Draft changes with fuel consumption, ballast, and cargo operations.
2. Container weight limits per ISO 668/ISO 1496-1: 20ft and 40ft standard max gross
   mass = 30,480 kg. Individual bay stack weight limits per vessel's bay plan and
   Classification Society approval — exceeding stack weight risks structural failure.
3. IMO Dangerous Goods (IMDG Code): the segregation table (Chapter 7.2) governs
   which hazmat classes may share container, hold, or on-deck positions. Violations
   are criminal offences in most jurisdictions. Stowage category must match.
4. SOLAS VGM (Verified Gross Mass): since 1 July 2016, every packed container must
   have a verified gross mass before vessel loading. Bill of Lading must accurately
   state cargo weight. Non-compliance = container refused for loading.
5. Transit time realism: Suez Canal transit ~12-16 hours (not days). Shanghai to
   Rotterdam via Suez ~25-30 sailing days at 14-16 knots (not 14 days). Cape of
   Good Hope adds ~8-10 days. Any model using unrealistic transit times is wrong.
6. Bunker fuel consumption scales approximately with v^3 (cubic law from hull
   resistance physics). Slow steaming from 24kn to 18kn reduces consumption by
   ~58% per day (though voyage duration increases). Optimum speed balances fuel
   cost against time cost and charter rate.
7. Demurrage/despatch: laytime calculation method must be specified in the charter
   party. SHINC (Sundays and Holidays Included), SHEX (Sundays and Holidays
   Excluded), WWD (Weather Working Days) yield materially different results.
   Demurrage accrues from laytime expiry at the contractual daily rate.
8. Cargo securing per CSS Code: lashing forces must exceed calculated forces from
   ship motion accelerations (roll, pitch, heave). Under-secured cargo = structural
   damage, loss of life, or loss of vessel.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
- Prefer FCL over LCL for high-value cargo (reduces handling, damage, and pilferage risk).
- Prefer direct service over transhipment where schedule permits (each tranship adds 1-3 days + handling risk).
- Target 2-day port buffer for weather delays and congestion at major hub ports.
- Prefer slot booking with schedule reliability data (carrier OTDP benchmarks).

[Verification procedures]
- Verify vessel draft against ALL ports and channels in the rotation, not just origin/destination.
  Account for intermediate bunkering, ballast changes, and tidal windows.
- Check that transit time estimates use realistic service speed (12-18 knots), not maximum speed.
- Confirm IMDG segregation compliance for every DG container pair in the stowage plan.
- Verify container stack weights do not exceed limits for the specific bay and tier position.
- Check that VGM documentation is complete before cut-off.
- Validate ETA calculations include canal waiting time (Suez convoy scheduling, Panama slot booking).

[Diagnostic heuristics]
- If the draft check passes at every port, check the water density at each berth
  before accepting the departure condition. The Panama Neopanamax 15.2m figure is
  tropical fresh water, and a vessel floats deeper in fresh water than its salt-water
  draft suggests.
- If a stowage plan reports no IMDG conflict, check the segregation requirement
  against the actual bay, row and tier positions rather than the class pairs alone.
  "Away from" is satisfiable inside a single hold; "separated from" is not, and the
  decisive input is distance, not class.
- If a voyage fuel figure looks low, check whether the speed used is service speed or
  design speed, then check the displacement the cubic law was applied at. A laden leg
  and a ballast leg at the same speed are not the same consumption.
- If demurrage is disputed, check the Notice of Readiness tender time and its validity
  before re-doing the laytime arithmetic. An invalid NOR moves the start of laytime
  and makes every figure after it wrong.
- If a declared VGM differs from the terminal weighbridge by a few per cent, check the
  tare stamped on the container's CSC safety approval plate against the tare used in
  the Method 2 calculation. A repaired or re-plated box carries a tare the shipper's
  static data does not hold.
- If a 25-day Shanghai to Rotterdam schedule shows no slack, check canal convoy and
  slot timing before blaming port productivity. Suez runs scheduled convoys and Panama
  runs booked slots, so arriving early is waiting, not saving.
- If lashing calculations pass, check the accelerations against the actual GM and the
  position along the ship. CSS Code Annex 13 accelerations rise with metacentric
  height, so a stiff ship gives larger transverse accelerations, not smaller.

[Known failure modes]
- Salt-water draft compared against a fresh-water limit, or the reverse.
- Tare taken from static master data rather than from the CSC plate.
- IMDG segregation assessed by class pair alone, ignoring stowage position and the
  on-deck-only stowage category.
- Design or maximum speed used in place of service speed in a transit estimate.
- Laytime counted from arrival rather than from a valid NOR plus the contractual
  notice period.
- SHINC and SHEX interchanged, or weather working days excluded with no weather record
  to support the exclusion.
- 30,480 kg treated as payload when it is maximum gross mass, so a 40ft box's usable
  payload is nearer 26,500 kg once tare is deducted.
- Transhipment modelled as a single handover rather than 2 port calls with 2 cut-offs
  and 2 chances of a rollover.
- Slow steaming modelled as a saving per voyage when the cubic law governs consumption
  per day and the voyage gets longer.

[Exception catalogue]
- SOLAS VGM applies to containers packed for carriage by sea on an international
  voyage. Domestic and cabotage movements, and ro-ro trailers and chassis-mounted
  units carried on their own wheels, fall outside it. Demanding a VGM for a ro-ro
  trailer refuses correct work.
- Published draft maxima are not constants. A berth may lawfully take a deeper draft
  inside a tidal window, and permissible Suez draft is a function of beam read from
  the SCA table, so a draft above the figure quoted for the widest beam class can be
  entirely lawful.
- The 30,480 kg ISO limit binds the box, not the journey. On the land leg the
  operative limit is the national axle-weight and gross-combination limit, so a
  lawfully packed container is frequently unlawful to move by road without a permit or
  a rail leg.
- IMDG segregation is relaxed by the Code's own provisions: limited and excepted
  quantities are exempt from most of it, and Class 1 explosives are governed by the
  compatibility-group table rather than by the general segregation table.
- Laytime and demurrage exist only under a voyage charter. A liner booking or a
  berth-terms contract of carriage carries neither, so demurrage arithmetic applied to
  a liner shipment is a category error rather than a calculation error; carrier
  detention and terminal storage are different charges on different terms.
- The cubic consumption law holds inside the hull's design speed band. Below the main
  engine's minimum safe continuous load the hotel and auxiliary load dominates,
  specific consumption rises, and engine makers require de-rating, so "slower is
  always cheaper" is false at the bottom of the range.

[Tool limitations]
- A stowage planning system validates against the declared commodity and the declared
  weight. It cannot detect a mis-declaration, which is the dominant cause of container
  fires, and an IMDG check on cargo declared as machinery parts returns clean.
- An AIS-derived ETA is the destination and ETA field typed on the bridge. It is a
  statement about what was entered, frequently stale, and not a computed arrival.
- Weather routing works on a grid of tens of kilometres and hours, so it
  systematically under-represents the short, steep seas that produce parametric
  rolling and the largest lashing loads.
- A loading computer checks stability and hull-girder strength for the condition
  entered, and that condition is assembled from declared weights. Its pass is
  conditional on a declaration it cannot audit.
- Consumption models calibrated on sea trials assume a clean hull and a trim band.
  Fouling adds materially across a docking cycle, and no noon-report model separates
  fouling from weather without a long baseline.

[Limitations]
- Does not cover tanker operations (VLCC, chemical, LNG/LPG carriers).
- Does not cover dry bulk cargo (grain trimming, ore density, liquefaction risk).
- Does not cover passenger vessel regulations (SOLAS Chapter II-1 subdivision).
- Does not cover inland waterway transport or short-sea shipping.
- Does not cover naval architecture or vessel design.
