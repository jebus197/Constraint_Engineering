Domain: Maritime Shipping and Container Logistics
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-03-14
References: SOLAS (VGM amendment 2016), IMDG Code, CSS Code, ISM Code, Suez Canal Authority regulations

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. Vessel draft must not exceed channel, port, or berth limits at any point in the
   rotation. Key constraints: Suez Canal max 16.0m (varies by beam — SCA tables),
   Panama Canal Neopanamax locks 15.2m TFW, Singapore Strait ~21m, Malacca Strait
   ~20.5m. Draft changes with fuel consumption, ballast, and cargo operations.
2. Container weight limits per ISO 668/ISO 1496-1: 20ft and 40ft standard max gross
   mass = 30,480 kg. Individual bay stack weight limits per vessel's bay plan and
   Classification Society approval — exceeding stack weight risks structural failure.
3. IMO Dangerous Goods (IMDG Code): the segregation table (Chapter 7.2) governs
   which hazmat classes may share container, hold, or on-deck positions. Violations
   are criminal offences in most jurisdictions. Stowage category must match.
4. SOLAS VGM (Verified Gross Mass): since 1 July 2016, every packed container must
   have a verified gross mass before vessel loading. Bill of Lading must accurately
   state cargo weight. Non-compliance = container refused for loading.
5. Transit time realism: Suez Canal transit ~12-16 hours (not days). Shanghai to
   Rotterdam via Suez ~25-30 sailing days at 14-16 knots (not 14 days). Cape of
   Good Hope adds ~8-10 days. Any model using unrealistic transit times is wrong.
6. Bunker fuel consumption scales approximately with v^3 (cubic law from hull
   resistance physics). Slow steaming from 24kn to 18kn reduces consumption by
   ~58% per day (though voyage duration increases). Optimum speed balances fuel
   cost against time cost and charter rate.
7. Demurrage/despatch: laytime calculation method must be specified in the charter
   party. SHINC (Sundays and Holidays Included), SHEX (Sundays and Holidays
   Excluded), WWD (Weather Working Days) yield materially different results.
   Demurrage accrues from laytime expiry at the contractual daily rate.
8. Cargo securing per CSS Code: lashing forces must exceed calculated forces from
   ship motion accelerations (roll, pitch, heave). Under-secured cargo = structural
   damage, loss of life, or loss of vessel.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
- Prefer FCL over LCL for high-value cargo (reduces handling, damage, and pilferage risk).
- Prefer direct service over transhipment where schedule permits (each tranship adds 1-3 days + handling risk).
- Target 2-day port buffer for weather delays and congestion at major hub ports.
- Prefer slot booking with schedule reliability data (carrier OTDP benchmarks).

[Verification procedures]
- Verify vessel draft against ALL ports and channels in the rotation, not just origin/destination.
  Account for intermediate bunkering, ballast changes, and tidal windows.
- Check that transit time estimates use realistic service speed (12-18 knots), not maximum speed.
- Confirm IMDG segregation compliance for every DG container pair in the stowage plan.
- Verify container stack weights do not exceed limits for the specific bay and tier position.
- Check that VGM documentation is complete before cut-off.
- Validate ETA calculations include canal waiting time (Suez convoy scheduling, Panama slot booking).

[Limitations]
- Does not cover tanker operations (VLCC, chemical, LNG/LPG carriers).
- Does not cover dry bulk cargo (grain trimming, ore density, liquefaction risk).
- Does not cover passenger vessel regulations (SOLAS Chapter II-1 subdivision).
- Does not cover inland waterway transport or short-sea shipping.
- Does not cover naval architecture or vessel design.
