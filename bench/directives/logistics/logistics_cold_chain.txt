Domain: Temperature-Controlled Logistics (Cold Chain)
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-03-14
References: EU GDP 2013/C 343/01, WHO PQS, Codex Alimentarius, ISTA 7E, USP <1079>

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. Temperature excursions are cumulative and irreversible for most biologics and
   foods. Total integrated time-out-of-range (TTOR) determines product viability,
   not just instantaneous temperature. A product that spent 4 hours at +12C across
   three separate events is as damaged as one continuous 4-hour excursion.
2. EU Good Distribution Practice (GDP) per 2013/C 343/01: continuous temperature
   monitoring is mandatory throughout the pharmaceutical distribution chain.
   Monitoring gaps are regulatory non-compliance regardless of whether an excursion
   occurred. USP <1079> provides equivalent US guidance.
3. Reefer container set point != actual cargo temperature. Reefer units cool the
   air, not the cargo directly. Cargo must be pre-cooled to target temperature
   BEFORE loading. Loading warm cargo into a cold reefer causes condensation,
   uneven cooling, and potential product damage. Pre-cooling verification is
   mandatory.
4. Frozen goods: maintain at or below -18C throughout the chain. Temperature must
   not rise above -15C at any point during transport (Codex Alimentarius, Quick
   Frozen Foods standard). For ice cream: -18C with zero tolerance. Partial thaw
   and refreeze causes textural degradation and potential microbial risk.
5. Vaccines (WHO PQS requirements): transport and storage at +2C to +8C. Freeze-
   sensitive vaccines (e.g., DTP, Hep B, IPV) must NEVER reach 0C or below —
   freezing destroys the adjuvant and renders the vaccine ineffective with no
   visible indication. This is a silent failure mode.
6. Door-opening protocol: each door opening is a discrete thermal ingress event.
   Infiltration heat load Q_inf = rho * V_exchange * c_p * delta_T per opening.
   Cumulative effect of multiple openings (multi-drop delivery) must be modelled
   in thermal budget. A 10-drop route is not thermally equivalent to a 2-drop route.
7. Phase change materials (PCM) / eutectic plates: thermal energy budget
   Q = m * L (mass * latent heat of fusion). Duration of temperature maintenance
   = Q / q_ingress where q_ingress = heat ingress rate (W). Undersizing PCM mass
   for the journey duration causes excursion. Ambient temperature and insulation
   R-value determine q_ingress.
8. Backup power for cold storage facilities: diesel generator with automatic
   transfer switch (ATS) is mandatory. Target switchover time < 30 seconds.
   UPS required for monitoring and alarm systems to maintain visibility during
   transfer. Loss of monitoring during power failure is unacceptable.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
- Prefer active (mechanically refrigerated) over passive (insulated + PCM) for journeys > 48h.
- Prefer data loggers with real-time cellular/satellite telemetry over download-at-destination.
- Target < 2% product loss from temperature excursions across the network.
- Prefer qualified shipping lanes (ISTA 7E or equivalent thermal profile data) over unqualified.

[Verification procedures]
- Verify thermal mapping qualification exists for the specific route AND season (winter != summer).
- Check monitors are calibrated (annual min) and placed at worst-case positions (door end, top).
- Confirm contingency plan for refrigeration failure with max safe hold time per product category.
- Verify pre-cooling records: cargo temp at loading must be documented.
- Check PCM conditioning (freeze/thaw activation) completed — unconditioned PCM = zero protection.

[Limitations]
- Does not cover cryogenic transport (LN2 -196C, dry ice -78.5C) or organ transport.
- Does not cover controlled atmosphere (CA) for fresh produce (O2/CO2/N2 management).
- Does not cover ultra-low temperature storage (-80C, mRNA vaccines) or cell/gene therapies.
