Domain: Temperature-Controlled Logistics (Cold Chain)
Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-03-14
References: EU GDP 2013/C 343/01, WHO PQS, Codex Alimentarius, ISTA 7E, USP <1079>

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. Temperature excursions are cumulative and irreversible for most biologics and
   foods. Total integrated time-out-of-range (TTOR) determines product viability,
   not just instantaneous temperature. A product that spent 4 hours at +12C across
   three separate events is as damaged as one continuous 4-hour excursion.
2. EU Good Distribution Practice (GDP) per 2013/C 343/01: continuous temperature
   monitoring is mandatory throughout the pharmaceutical distribution chain.
   Monitoring gaps are regulatory non-compliance regardless of whether an excursion
   occurred. USP <1079> provides equivalent US guidance.
3. Reefer container set point != actual cargo temperature. Reefer units cool the
   air, not the cargo directly. Cargo must be pre-cooled to target temperature
   BEFORE loading. Loading warm cargo into a cold reefer causes condensation,
   uneven cooling, and potential product damage. Pre-cooling verification is
   mandatory.
4. Frozen goods: maintain at or below -18C throughout the chain. Temperature must
   not rise above -15C at any point during transport (Codex Alimentarius, Quick
   Frozen Foods standard). For ice cream: -18C with zero tolerance. Partial thaw
   and refreeze causes textural degradation and potential microbial risk.
5. Vaccines (WHO PQS requirements): transport and storage at +2C to +8C. Freeze-
   sensitive vaccines (e.g., DTP, Hep B, IPV) must NEVER reach 0C or below —
   freezing destroys the adjuvant and renders the vaccine ineffective with no
   visible indication. This is a silent failure mode.
6. Door-opening protocol: each door opening is a discrete thermal ingress event.
   Infiltration heat load Q_inf = rho * V_exchange * c_p * delta_T per opening.
   Cumulative effect of multiple openings (multi-drop delivery) must be modelled
   in thermal budget. A 10-drop route is not thermally equivalent to a 2-drop route.
7. Phase change materials (PCM) / eutectic plates: thermal energy budget
   Q = m * L (mass * latent heat of fusion). Duration of temperature maintenance
   = Q / q_ingress where q_ingress = heat ingress rate (W). Undersizing PCM mass
   for the journey duration causes excursion. Ambient temperature and insulation
   R-value determine q_ingress.
8. Backup power for cold storage facilities: diesel generator with automatic
   transfer switch (ATS) is mandatory. Target switchover time < 30 seconds.
   UPS required for monitoring and alarm systems to maintain visibility during
   transfer. Loss of monitoring during power failure is unacceptable.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
- Prefer active (mechanically refrigerated) over passive (insulated + PCM) for journeys > 48h.
- Prefer data loggers with real-time cellular/satellite telemetry over download-at-destination.
- Target < 2% product loss from temperature excursions across the network.
- Prefer qualified shipping lanes (ISTA 7E or equivalent thermal profile data) over unqualified.

[Verification procedures]
- Verify thermal mapping qualification exists for the specific route AND season (winter != summer).
- Check monitors are calibrated (annual min) and placed at worst-case positions (door end, top).
- Confirm contingency plan for refrigeration failure with max safe hold time per product category.
- Verify pre-cooling records: cargo temp at loading must be documented.
- Check PCM conditioning (freeze/thaw activation) completed — unconditioned PCM = zero protection.

[Diagnostic heuristics]
- If an excursion report shows every logged point within limits but the product fails
  release, check the logging interval and the probe position before the vehicle. A
  15-minute interval cannot resolve a 4-minute door-open spike, and a probe buried in
  the pallet core will not see it at all.
- If a temperature trace is unnaturally flat, with no diurnal ripple at all, check
  whether the reading comes from the reefer's return-air sensor rather than a cargo
  probe. Return air is a duct temperature and tracks the set point by design.
- If a freeze-sensitive vaccine shows no excursion but fails potency, check the
  recorded MINIMUM and the latching freeze indicator, never the mean. A 20-minute dip
  below 0C disappears into an average taken over a 10-day shipment.
- If a passive shipper qualified on an ISTA 7E summer profile fails in winter, check
  the direction of failure first. Winter failures are usually the PCM freezing the
  payload — packs conditioned at -20C against a +2C to +8C product — not insufficient
  cooling capacity.
- If cargo arrives warm while the reefer logged its set point throughout, check the
  air path before the unit. Stacking above the red load line, or pallets blocking the
  T-floor channels, defeats circulation while the unit reports correctly.
- If an excursion appears on the first leg only and never repeats, check the
  pre-cooling record and the loading duration before suspecting the vehicle. Warm
  cargo takes hours to pull down and the ingress is a single load event.
- If a mean kinetic temperature calculation clears a shipment whose trace breached the
  upper limit, check whether MKT is admissible for that product at all. MKT is a
  storage statistic for stability assessment and does not license a transport
  excursion against a hard limit.

[Known failure modes]
- Probe placed in the free air stream rather than at the mapped warmest and coldest
  load positions.
- Logger started after loading, so the pull-down excursion is never recorded.
- Frozen gel packs used for a +2C to +8C payload, which freezes the product.
- PCM mass sized for nominal ambient rather than the lane's seasonal extreme.
- Reefer set point cited as evidence of cargo temperature.
- A monitoring gap at a carrier handover reported as "no excursion".
- Alarm thresholds set at the product limit with no margin, so every alarm arrives
  after the breach rather than before it.
- Partial thaw and refreeze reported as compliant because the arrival temperature is
  in range.
- Logger accuracy (typically +/-0.5C) ignored against a 6C-wide window, and
  calibration drift left uncorrected.

[Exception catalogue]
- Lyophilised (freeze-dried) vaccines — BCG, measles, yellow fever — are not
  freeze-sensitive before reconstitution and may legitimately be shipped and stored
  frozen. The never-below-0C rule attaches to liquid adjuvanted vaccines and to the
  accompanying diluent, which must not freeze.
- Where the marketing authorisation holder holds an approved stability budget derived
  from ICH Q1A data, a documented time-out-of-range within that budget does not render
  the product non-viable. The TTOR accounting still applies; the disposition decision
  belongs to the authorisation holder, not to the carrier.
- Controlled room temperature product (+15C to +25C) sits within GDP scope but has no
  cold chain, and refrigerating it can itself cause damage, crystallisation in
  suspensions being the usual example. The +2C to +8C window is product-specific, not
  a default for everything GDP covers.
- Continuous per-consignment logging is a GDP expectation for temperature-sensitive
  medicines, and national competent authorities differ on ambient and oral solid dose
  product, where a qualified lane profile plus vehicle mapping is accepted in place of
  a logger in every box. Check the destination regime before declaring a gap.
- A standby generator is not a universal requirement. Where a documented
  maximum-safe-hold analysis shows the room holds its range longer than the longest
  credible outage with doors closed, the control is hold time and door discipline. EU
  GDP names no generator; specific national rules and WHO PQS facility requirements
  do.

[Tool limitations]
- A data logger reports the temperature at the probe, not of the product. Without a
  thermal mapping study relating probe position to the extreme load points, a
  compliant trace is evidence about 1 point in the load.
- Mean kinetic temperature collapses a trace to a single figure and is mathematically
  incapable of showing that a freeze event occurred, so it can never clear a
  freeze-sensitive product.
- Thermal models calibrated on an empty-chamber or full-payload qualification
  over-predict hold time for a part load, which carries less thermal mass and more
  free air volume than the qualification run.
- Latching freeze indicators record that a threshold was crossed, not for how long, so
  they cannot separate a transient surface dip from a frozen core.
- Calibration certificates state accuracy at the points actually calibrated, usually
  0C and +25C. Accuracy at -20C or -70C is extrapolation unless the certificate covers
  that point explicitly.

[Limitations]
- Does not cover cryogenic transport (LN2 -196C, dry ice -78.5C) or organ transport.
- Does not cover controlled atmosphere (CA) for fresh produce (O2/CO2/N2 management).
- Does not cover ultra-low temperature storage (-80C, mRNA vaccines) or cell/gene therapies.
