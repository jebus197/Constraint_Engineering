DOMAIN: Cross-Domain — General
SCOPE: Any problem spanning 2 or more STEM domains, including meta-reasoning
       about method, protocol and calibration. The general box for composing
       the constraints and the scales of 2 or more declared domains.
VERSION: 1.0
TIER: DRAFT
SEVERITY SCALE: composed. By construction a cross-domain finding carries 2 or
       more scales.
SOURCES: The composition rule below is the founder's, 2026-11-12, and is
       quoted verbatim because paraphrasing it would change it.

★ WHY THIS ENCODING EXISTS. The 3 existing cross-domain boxes answer 3 specific
PAIRS -- mechanical-electrical, software-hardware, thermal-electrical. The March
2026 analysis found that 2 of the 5 cross-domain frontier tasks were
META-REASONING problems about proof protocols and calibration, "no
mechanical-electrical content at all", and they were given the
mechanical-electrical box because it sorts first alphabetically. This is the
general box that was missing, and it is the declared default.

★★★ THE COMPOSITION RULE, HIS WORDS, VERBATIM:

  "where two (or more) quantities across domains don't compose, the solution is
  straightforward enough, which is to instruct the models to quote both
  quantiles in their own specific context. Just as you said earlier today a sum
  written as 1 inch + 1 cm (or similar) is both nonsensical and they do not
  compose. We should not expect potentially more complex examples to compose in
  this case either. (The only exception being where a valid conversion between
  any two quantities may already formally exist.)"

VERIFIED WITH pint, 2026-11-12. Where a formal conversion exists it composes:
1 inch + 1 cm = 3.540000 cm, with SymPy's exact 127/50 and pint agreeing to
0.000e+00. Where none exists it is refused: 1 m + 1 s and 1 K + 1 V both raise.
BUT THE SEVERITY CASE IS NOT COVERED BY pint, and this is the operative
limitation: a clinical grade and a software score are both DIMENSIONLESS, so
`CTCAE 3 + CVSS 7.0` evaluates happily to 10.0. Dimensional analysis has
nothing to refuse. The refusal must therefore be on the SCALE NAME, which means
the name must be carried with every number.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

REPORT EACH QUANTITY IN ITS OWN SCALE unless a formally defined conversion
  between the 2 scales already exists. Do not average, sum, or otherwise
  combine 2 scales into 1 number. 2 numbers, each with its scale named, is the
  correct output and is not a failure to answer.

Every domain in scope must be DECLARED, and each contributes its own
  constraints in full. A cross-domain problem does not relax either domain's
  hard constraints; it is bound by the union of them.

Where 2 domains' constraints CONFLICT, the conflict is the finding. Report it
  rather than resolving it by preference, and name which domain each side comes
  from.

The interface is where cross-domain errors live. Units, sign conventions,
  sampling rates, coordinate frames and reference states must be stated at
  every boundary, because each domain has its own defaults and the defaults
  disagree silently.

A conversion that exists formally must be cited, not assumed. "These are both
  measures of severity" is not a conversion.

Where 1 domain declares a scale and the other declares none, there is nothing
  to convert between and the composed report is the only honest output.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer naming the dominant domain for the decision while reporting both scales.
Prefer a dimensionless formulation at the interface where one exists.
Prefer stating which domain's constraint binds first, so a reader can see the
  order of attack.
Where the problem is meta-reasoning about method rather than about a physical
  system, say so explicitly -- the domain boxes then apply to the method, not
  to a substance.

[Verification procedures]

pint for every dimensional quantity crossing a boundary, and an explicit check
  for whether a formal conversion exists before composing anything.
The declared scale name, carried with every severity, as the arbiter for
  ordinal scales -- because pint cannot refuse their combination.
Check the union of both domains' verification procedures, not the intersection.
For any interface, round-trip a quantity across it and back, and report the
  residual.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a composed number is suspiciously interpretable, 2 scales were probably
  averaged.
If a result is insensitive to one of the declared domains, either that domain
  is not actually in scope or its contribution was dropped.
If 2 domains give the same answer by different routes, that is the strongest
  evidence available here.
If a conflict resolves too easily, check whether one side's constraint was
  quietly relaxed.

[Known failure modes]

Averaging or summing 2 ordinal scales, which no dimensional check refuses.
Applying only the first-named domain's constraints.
Assuming a conversion exists because both quantities share a name.
Dropping a unit at an interface, where each side's default differs.
Treating a meta-reasoning task as a physical one and applying a substance box
  to it, which is exactly what the 2026-03 analysis found happening.
Resolving a genuine inter-domain conflict by preference and reporting a single
  answer.

[Exception catalogue]

Formally defined conversions DO compose: SI prefix changes, inch to
  centimetre, calorie to joule, and any conversion with a defining standard
  behind it.
Where both quantities are on the SAME named scale they compose trivially,
  which is the only case where severities combine.
A domain declaring no scale of its own inherits the application's, so 2 such
  domains in the same application may share a scale after all -- check before
  refusing.
Some cross-domain problems reduce to one domain once non-dimensionalised, and
  then the single-domain box is correct.

[Tool limitations]

pint refuses dimensional mismatches and CANNOT refuse a combination of 2
  dimensionless scales. This is the central limitation of the domain and the
  reason the scale name is carried as data.
No tool enumerates which conversions formally exist; that is a standards
  question and must be cited.
A unit library cannot detect a sign-convention mismatch at an interface,
  because both sides are dimensionally identical.

[Limitations]

Does not replace either domain's own encoding; it composes them.
Does not cover the 3 specific pairs that have their own boxes.
Does not adjudicate which domain's constraint should win in a conflict, by
  design.
