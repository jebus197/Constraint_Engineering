DIRECTIVE: Mechanical-Electrical Interface
DOMAIN: Cross-Domain (Mechanical + Electrical/Electronic)
SCOPE: Physical integration of electronic assemblies — connectors, cabling, enclosures, EMI
STANDARDS: IEC 60529, IEC 60068-2 series, ASTM B488, IPC-TM-650,
           MIL-STD-810, IEC 61000-5-7, CISPR 32

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]
1. Connector mating force must not exceed PCB retention strength (TH/SMT/press-fit).
   Exceeding causes pad lift, via fracture, or delamination.
   Verify: connector datasheet vs IPC-TM-650 2.4.21 pad pull strength.
2. Cable bend radius: minimum per cable specification.
   Dynamic (repeated flex): >= 4x outer diameter.
   Coaxial: 6-10x outer diameter. Single flex-to-install: >= 1x.
   Violating bend radius damages conductors/shielding — intermittent field failures.
3. Vibration: PCB f_n must not coincide with excitation frequencies.
   Rectangular plate: f_n = (pi/2)*sqrt(D/(rho*h*a^4)), D = E*h^3/(12*(1-nu^2)).
   If f_n in excitation band: stiffen or damp. FEA for complex geometries.
4. Connector contact wear: gold plating thickness determines rated mating cycles.
   Per ASTM B488 Type I (hard gold, >= 99.7% Au with Co/Ni hardener):
   0.2 um ~ 50 cycles, 0.8 um ~ 500 cycles, 1.27 um ~ 1000+ cycles.
   Insufficient plating causes contact resistance increase and intermittent connections.
5. IP rating per IEC 60529: IP65 = dust-tight + jet, IP67 = dust-tight + immersion.
   Gasket compression 20-30% (solid) / 40-60% (foam). Surface Ra <= 1.6 um for seals.
6. Thermal expansion mismatch:
   delta_epsilon = (alpha_1 - alpha_2) * delta_T
   CTE of FR4: 14-18 ppm/C in-plane, 40-70 ppm/C z-axis.
   Alumina ceramic: 6-7 ppm/C. Silicon die: 2.6 ppm/C.
   Mismatch stresses solder joints — fatigue life per Coffin-Manson (see thermal directive).
7. EMI shielding: SE(dB) = 20*log10(E_i/E_t). Max aperture <= lambda/20.
   At 1 GHz: 15 mm max. At 10 GHz: 1.5 mm. Small holes leak less than one large hole.
8. Strain relief at cable entry: cable secured so no flexing at solder/crimp/IDC joint.
   Must withstand rated pull force without loading termination.
9. Creepage and clearance per IEC 60664-1: minimum distances determined by
   working voltage, pollution degree, material group. Insufficient = arc/tracking risk.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]
1. Prefer keyed/polarised connectors to prevent mis-mating.
   Colour-coding and labelling are supplementary, not primary, anti-error measures.
2. Prefer board-to-board connectors over wire harnesses where volume permits.
3. Cable routing: clearance from moving parts >= cable OD + displacement + tolerance.
4. Prefer captive fasteners for field-serviceable enclosures.
5. Prefer conformal coating (IPC-CC-830) in humid/contaminated environments.

[Verification procedures]
1. Check connector retention vs mating force and cable pull (axial + lateral).
2. Verify thermal expansion for full operating range — worst-case CTE values.
   Particular attention to large BGA/ceramic where DNP amplifies strain.
3. Confirm IP rating test covers actual installation orientation —
   water ingress is direction-dependent under IEC 60529.
4. Check vibration analysis covers installed environment profile.
   Confirm PCB f_n does not coincide with dominant excitation frequencies.
5. Verify EMI: max aperture vs lambda/20 at highest frequency.
   Check gasket-sealed joints maintain conductivity across seam.

[Diagnostic heuristics]
1. Intermittent connection that shows only when the assembly is handled, mounted or
   vibrated: load the termination mechanically before probing it electrically. A
   continuity test on an unloaded bench assembly cannot reproduce a strain-opened
   crimp or a cracked fillet, and will report the joint good.
2. Unit passes EMC on the bench and fails inside its enclosure: measure seam and
   gasket contact resistance before re-tuning the circuit. Anodising, paint and
   passivation are insulators, so a chassis bond that is mechanically perfect can be
   electrically open, and the enclosure then radiates instead of shielding.
3. EMI signature changes when a cable is moved: the leak is the shield termination,
   not the aperture. Check for a 360-degree backshell bond before recalculating
   lambda/20 — a pigtailed shield is an antenna at the frequencies that matter.
4. Contact resistance rising over life with no corrosion present: count mating cycles
   against the plating actually fitted (ASTM B488 thickness), not against the
   connector family. Wear-through to the nickel underplate is a mechanical count.
5. Arcing or tracking where the measured air gap is adequate: measure the CREEPAGE
   path along the surface, over ribs, labels, slots and any potting meniscus.
   Mechanical features change the surface path independently of the clearance.
6. Enclosure passes IP67 on test and admits water in service: check the installed
   orientation and whether internal heating cycles pump air past the seal. A static
   immersion test does not cover a breathing enclosure.
7. Solder joints cracking near a fastener, standoff or press-fit boss: check board
   flex at assembly — torque sequence, standoff flatness, boss height tolerance —
   before the reflow profile. A board bent at build has spent its strain budget
   before the first power cycle.

[Known failure modes]
1. Grounding through an anodised, painted or passivated surface: mechanically bonded,
   electrically open.
2. Using the connector's rated pull force as though it were the board's retention
   strength. The 2 figures are set by different parties and the weaker governs.
3. Specifying mating cycles from the connector family rather than from the plating
   option actually ordered.
4. Applying the static bend radius to a cable that flexes in service, or the dynamic
   figure to a harness routed once and clamped.
5. Dressing a cable so the crimp, IDC or solder joint carries the flex instead of the
   strain relief carrying it.
6. Vibration-testing a bare board rather than the installed assembly, so mounting
   stiffness, added connector mass and cable damping are all absent from the result.
7. Setting gasket compression from the gasket spec alone and ignoring fastener pitch,
   so the lid bows between screws and the seal opens mid-span while the corners pass.
8. Sizing an EMI aperture at the clock frequency rather than at the highest harmonic
   carrying significant energy.
9. Reading FR4's in-plane CTE where the stress path is through-thickness: 14-18
   against 40-70 ppm/C is a factor of 3 in the strain.

[Exception catalogue]
1. Press-fit and compliant-pin connectors legitimately exceed solder-pad retention,
   because there is no pad to lift. Retention is in the plated barrel and the limit is
   barrel and annular-ring stress, so the IPC-TM-650 2.4.21 pad-pull check named in
   HARD 1 is the wrong instrument rather than a failed one.
2. Break-away, magnetic and sacrificial connectors are SPECIFIED to release below the
   board retention strength. The constraint is inverted by design: low retention is
   the safety feature, and the board survives because the connector lets go first.
3. A PCB on elastomeric or wire-rope isolators responds to the TRANSMITTED spectrum,
   not the input. An f_n inside the input excitation band is acceptable where the
   isolator's transmissibility has rolled it off, so HARD 3 must be evaluated against
   the spectrum at the board, which is the only excitation the board can see.
4. A pressure-equalising membrane vent breaks the sealed-volume route to an immersion
   rating deliberately, and is the correct choice where thermal cycling would
   otherwise pump moisture past a gasket. The enclosure then carries the vent's own
   rating and HARD 5's compression figures govern the static seals only.
5. Conductive EMI gaskets take their compression from the contact-resistance curve,
   not from the 20-30 / 40-60 per cent sealing figures. Compressing a wire-mesh or
   oriented-wire gasket to a sealing spec crushes the conductive path and leaves a
   joint that seals and does not shield.
6. On a non-metallic enclosure the lambda/20 aperture limit has no subject at the
   wall. The shield is a conductive coating or an internal can, and the aperture rule
   applies to that boundary and its seams, not to the plastic that carries it.

[Tool limitations]
1. Modal FEA returns the frequencies of the stiffness and mass supplied to it, and the
   fasteners, gasket, connector mass and cable dressing are exactly what set the real
   boundary condition. Measured f_n commonly differs from predicted by tens of per
   cent, and in the direction of whatever was left out.
2. Shielding effectiveness measured on a coupon cannot predict an assembled
   enclosure, because the leaks are at seams, fastener pitch and cable penetrations,
   and a coupon has none of them.
3. CAD interference checking finds solid-body collisions. Creepage and clearance are
   distances in air and along surfaces rather than collisions, so a model reporting no
   interference says nothing about IEC 60664-1 compliance.
4. An IP test is pass/fail at a single orientation for a single duration and yields no
   margin. A unit that passed IP67 gives no number from which to extrapolate to
   another orientation, a longer immersion or an aged gasket.
5. A connector's rated mating cycles are measured on the manufacturer's fixture under
   controlled alignment with no side load. They do not predict the life of a
   hand-mated connector entered at an angle in a field enclosure.

[Limitations]
1. Does not cover high-voltage connectors (> 1 kV) — arc-flash requirements apply.
2. Does not cover fibre optic interfaces — different rules and cleanliness requirements.
3. Does not cover fluid/pneumatic connections to electronic assemblies.
4. Does not cover MEMS packaging — different scale and failure modes.
5. MIL/aerospace (MIL-DTL-38999, MIL-STD-810) impose additional requirements.
