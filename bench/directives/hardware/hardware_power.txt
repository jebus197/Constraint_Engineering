[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Thermal design — junction temperature must remain below absolute maximum:
  T_j = T_a + P_diss * (R_theta_jc + R_theta_cs + R_theta_sa)
  where R_theta_jc = junction-to-case (datasheet), R_theta_cs = case-to-sink (interface material),
  R_theta_sa = sink-to-ambient (heatsink specification).
  T_j,max is absolute maximum rated (e.g., 175C for SiC MOSFETs, 150C for Si MOSFETs).
  Design target: T_j < T_j,max - 25C margin at worst-case ambient.

Multiple devices on shared heatsink — thermal superposition:
  T_j,n = T_a + P_n * (R_theta_jc,n + R_theta_cs,n) + R_theta_sa * sum(P_1..P_N)
  Each device sees its own jc+cs path PLUS the shared heatsink loaded by ALL devices.
  Thermal coupling between devices adds cross-heating (model with thermal resistance matrix
  if devices are within 2x their footprint spacing on the heatsink).

MOSFET and IGBT Safe Operating Area (SOA) must not be exceeded at any operating point.
  SOA boundaries: max V_DS (or V_CE), max I_D (or I_C), max power hyperbola, secondary
  breakdown limit (bipolar/IGBT), thermal limit for pulse duration.
  Derate with temperature: R_DS(on) increases ~0.4%/C for Si MOSFETs (positive tempco).

Switching losses per transition:
  P_sw = 0.5 * V_DS * I_D * (t_on + t_off) * f_sw
  where t_on, t_off from datasheet at specified gate resistance and V_GS.
  Total switching loss scales linearly with frequency — primary efficiency limiter at f_sw > 100kHz.
  Include reverse recovery loss of body diode or freewheeling diode:
  P_rr = 0.5 * Q_rr * V_DS * f_sw

Conduction losses:
  MOSFETs: P_cond = I_rms^2 * R_DS(on) at operating T_j (use hot R_DS(on), not 25C value).
  IGBTs: P_cond = V_CE(sat) * I_C_avg + r_CE * I_C_rms^2 (linearised model from datasheet curves).
  For MOSFETs, R_DS(on) at 125C is typically 1.5-2x the 25C value.

Snubber circuits required when switching inductive loads to limit dV/dt:
  dV/dt < device dV/dt rating (typ. 5-50 V/ns depending on device).
  RC snubber: C_snub >= I_peak * dt / dV_max, R_snub ~ sqrt(L_stray / C_snub).
  Snubber dissipation: P_snub = 0.5 * C_snub * V_peak^2 * f_sw.
  Voltage overshoot without snubber: V_spike = L_stray * dI/dt (can exceed V_DS,max).

Creepage and clearance distances for mains-connected circuits:
  Per IEC 62368-1 / IEC 60664-1, at 240Vrms working voltage, pollution degree 2:
  Basic insulation: clearance >= 2.5mm, creepage >= 3.2mm (material group IIIb).
  Reinforced insulation: double the basic values.
  PCB slot widths count toward clearance only if >= 1mm wide.

Conducted EMC emissions must comply with applicable standard:
  CISPR 32 Class B (consumer/residential): 150kHz-30MHz, quasi-peak limits.
  Common-mode choke + X/Y capacitors at mains input are baseline requirements.
  Switching frequency and harmonics must fall below limit line.
  Y-capacitor value limited by earth leakage current: I_leak = V_mains * 2*pi*f * C_Y < 3.5mA.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer integrated gate drivers for low/medium power (<1kW) — reduces component count, improves timing.
Prefer SMD packages for production (reflow-compatible); TO-247/TO-220 acceptable for high-power.
Target efficiency > 90% at rated load (achievable for most topologies above 50W).
Prefer synchronous rectification over Schottky diodes above 5A output (lower conduction loss).
Minimise loop area of high dI/dt paths (gate drive loop, power switching loop) for EMI.

[Verification procedures]

Calculate total power dissipation at worst-case operating point (max load, max ambient, max V_in):
  P_total = P_cond + P_sw + P_gate + P_quiescent
Verify heatsink thermal resistance is adequate for both steady-state AND transient conditions.
  Transient thermal impedance (Z_theta_jc vs pulse duration) from datasheet for pulsed loads.
Check gate drive voltage against MOSFET V_GS(th) specification:
  V_GS_drive >= V_GS(th,max) + 2V for full enhancement (typically 10-12V for standard MOSFETs).
  V_GS_drive <= V_GS,max (absolute maximum, typically +/-20V).
Simulate inductive switching transients: verify voltage overshoot stays within V_DS,max.
  Include parasitic inductance of PCB layout (estimate 1nH per mm of trace for power loop).
Measure conducted emissions at prototype stage — pre-compliance scan before formal lab test.
Verify thermal performance with IR camera or thermocouples at worst-case operating point.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a converter is stable on a bench supply and oscillates behind the product's own input
  filter, compare the filter's output impedance with the converter's input impedance
  (Middlebrook criterion) before touching the compensation network — the loop was never the
  problem, the source impedance was.
If measured efficiency falls short of calculation and the shortfall GROWS with switching
  frequency, the missing term is switching, gate-charge or core loss. Conduction loss is
  frequency-independent at fixed I_rms, so a gap that scales with f_sw exonerates it.
If a device runs hotter than the loss budget predicts but its case is cool, measure T_c and
  back out R_theta_cs before adding heatsink: a dry, starved or pumped-out interface shows as
  a large T_j - T_c gap with entirely normal electrical waveforms.
If turn-off overshoot exceeds the calculation, question the measurement before fitting a
  snubber. A 150mm probe ground lead is about 150nH and rings with the probe capacitance, so
  use a low-inductance tip or a coaxial shunt first.
If conducted emissions fail above roughly 5-10MHz rather than at f_sw and its low harmonics,
  work the common-mode path — switch-node-to-heatsink capacitance, Y-capacitors, earth
  bonding — rather than re-sizing the differential filter, which is the low-frequency remedy.
If a half-bridge shows a current spike as the opposite switch turns on, separate dead time
  from diode reverse recovery before suspecting the driver: shoot-through scales with dead
  time, and Q_rr-driven current does not.
If a failure gets BETTER as load increases, it is not thermal. Look at discontinuous-mode
  behaviour, bootstrap or charge-pump refresh at long off-times, and minimum-load requirements.
If a thermal measurement disagrees with the model, check the instrument before the model. An
  IR camera on bare aluminium (emissivity about 0.05) reads far too cold, and a thermocouple
  bonded to the sink rather than the case is measuring the wrong node.

[Known failure modes]

Gate drive loop and power loop sharing a return path, so power-loop dI/dt appears as gate
  voltage (common source inductance) and the device partially re-triggers itself.
Dead time set from the driver's typical propagation delay, with no allowance for turn-on and
  turn-off delay mismatch across temperature and part spread.
Bootstrap capacitor sized without the duty extremes, so the high-side driver browns out near
  100% duty or at very low switching frequency.
Interface thermal resistance taken from a material's bulk conductivity, with no account of
  bond-line thickness, mounting pressure, surface finish, or pump-out over thermal cycles.
Heatsink R_theta_sa quoted at an airflow the enclosure never delivers, or a natural-convection
  figure used inside a sealed box.
A repetitive pulsed load sized from the single-pulse Z_theta_jc curve instead of the
  duty-cycle family, which understates the steady-state rise it accumulates.
SOA checked only at DC operating points, with linear-mode operation — inrush limiting,
  hot-swap, current sharing — assumed safe because the DC SOA permits the point.
Y-capacitance increased to pass an EMC scan without recomputing earth leakage current,
  trading a compliance failure for a safety failure.
Electrolytic bulk capacitor endurance not derated for ripple-induced core temperature:
  lifetime roughly halves per 10C, so a 2000h/105C part running a 125C core lasts about 500h.

[Exception catalogue]

The snubber requirement is for hard-switched inductive turn-off. A resonant or ZVS topology,
  an active clamp, or a synchronous rectifier whose channel carries the freewheel current all
  recover that energy by design, and bolting on an RC snubber dissipates exactly what the
  topology was built to return.
P_sw = 0.5 * V_DS * I_D * (t_on + t_off) * f_sw is a hard-switching estimate that assumes
  linear transitions. Under ZVS or ZCS it over-predicts by a large factor; under hard
  switching into a charged C_oss it under-predicts, because C_oss energy and Q_rr are separate
  terms the expression does not contain.
The 2.5mm clearance and 3.2mm creepage figures hold for 240Vrms working voltage, pollution
  degree 2, material group IIIb and basic insulation. A circuit that is SELV throughout takes
  none of them, and a coated assembly qualified to IEC 60664-3 or a potted assembly meeting
  the solid-insulation clauses is assessed on different tables entirely.
The 3.5mA leakage figure is for an earthed, pluggable appliance. A Class II double-insulated
  product, a permanently connected installation, and anything under IEC 60601-1 carry
  different and, for medical equipment, far lower limits — so the number must come from the
  product's own standard rather than from this line.
The 25C T_j margin is a default, not a law. Where a mission profile and a thermal-cycle budget
  exist — AEC-Q101 qualification, or a module with bond-wire and solder-fatigue data — the
  governing criterion is cycles to failure against delta T_j, and a qualified part may run
  legitimately closer to T_j,max than this margin allows.
The silicon MOSFET temperature coefficients here do not transfer. SiC and GaN have different
  R_DS(on) tempcos, and an IGBT's V_CE(sat) tempco can be negative below a crossover current
  — which is why paralleled IGBTs do not share current the way paralleled MOSFETs do.

[Tool limitations]

Vendor SPICE models commonly carry no thermal network, a simplified C_oss and no reverse
  recovery charge; and the parasitic inductance that actually sets the overshoot is a property
  of the copper, absent from the netlist. A clean simulated waveform is therefore not evidence
  about the built circuit.
A thermal simulation's boundary conditions — ambient, airflow, emissivity, mounting
  resistance — are inputs, not results. A 2x error in surface heat transfer coefficient moves
  T_j further than most device-level choices do.
A bench pre-compliance scan does not reproduce the product's earthing and cable configuration,
  and conducted results move several dB with cable dress and equipment placement. Passing a
  pre-scan detects gross failures and predicts nothing about the chamber.
Current measurement perturbs the loop it measures: a clamp probe cannot see DC, so it cannot
  detect transformer flux walk, and a shunt adds inductance to the commutation path it was
  inserted to observe.
IR thermography and thermocouples read SURFACES, with an emissivity the operator supplies. The
  limit is written against T_j, which only an on-die sensor or a calibrated V_f / V_GS(th)
  method reaches, and under pulsed load the surface-to-junction difference is the whole design
  margin.

[Limitations]

Does not cover high-voltage DC applications (>1kV — different insulation coordination, partial discharge).
Does not cover battery management systems (BMS) — cell balancing, SoC estimation, safety cutoffs.
Does not cover wireless power transfer (resonant coupling, Qi/AirFuel standards).
Does not cover grid-tied inverters (anti-islanding, grid code compliance, power quality standards).
Does not cover GaN/SiC-specific gate drive requirements (different threshold, dV/dt sensitivity).
