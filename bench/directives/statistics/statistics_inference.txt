DOMAIN: Statistics — Inference and Estimation
SCOPE: Point and interval estimation, hypothesis testing, likelihood, Bayesian
       and frequentist inference, multiplicity, model selection
VERSION: 1.0
TIER: DRAFT
SEVERITY SCALE: none. Statistics has no recognised consequence scale of its
       own: a wrong interval is epistemic until something acts on it, so the
       consequence belongs to the application. Clinical work inherits CTCAE,
       engineering work inherits FMEA.
SOURCES: American Statistical Association, "Statement on p-Values: Context,
       Process, and Purpose" (2016), 6 principles, not formally superseded --
       the later ASA President's Task Force on statistical significance and
       replicability gave broad pointers to practice rather than restating
       them. ISO 3534 for vocabulary.

WHY THIS IS THE DEFAULT SUBDOMAIN. Inference is this project's own most-used
statistical mode: every proportion it reports carries a confidence interval by
standing rule, and 2 independent routes must agree on it. The constraints below
are therefore the ones the project applies to itself.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Every reported proportion carries an interval. A bare point estimate of a
  proportion is not a measurement, because it does not say how much of the
  value is sample size.

The interval METHOD must be named. Wald, Wilson, Clopper-Pearson, Agresti-Coull
  and Jeffreys disagree materially at small n and near 0 or 1, and Wald is
  known to fail there -- it can produce a lower bound below 0. Naming the
  method is what makes the number reproducible.

A p-value is NOT the probability the hypothesis is true, NOT the probability
  the data arose by chance, and says NOTHING about effect size. Those 3 are the
  errors the 2016 ASA statement was written to address and they remain the
  commonest in the literature.

A threshold crossing is not a finding. "p < 0.05" licenses no claim on its own;
  the ASA statement is explicit that treating it as a licence "leads to
  considerable distortion of the scientific process".

Multiplicity must be declared. k tests at level alpha do not hold level alpha
  jointly, and an undeclared multiple-comparison correction is the difference
  between a result and an artefact. State the number of tests performed, not
  the number reported.

The denominator must be stated and must be the right one. A proportion whose
  denominator is a count of something else is not a weaker measurement, it
  answers a different question.

A census is not a sample: where every member of the population is examined
  there is no sampling uncertainty and attaching an interval is an error, not
  caution.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer an effect size with its interval over a test statistic with a p-value.
Prefer exact methods at small n; asymptotic approximations are convenient
  rather than correct.
Prefer pre-registration of the analysis where the design permits it, and say
  plainly when an analysis is exploratory.
Report the interval to no more precision than the data support.
Where 2 methods disagree, report both and name the disagreement rather than
  choosing the more favourable.

[Verification procedures]

Compute every interval on 2 INDEPENDENT routes and report the agreement to
  machine precision. statsmodels and scipy are NOT independent for this
  purpose where both resolve to the same verifier; an independent second route
  is a closed form in mpmath, or an exact binomial tail search.
scipy.stats for the test statistic; statsmodels for the regression
  diagnostics; NumPy for the computation; mpmath where precision matters near
  0 or 1.
Simulate the operating characteristic rather than asserting it: generate under
  the null, count rejections, and confirm the realised level matches the
  nominal one.
For a power claim, simulate. The closed form is often optimistic, and this
  project has a recorded instance where it read 35 against a simulated 0.6472.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If an interval includes an impossible value -- a negative proportion, a
  probability above 1 -- the method is Wald and the n is small.
If a p-value is extraordinarily small, check the degrees of freedom before
  believing it; a wrong df inflates significance in the convincing direction.
If removing 1 observation changes the conclusion, the conclusion is about that
  observation.
If the result is sensitive to the interval method, n is too small for any
  asymptotic claim.
A correlation near 1 between 2 derived quantities usually means they share an
  input, not that they are related.

[Known failure modes]

Wald intervals at small n or extreme p.
Reporting a point estimate as the finding and the interval as a footnote.
Treating a non-significant result as evidence of no effect.
Fishing: trying several tests and reporting the one that crossed.
Using a 1-sided test after seeing the direction of the data.
Conflating a confidence interval with a credible interval; they answer
  different questions and coincide only under specific priors.
Pooling across groups where the groups differ, which can reverse the direction
  of an association entirely.

[Exception catalogue]

A census has no sampling uncertainty, as above -- attaching an interval there
  is the denominator error in a new costume.
Bounded or restricted-range data makes a correlation attenuate toward 0 by
  construction, so a weak correlation on a narrow window is uninformative
  rather than negative. This project has a recorded instance: a kappa of
  -0.0227 over a severity window 0.09 wide was read as "no better than
  chance" when the window alone gave it power 0.1443.
Zero events in n trials gives a one-sided interval; the point estimate of 0 is
  not the finding, the upper bound is.
Heavily dependent observations make the effective sample size far below n, so
  an interval computed on n is too narrow by a factor that must be estimated
  rather than ignored.

[Tool limitations]

statsmodels and scipy often share an implementation path, so agreement between
  them can be 1 function called twice rather than 2 routes. Independence means
  a different ROUTE to the quantity, not a different import.
mpmath's incomplete beta can fail to converge at extreme parameters; where it
  does, compute the exact binomial tails instead and say so.
A simulated operating characteristic has its own Monte Carlo error, which must
  be reported alongside the estimate it qualifies.
No tool can tell a pre-registered analysis from a post-hoc one; that is a
  record-keeping property, not a computable one.

[Limitations]

Does not cover experimental design, power or randomisation; see the
  experimental-design encoding.
Does not cover time-series dependence; see the time-series encoding.
Does not cover causal identification, instrumental variables or
  difference-in-differences.
Does not cover machine-learning model evaluation beyond interval reporting.
