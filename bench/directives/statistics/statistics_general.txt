DOMAIN: Statistics — General
SCOPE: Descriptive statistics, data quality, measurement scales, visualisation,
       aggregation; the constraints that apply before any inferential or design
       question arises
VERSION: 1.0
TIER: DRAFT
SEVERITY SCALE: none of its own; inherits the application's.
SOURCES: ISO 3534 for vocabulary. Stevens' levels of measurement for the
       admissibility constraints below.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

THE MEASUREMENT SCALE DETERMINES WHICH STATISTICS ARE ADMISSIBLE, and this is
  the constraint this project learned the hard way. On a NOMINAL scale only
  counts and modes are meaningful. On an ORDINAL scale, order statistics --
  selected values such as a minimum, a maximum, or a median at odd n -- are
  admissible; SUMS and MEANS are NOT, because they assume the gaps between
  levels carry magnitude.
  ★ THE INTERPOLATED MEDIAN AT EVEN n IS NOT ADMISSIBLE ON AN ORDINAL SCALE,
  because averaging the 2 middle values is an arithmetic operation on labels.
  Selecting one of them is admissible; averaging them is not.

A CUT NAMED BY A FIXED VALUE IS NOT INVARIANT ON AN ORDINAL SCALE. A strictly
  increasing re-scaling preserves every pairwise order and can still change how
  many items sit above a fixed number. A cut must name a DISTINGUISHED LEVEL of
  the scale and travel with it. Measured on this project's own archive: an
  order-preserving affine re-scaling destroyed 1,416 of 1,416 critical calls --
  1.000000, Wilson [0.997294, 1.000000] -- with a rank correlation of exactly 1.

THE SCALE NAME TRAVELS WITH THE NUMBER. Two ordinal scales are both
  dimensionless, so dimensional analysis cannot refuse their sum: measured with
  pint, a clinical grade 3 plus a software score 7.0 evaluates to 10.0. The
  refusal has to be on the scale name, which means the name must be carried.

A DECIMAL LITERAL IS NOT THE REAL NUMBER IT SPELLS. The binary64 nearest to
  0.7 is 0.6999999999999999555910790149937383830547332763671875, strictly
  BELOW the real 7/10. A comparison at a boundary is therefore decided by the
  representation. Measured on this project's archive: 174 of 1,416 critical
  calls -- 0.122881, Wilson [0.106795, 0.141009] -- turn on a tie at exactly
  that value. Comparing a decimal string against a float will report a spurious
  mismatch at the 1e-17 level for the same reason.

Aggregation must preserve the denominator. A proportion of proportions is not
  a proportion, and a mean of means is not a mean unless the groups are equal
  in size.

Missing data must be reported, not dropped silently. The count of complete
  cases and the count of records are different denominators and answer
  different questions.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer the median and an interquartile range for skewed data, and say which.
Prefer reporting the distribution over reporting a summary where n permits.
State units and scale for every quantity; a number without both is not a
  measurement.
Prefer a count with its denominator over a bare percentage.
Prefer showing the data to summarising it where the n is small enough.

[Verification procedures]

NumPy and pandas for the computation; mpmath where a boundary comparison or an
  extreme value is involved, and exact rationals where a tie at a threshold
  decides an outcome.
Recompute every aggregate on 2 routes with different grouping order and
  confirm agreement; set iteration order has made a measurement
  non-deterministic in this project's own record.
Check the denominator by execution: print it beside the numerator rather than
  asserting it.
For any threshold comparison, report how many observations lie exactly ON the
  threshold. That count is the sensitivity of the result to the representation.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If a percentage and its count disagree, the denominator moved between them.
If an aggregate changes when the grouping order changes, a set is being
  iterated and the result is not deterministic.
If a mean and a median differ greatly, the mean is reporting the tail.
If a figure is suspiciously round, check whether it was typed rather than
  computed.
If a proportion sits at exactly 0 or 1, the interval is one-sided and the
  point estimate is not the finding.

[Known failure modes]

Averaging an ordinal scale, including the interpolated even-n median.
Comparing values from 2 different scales, which no dimensional check refuses.
A count ratio reported as coverage, which hides an equal number of misses and
  spurious matches. This project has a recorded instance reporting 318 of 318
  at 100 per cent while missing 18 items and duplicating 18 others, which is
  why a SET must be pinned rather than a count.
Quoting a measured figure with no script that produces it, which makes it a
  claim about evidence rather than evidence.
Comparing a decimal string against a binary float and reporting the 1e-17
  difference as a disagreement.
Reporting a trend in a figure rather than the figure, because a trend claim is
  a claim about the next measurement and decays faster than the level.

[Exception catalogue]

A census has no sampling uncertainty and needs no interval.
An ordinal scale with equally spaced, externally calibrated levels may support
  interval operations, but the calibration is the evidence and must be cited.
Ratio-scale data permits all the usual operations; the restriction is a
  property of the scale, not of statistics.
Where a scale is INVERTED -- GHS hazard categories run from 1 as the most
  severe -- a higher number is less severe, so every ordering argument must
  check the direction rather than assume it.

[Tool limitations]

pandas will happily take the mean of an ordinal column, and NumPy will take
  the mean of anything numeric. Neither knows the scale, so admissibility is
  never enforced by the tool.
pint cannot refuse a sum of 2 dimensionless quantities, as above.
Floating-point comparison at a boundary is representation-dependent, and no
  library makes that go away; exact rationals or a stated tolerance are the
  only answers.
Set and dict iteration order can vary between runs for some constructions, and
  an aggregate that depends on it is not reproducible.

[Limitations]

Does not cover design or power; see the experimental-design encoding.
