WHY THIS IS A SEPARATE SUBDOMAIN. Power, pre-registration and multiplicity are
a different discipline from estimation, and they are directly load-bearing for
this project's own experiments: the decision rule for a comparison must be
written before the comparison is run, or the comparison cannot fail.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

A power claim requires a stated effect size, a stated alpha, a stated test and
  a stated n. A "powered study" without all 4 is not a claim.

POWER MUST BE SIMULATED, not only derived. The closed form is frequently
  optimistic; this project has a recorded instance where the closed form said
  35 and the simulation of the same design returned 0.6472 against a target of
  0.80, so the closed-form figure must not be quoted.

The decision rule must be fixed BEFORE the data are seen. A threshold chosen
  after looking at the result is not a test, and no amount of arithmetic
  recovers it.

Randomisation must be concealed as well as performed. Known allocation
  defeats randomisation even where the sequence itself was random.

A stopping rule changes the operating characteristic. Repeatedly testing as
  data accumulate inflates the type-1 error far above nominal, and the
  inflation must be accounted for by design rather than corrected afterwards.

The analysis population must be defined before exclusions. Excluding after the
  fact, for any reason correlated with outcome, breaks the comparison.

Blocking and stratification factors must be declared and must be used in the
  analysis. Blocking in the design and ignoring it in the analysis discards the
  precision the blocking bought.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer a factorial design where several factors are of interest; it buys main
  effects at the same n and reveals interactions a sequence of 1-factor
  studies cannot see.
Prefer a within-subject design where carryover can be bounded; it removes
  between-subject variance.
State the minimum effect of interest rather than the effect you expect.
Prefer registering the analysis plan even where no external registry applies;
  an internal dated record serves the same purpose.

[Verification procedures]

Simulate the design end to end: generate under the null, apply the planned
  analysis, count rejections, and confirm the realised level matches nominal.
  Then generate under the alternative and read the power off the same harness.
scipy.stats and NumPy for the simulation; statsmodels for the planned model.
Check the randomisation by execution: generate many allocations and confirm
  the balance distribution matches what the scheme claims.
For any sequential design, simulate the stopping rule rather than applying a
  textbook correction, because the correction depends on the exact looks.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

If the required n is suspiciously small, the assumed effect is suspiciously
  large; restate it as a proportion of the outcome's standard deviation and ask
  whether it is plausible.
If power is above 0.99 at a modest n, check whether the test is 1-sided and
  whether that was justified before the data.
If a design's power is flat in n, the variance assumption is wrong.
A balanced design that came out unbalanced indicates either the randomisation
  or the exclusions; check the exclusions first.

[Known failure modes]

Quoting a closed-form power without simulating it.
Choosing alpha after seeing the p-value.
Post-hoc power: computing power from the observed effect, which is a
  restatement of the p-value and carries no information about the design.
Treating a pilot study's effect estimate as the design effect, which is biased
  upward by selection.
Counting a power figure as a workload. This project has a recorded instance
  where 63 was a power figure and was reported to the founder as 63 units of
  his own adjudication time.
Ignoring clustering, which inflates the effective alpha when the unit of
  randomisation is not the unit of analysis.

[Exception catalogue]

Equivalence and non-inferiority designs invert the hypotheses, so a
  non-significant result is the positive finding and the usual reading is
  backwards.
Adaptive designs legitimately change n or allocation mid-study, but only under
  a pre-specified rule; the rule, not the adaptation, is what preserves the
  level.
A census needs no power calculation, because there is no sampling.
Where the outcome is rare, the binomial approximation to normality fails and
  the sample-size formula based on it understates n substantially.

[Tool limitations]

A power simulation is itself an estimate with Monte Carlo error, which must be
  reported; 1000 replications give roughly 1.5 percentage points of standard
  error near 0.5.
Standard sample-size functions assume independence and will silently give an
  answer for clustered data.
No tool can verify that a decision rule was fixed in advance; only a dated
  record can, which is why the record matters more than the arithmetic.
