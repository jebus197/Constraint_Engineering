SEVERITY SCALE: none of its own; inherits the application's.
SOURCES: ASA statement on p-values (2016) for the inferential constraints that
       carry over.

WHY THIS IS A SEPARATE SUBDOMAIN. Autocorrelation and non-stationarity
invalidate the independence assumption every constraint in the inference box
rests on. A result computed with inference-box methods on dependent data is not
less precise, it is wrong in a direction that flatters it: intervals come out
too narrow and significance too easy.

[HARD constraints — non-negotiable]

Dependence must be tested, not assumed absent. An interval computed on n
  dependent observations is too narrow by a factor depending on the
  autocorrelation, and the direction is always toward false confidence.

Stationarity must be established before any method that assumes it. A unit
  root makes ordinary regression between 2 series produce significant
  relationships between independent random walks -- spurious regression -- at
  rates far above nominal.

Differencing changes the quantity being modelled. A model of the differences
  is not a model of the levels, and forecasts must be integrated back with
  their uncertainty propagated, not merely their point values.

The effective sample size, not n, governs precision. Reporting an interval on
  n for autocorrelated data is the same class of error as reporting a
  proportion on the wrong denominator.

A forecast interval must widen with horizon. One that does not has a variance
  term missing, and the missing term is usually the accumulated innovation.

Look-ahead bias is disqualifying. Any feature computed using information not
  available at the forecast origin invalidates the evaluation entirely, and
  the resulting performance is not an overestimate, it is meaningless.

A change-point found by searching is not a change-point tested at that date.
  The search must be accounted for, exactly as multiplicity is in the
  inference box. This project has a recorded instance: a change-point analysis
  that cut at the first failure found an isolated blip, and cutting at a
  hand-picked date launders it as a derived one.

[SOFT constraints — negotiable]

Prefer an explicit model of the dependence over a correction to an
  independence-based statistic.
Prefer out-of-sample evaluation with a rolling origin over in-sample fit.
Prefer reporting the autocorrelation function over a single lag-1 coefficient.
State the sampling frequency and whether the series is aggregated; aggregation
  changes the dependence structure.

[Verification procedures]

statsmodels for the unit-root tests, the autocorrelation function and the
  model fitting; scipy and NumPy for the computation.
Test for a unit root BEFORE regressing 2 series on one another, and report the
  test rather than the conclusion.
Simulate the spurious-regression rate: generate 2 independent random walks,
  regress them, and count significant results. The realised rate is the
  evidence that the test was needed.
Rolling-origin backtest rather than a single split, with the number of origins
  stated.
Residual diagnostics by execution: if the residuals are still autocorrelated,
  the model has not captured the dependence whatever the fit statistic says.

[Diagnostic heuristics]

An R-squared above 0.9 between 2 economic or physical level series is usually
  a shared trend, not a relationship. Difference both and look again.
A significant coefficient that becomes insignificant after differencing was a
  trend artefact.
Residual autocorrelation at exactly the seasonal lag means the seasonality is
  unmodelled, not that the model is poor.
A forecast that reverts to the mean immediately has an overdifferenced or
  misspecified model.
If a change-point moves when 1 observation is added, it was not a change-point.

[Known failure modes]

Regressing levels on levels without a unit-root test.
Reporting an interval on n rather than the effective sample size.
Look-ahead bias through a feature computed on the full sample, which is easy
  to introduce by normalising before splitting.
Overfitting a model order on in-sample fit and reporting it as predictive.
Treating a structural break as noise, or noise as a break.
Using a single train/test split and reporting the result as the performance.

[Exception catalogue]

A deterministic trend and a stochastic trend both produce non-stationarity but
  need different treatments, and a unit-root test alone does not distinguish
  them reliably at short lengths.
Cointegrated series may legitimately be regressed in levels, and differencing
  them discards the long-run relationship that was the point.
Seasonal unit roots exist and ordinary differencing does not remove them.
Very short series cannot support any dependence estimate; below roughly 30
  observations the autocorrelation estimates are too noisy to act on, and
  saying so is the honest result.

[Tool limitations]

Unit-root tests have low power against near-unit-root alternatives, so failing
  to reject is weak evidence of stationarity rather than evidence of it.
Automatic model-order selection optimises an in-sample criterion and will
  happily select an order that forecasts worse.
A rolling backtest with overlapping windows produces dependent performance
  estimates, so the interval on the performance itself is too narrow.
No tool detects look-ahead bias; it is a property of how the features were
  built and must be reasoned about.

[Limitations]

Does not cover estimation on independent data; see the inference encoding.
Does not cover design and power; see the experimental-design encoding.
Does not cover spatial or network dependence.
Does not cover high-frequency or irregularly-spaced series in detail.
