### 1. ENRICHMENT STRATEGY

To move from "thin standards" to "domain expertise", the encodings require tacit heuristics and failure mode catalogues. 

**a. Missing Categories of Expert Knowledge**
- **Diagnostic Heuristics:** Rules of thumb used to quickly bound a problem before running full calculations.
- **Failure Mode Biases:** Knowledge of which 20% of the components cause 80% of the field failures.
- **Tool Chain Reality:** Acknowledgment that theoretical standards often conflict with the quirks of industry-standard software (e.g., Ansys, Moldflow, SAP2000).
- **Standard Loopholes & "Gotchas":** Knowing where the code is silent, contradictory, or where "industry practice" diverges from literal interpretation.

**b. Concrete Expert Additions (Examples)**
- *Biomedical Device:* The thin directive quotes ISO 10993. An expert encoding adds: *"Extractables/leachables failures rarely originate from the bulk polymer; they originate from undocumented mould-release agents, colourant masterbatch carriers, or adhesive off-gassing. Always mandate FTIR/GC-MS on the exact manufacturing process, not just raw material coupons."*
- *Industrial CNC:* The thin directive quotes cutting speeds. An expert encoding adds: *"Internal corner radii govern tool deflection. Program feed rate reductions to 50% when entering internal corners where tool engagement angle suddenly increases from 90° to 180°, otherwise chatter will gouge the part and break the tool."*
- *Software Real-time:* The thin directive quotes Rate Monotonic Scheduling. An expert encoding adds: *"RMS assumes zero context switch overhead. In practice, task context switching and cache-thrashing at high frequencies destroy schedulability. Any task period < 1ms on a standard MCU requires interrupt-driven bare-metal handling, not an RTOS task."*

**c. Tool-Verifiable vs. Human Judgment**
- *Tool-Verifiable (S_k eligible):* Software syntax/types, mathematics constraint solving (SymPy/Z3), dimensional analysis, explicit boundary checks, and API adherence.
- *Human Judgment (HIL required):* Hazard identification, physical world tool interactions (e.g., welding metallurgy realism), clinical safety relevance, and aesthetic/usability constraints.

### 4. ENRICHMENT PROCESS DESIGN

**a. Drafting Strategy: Generative-Adversarial DAG**
Do not use iterative collaboration (which trends toward polite consensus and mean-reversion). Use isolated adversarial drafting. Model A writes the directive. Model B is explicitly prompted: *"Assume Model A's directive will cause a critical failure in production. Find the exact mechanism of that failure."* Model C resolves the conflict.

**b. Detecting False Expertise**
The primary signal of hallucinated synthetic expertise is the absence of trade-off friction. Real engineering is constrained by time, cost, and physical limits. If a model proposes a verification procedure that requires infinite precision, perfectly rigid bodies, or economically unviable testing (e.g., "perform 100% X-ray CT scanning on all injection moulded consumer parts"), it is hallucinating textbook ideality.

**c. The S_k Framework as an Ouroboros**
Yes, S_k can verify encodings. 
1. Inject a known historical failure (e.g., a specific Python concurrency bug) into a codebase.
2. Provide the tool with the new `software_python_sk` directive.
3. If the directive is expertly written, the resulting fix will pass gates `g1-g3` and `e1-e2`, yielding a high S_k. 
4. If the directive is poor, the LLM will generate a fix that fails the gates. The S_k score of the *solutions produced under the directive* becomes the quantitative benchmark for the *quality of the directive itself*.
