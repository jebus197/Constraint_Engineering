`SEVERITY: 0.96`  
`DESCRIPTION:` The merge path is wired backwards. The prompt defines `MERGE C0001 <- [your_finding_id]` as “merge my duplicate into canonical finding `C0001`” in [run_exp35_policy_engine.py](/Users/georgejackson/Developer_Projects/Constraint_Engineering/bench/run_exp35_policy_engine.py#L591). The parser keeps `C0001` as the target in [run_exp35_policy_engine.py](/Users/georgejackson/Developer_Projects/Constraint_Engineering/bench/run_exp35_policy_engine.py#L365), the main loop attaches the `MERGE` verdict to that target in [run_exp35_policy_engine.py](/Users/georgejackson/Developer_Projects/Constraint_Engineering/bench/run_exp35_policy_engine.py#L1253), and `_update_finding_statuses()` then marks the target itself as `MERGED` in [run_exp35_policy_engine.py](/Users/georgejackson/Developer_Projects/Constraint_Engineering/bench/run_exp35_policy_engine.py#L451). The duplicate named on the right side of `<-` is never looked up or resolved.  
`FOLLOW:` This corrupts the blackboard’s official state: the canonical finding disappears, the duplicate stays open, support counts drift, and convergence can be decided on the wrong registry state.  
`PROPOSED_FIX:` Parse the right-hand finding ID as the finding being subsumed, look up its canonical alias, mark that duplicate as `MERGED`, and store `merged_into=C0001`. Do not mark `C0001` as merged unless it is itself being subsumed by another canonical entry.
