- omit `ANALYSE`, `FALSIFICATION`, `CORROBORATION`, or

# 2. Will the operational directive work?

## Short answer
**Partially, but not in the strong sense intended.**

Models will probably:
- understand the prose,
- attempt some self-critique,
- sometimes produce falsification sections,
- sometimes estimate qualitative risk factors.

They will **not reliably perform the full self-assessment discipline as an operational control loop** unless the runner and parser force it much harder.

## What likely works

### A. The directive is understandable to frontier models
The equation is conceptually legible:
- `q = η·d·p`
- Bayesian detection update
- fix efficacy `σ`
- re-injection `ν`
- break-even `ν*`
- stop if divergent

This is understandable prose/maths for strong models. They can reason about it qualitatively.

### B. Qualitative self-assessment is more likely than numeric compliance
The directive itself softens the requirement:
> “You do not need to report exact numbers. What matters is the qualitative assessment…”

That helps. Models can usually do:
- novelty low/high,
- diversity low/high,
- fix confidence high/medium/low,
- re-injection low/high.

So you’ll probably get **narrative self-assessment**.

## What likely fails

### C. Models are unlikely to compute the equation consistently on their own output
Failure mode:
- they mention `η`, `d`, `p`, `σ`, `ν`,
- maybe even quote `R_k`,
- but they won’t perform stable, comparable calculations across rounds/findings.

Reasons:
1. No explicit numeric output schema.
2. No parser-visible fields for the parameters.
3. No calibration examples.
4. No indication that outputs will be rejected if `CORROBORATION` is vague.
5. Models are being asked to do code review, registry comparison, verdicting, falsification, and self-risk accounting all at once.

So the directive will mostly act as **behavioral priming**, not a reliable measurement instrument.

### D. The novelty term `η` is under-supported in the prompt flow
The directive requires registry-relative novelty assessment. In star rounds `>0`, models do get registry summaries. Good.

But:
- Round 0 has no registry.
- Registry summaries may not expose enough root-cause structure for accurate novelty estimation.
- Models may confuse “different evidence” with “novel finding”.

So `η` will be noisy.

### E. The diversity term `d` is weakly grounded
Models are told to consider whether their approach differs from previous checks, but in star topology they generally do **not** see others’ actual reasoning traces, only registry state. That means they cannot robustly estimate method independence.

So `d` will often be hand-wavy.

### F. `ν*` as a HARD EXIT is unlikely to be obeyed consistently
This is the most ambitious part of the directive and the least likely to operationalise well.

Why:
- models are not doing actual edits in the codebase; they are proposing fixes;
- re-injection estimation is speculative;
- they are rewarded to produce fixes unless strongly constrained otherwise;
- the base prompt still says “Produce ALL findings you can identify” and asks for `PROPOSED_FIX`.

Net effect: many models will still propose fixes even when, under the directive, they should say “finding only, no safe fix”.

# 3. Are there bugs? Crashes, logic errors, mismatches

## Yes. Surviving issues:

### 3.1 Likely parser/schema mismatch bug
**Most important implementation risk.**

The prompt schema shown in `base_prompt` does **not include** the mandatory new sections required by the operational directive. Yet the experiment description says:
> “A finding without a FALSIFICATION section will be rejected by the parser.”

If `parse_findings(...)` actually enforces that gate, then many model outputs will be rejected because the **primary requested format doesn’t contain that field**.

That creates a runner-induced false negative:
- not “model failed to falsify”
- but “runner asked for incompatible structure”

This is the strongest surviving bug/mismatch.

### 3.2 Comment/documentation mismatch: C(n) vs new R-based equation
In `_dispatch_single_model`:
```python
# Append operational protocol — models compute C(n), R_n, γ on own output
```
For Exp 37 this is stale. The operational directive explicitly says simplified `C(n)` was replaced. `γ` is panel telemetry, not something models compute “on own output” in the same sense.

Not a crash bug, but evidence of implementation drift.

### 3.3 Experiment identity mismatch in logs/messages
The main loop docstring/logs still say:
- `"""Run Experiment 36: ..."""`
- `_log("EXPERIMENT 36: Evidence Layer Code Review")`

This is not fatal, but it is a real operational bug:
- wrong experiment labeling,
- potential resume/report confusion,
- analysis contamination if downstream tooling keys off experiment identity text.

### 3.4 Possible multiturn chunking label/content corruption risk
`_multiturn_fallback` splits `full_code` using:
```python
re.split(r'\n\n===\s+(?:TARGET|CONTEXT)\s+', full_code)
```
But the artifact headers in `full_code` are:
- `=== TARGET FILE (REVIEW THIS): ... ===`
- `=== CONTEXT FILE: ... ===`

Because the regex cuts at `=== TARGET` / `=== CONTEXT`, the resulting chunks may begin mid-header, dropping semantic framing like “FILE”, “REVIEW THIS”, path, or warning text. That may reduce decomposition quality and can blur target/context distinction.

I would not call it a guaranteed crash, but it is a real logic-quality bug.

### 3.5 Metrics injection starts only at round >= 2
Operational text says models will receive per-round metrics each round. Runner injects metrics only when:
```python
if round_idx >= 2 and registry is not None:
```
So rounds 1 lacks metrics even though there is already registry state from round 0.

This is a mismatch, not catastrophic, but it weakens the “operational directive in first production deployment” claim.

### 3.6 Round numbering semantics may confuse models
The star message says:
- Round 0: blind
- Round 1+: registry available
- metrics only from Round 2

But the operational text treats rounds/cycles as continuous self-calibration. Models may interpret missing metrics in Round 1 as omission or inconsistency.

### 3.7 Registry status count mismatch possibility
Metrics count:
- `OPEN`, `CONTESTED`
- `CONFIRMED`
- `CLOSED`, `MERGED`

Docstring earlier mentions:
- `OPEN -> CONFIRMED / MERGED / CONTESTED / UNCONFIRMED`

If `UNCONFIRMED` exists but is omitted from metrics/reporting summaries, panel state shown to models is incomplete. I can’t prove from the excerpt that this status is actively used, so this survives only as a likely mismatch, not a confirmed bug.

# 4. What will go wrong? Most likely failure modes

## 4.1 Output-format conflict will dominate behavior
Most likely outcome:
- models follow the explicit key list in `base_prompt`,
- sprinkle some extra prose from the operational directive,
- parser either partially parses or rejects sections,
- you get low-quality comparability across models.

This is the highest-probability failure mode.

## 4.2 “Falsification” will collapse into self-assertion
Even strong models often respond with:
- “I checked X”
- “No counterexample found”
- “Fix seems safe”

without a concrete adversarial test. Unless the parser demands structured falsifier/attempt/result content, much of the claimed falsification will be ceremonial.

## 4.3 CORROBORATION/R estimates will be non-comparable
You will likely see:
- some models use qualitative labels,
- some give rough numbers,
- some ignore the equation,
- some report `R_k` without the intermediate variables,
- some confuse flaw-class residual risk with finding confidence.

So any cross-model quantitative analysis of the new equation will be weak.

## 4.4 Registry novelty calibration will be noisy
Especially in later rounds, models may:
- avoid valid rediscoveries that would have provided confirmation,
- incorrectly label extensions as novel,
- over-suppress output under low `ρ̄₃/high γ`,
- or game toward “no novel findings” too early.

## 4.5 The 4-layer stack may overload some models
You are combining:
- composed CDSFL directives,
- operational directive,
- topology instructions,
- prior fix summary,
- target/context artifact,
- registry summary,
- metrics,
- structured output schema.

This is a lot. Likely effects:
- instruction dilution,
- partial compliance,
- model-specific collapse to shortest familiar format,
- more multiturn fallback use.

## 4.6 Multiturn decomposition may weaken target/context discipline
Because chunking appears header-fragile, some models may lose the strong distinction:
- review `evidence.py`,
- do not review `verification_chain.py`.

This may produce spurious interface findings or context-file criticism.

## 4.7 Experiment interpretation will be confounded
If outcomes are poor, you won’t know whether the cause was:
- the new equation,
- the 4-layer schema,
- parser strictness,
- registry/metrics pressure,
- or model-specific routing fixes.

That is a design-level failure mode.
